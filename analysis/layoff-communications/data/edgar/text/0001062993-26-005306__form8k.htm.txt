(i) History The Dubbo Project was first initiated by Alkane, which secured an exploration license over the Toongi deposit in 1987. Subsequent drilling and metallurgical test work between 1999 and 2002 confirmed the deposit's uniform grade and demonstrated that a sulfuric acid roast and leach process could effectively treat the ore - an important breakthrough given the silica-related challenges encountered in comparable deposits. Although the project was temporarily deferred in 2003, Alkane continued advancing the technical foundations of the project, including construction of a pilot-scale demonstration plant at ANSTO in 2008. This work enabled the development of a flowsheet capable of producing discrete products across light and heavy REEs, Zr, Hf and Nb. Building on these results, a series of technical and economic studies were undertaken over the following decade. A Definitive Feasibility Study (the " DFS ") was completed in 2013 by TZ Minerals International, building on earlier studies by TZMI (2011) and SNC Lavalin (2002). Further engineering input was provided by Hatch in 2015 and GHD Ltd. in 2017, culminating in the Maiden Ore Reserves Report released in 2017. These studies collectively confirmed the project's technical viability and established the basis for future development. To accelerate development, ASM was subsequently demerged from Alkane and assumed responsibility for progressing the Dubbo Project. ASM completed an Optimisation Feasibility Study (the " OFS" ) in 2021, resulting in an updated Ore Reserves Estimate later that year. The OFS incorporated significant enhancements to the flowsheet following extensive collaboration with ANSTO, improving overall recoveries and process efficiency. It also assessed a processing circuit designed to produce a comprehensive suite of high-value oxides across the REEs (both heavy and light) as well as Zr, Nb and Hf and incorporated infrastructure upgrades to strengthen ESG performance, including a chlor-alkali plant, brine concentrator and rail refurbishment. (ii) Project studies In 2024, ASM launched the Rare Earth Options Assessment (the " REOA ") to evaluate alternative pathways for developing the Dubbo Project. The goal of the REOA is to identify lower-capital, shorter implementation options for recovering both light rare earths (Nd, Pr) and heavy rare earths (Dy, Tb) as high-purity, separated oxides. 17 A key opportunity emerging from this work is the potential to deliver the Dubbo Project in phases - beginning with a first phase focused on REE recovery and a second phase to target the broader suite of critical minerals in the Dubbo Project deposit. The REOA identified several technically viable options, particularly heap and atmospheric tank leaching. These methods have the potential to remove the need for a capital- and energy-intensive acid bake in the initial development phase, significantly simplifying the process flowsheet. To assess these options, ASM conducted extensive metallurgical test work, including scoping variability tank leach and bottle roll tests across different zones of the Dubbo Project deposit. In July 2025, ASM successfully completed and released the Heap Leach Scoping Study as part of the REOA work program. The Heap Leach Scoping Study provides a high-level economic evaluation (+/- 50%) of a heap leach, REE-focused first phase implementation of the Dubbo Project and represents a transformative shift in the Dubbo Project's development approach. The heap leach approach eliminates complex initial processing stages, simplifying construction and accelerating the pathway to cash flow generation. This approach offers a more agile and targeted entry point, aligning with current market priorities while potentially reducing initial capital requirements. Since the release of the Heap Leach Scoping Study, ASM made substantial progress across multiple technical workstreams in support of delivering a Pre-Feasibility Study (the " PFS ") specific to the heap leach process. Completion of the PFS is being accelerated by leveraging ASM's extensive prior technical and engineering work. Korean Metals Plant and potential American Metals Plant The KMP, located in South Korea, is the Company’s current REE metals and alloys metallization facility. Since opening in 2022 under ASM, the KMP has established itself as a strategically important, independent source of western/western-allied based REE metallization and alloying capacity. The buildings and equipment at KMP are owned by ASM, while the underlying land is leased. Certain equipment at KMP is subject to retention requirements and restrictions on disposal. The plant has twelve total installed furnaces (including the eight installed in 1H 2026) and one strip caster with an installed alloy nameplate production capacity of approximately 1,300 tpa neodymium-iron-boron (“ NdFeB ”) alloy. The facility is designed for a full nameplate capacity of 3,600 tpa of NdFeB alloy, which is expected to be enabled through a Phase 2 ramp‑up involving installation of additional furnaces (eight of which were installed in H1 2026) and a second strip caster, which was purchased and is currently being installed at KMP. Demand continues to grow for KMP products amid heightened geopolitical uncertainty and global efforts to diversify critical material supply chains, particularly from global magnet manufacturers and original equipment manufacturers (the " OEMs "), reflecting the growing importance of secure, western/western-allied supply alternatives. This has translated into ongoing and new customer discussions globally for the Company. In parallel with this rising demand, operational performance at the KMP has strengthened, with steadily increasing alloy output and consistent sales growth demonstrating the facility's maturing capability and market traction. This rising demand is expected to provide an outlet for any metal and/or alloy making capacity not required for the Company’s internal needs once the VAC Acquisition closes as contemplated. 18 To support growing customer demand, ASM initiated a phased expansion of the KMP. Expected Synergies Resulting from the Acquisition of VAC Following completion of the VAC Acquisition, the Combined Company is intended to be vertically integrated across the full REE value chain - from mining projects and separation (Energy Fuels' White Mesa Mill and Donald Project) through metals and alloys production (ASM's KMP and planned AMP) to finished permanent magnet manufacturing (VAC's European facilities and Sumter magnet plant). This vertical integration is intended to enable the Combined Company to: capture value at each conversion step in the supply chain – from ore to separated oxide, oxide to metal/alloy, and metal/alloy to finished magnet – rather than participating only in one or a subset of the manufacturing steps; internalize the upstream margin on REE feedstock currently paid to third-party suppliers by sourcing separated REE oxides and metals from within the Combined Company's own operations rather than purchasing them at market prices; reduce exposure to third-party supply chain disruptions and pricing volatility, particularly given recent foreign export restrictions on certain REE alloy forms that have required VAC to restructure its procurement arrangements (including sourcing REE oxides from European and U.S.-based suppliers and engaging intermediary processors for alloy refinement); and strengthen pricing power with end customers by creating a fully traceable, western/western-allied-origin, mine-to-magnet supply chain applicable to the aerospace, defense and automotive sectors, as suggested by western pricing. Changes in the fair value of these instruments have resulted in and may continue to result in material finance income or finance expenses.
