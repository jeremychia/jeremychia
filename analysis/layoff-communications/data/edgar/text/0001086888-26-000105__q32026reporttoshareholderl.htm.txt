Manulife Financial Corporation – Third Quarter 2026 3 Strategic Highlights We are expanding customer access to wealth solutions through product enhancements and expansion In Canada, we expanded our segregated fund lineup by adding eight new funds and broadened access to eleven existing funds. The enhanced suite increases investment choice and flexibility for customers and advisors, helping Canadians diversify their portfolios, build long-term wealth, and pursue their evolving financial goals . In Global WAM, we raised US$5.4 billion of capital through the close of Comvest Credit Partners VII. This marked Manulife | Comvest’s largest fundraise in the history of Comvest’s private credit platform 1 , attracting commitments from both existing and new institutional investors and expanding the platform’s global investor relationships . In the U.S., we broadened our ability to meet a wider range of customer protection and long-term planning needs through enhancements to our indexed universal life and survivorship indexed universal life solutions, and the introduction of three differentiated term life insurance solutions. Together, these enhancements and new offerings expand our protection portfolio and provide financial professionals with greater choice and flexibility to address evolving customer needs . We are scaling and advancing solutions as an AI-powered organization while further enhancing AI governance and responsible use We expanded our strategic partnership with Microsoft through a new five-year agreement. The agreement includes extending Microsoft 365 Copilot to more than 30,000 employees to further enable productivity and innovation, as well as deploying Microsoft Agent 365 to strengthen enterprise AI governance and drive AI solutions at enterprise scale. In Asia, we continued to extend proven AI-enabled advisory capabilities into new markets, bringing these capabilities to the Chinese Mainland and Malaysia to help agents analyze customer and family coverage, identify protection gaps across life, health, and investment goals, recognize life-event triggers, and prepare for customer meetings. In Malaysia, the tool also generates personalized talking points, including needs-based analysis questions, and recommends next steps linked to customers' financial planning goals . In Global WAM, we completed the rollout of an AI-enabled advisor engagement platform to all external wholesalers and investment specialists across North America Retail, identifying four times more product opportunities and capturing richer advisor insights. The platform also improved productivity through automated documentation, reducing meeting administration and follow-up activities, saving approximately 30 minutes per session . In the U.S., we scaled our Quick Quote underwriting support tool to automate more than 70% of preliminary assessments, up from nearly 50% in the first quarter of 2026, rapidly expanding our proven AI-enabled underwriting capability to reduce average turnaround time and optimize our utilization of underwriting expertise . We are advancing our health and longevity leadership through innovative strategic partnerships, expanded wellness offerings and simpler access to healthcare In Asia, we signed a Memorandum of Understanding (“MOU”) with Ant Health 2 , setting out a collaboration framework covering health management services, cross-boundary healthcare support, digital health capabilities and insurance technology. In October, we launched the first initiative under the MOU, offering cashless direct billing through Alipay for eligible customers at participating healthcare providers in the Chinese Mainland, making healthcare access easier . In Canada, we began offering AI-enabled virtual physiotherapy in partnership with Sword ® , a global AI health company, to eligible Group Benefits members on disability with musculoskeletal conditions. This initiative helps address barriers to care through convenient, personalized support from licensed Canadian clinicians while supporting faster recovery and return-to- work outcomes . In the U.S., we strengthened our leadership in longevity and differentiated behavioural insurance by expanding the health and wellness offerings available through John Hancock Vitality, including Prenuvo’s advanced heart health scan and ŌURA’s latest smart ring, providing customers access to additional tools and insights to help them live longer, healthier, better lives . Manulife Financial Corporation – Third Quarter 2026 5 Annual review of actuarial methods and assumptions We completed our annual review of actuarial methods and assumptions, which resulted in a net favourable impact of a $128 million 1 decrease in pre-tax fulfilment cash flows. Under International Financial Reporting Standards (as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board) (“IFRS”) 17, the impact of the annual review of actuarial methods and assumptions is reported in several places. The $128 million decrease in pre-tax fulfilment cash flows was comprised of a decrease in pre- tax net income attributed to shareholders of $217 million ( $175 million post-tax), a decrease in pre-tax net income attributed to participating policyholders of $4 million ( $3 million post-tax), an increase in CSM of $47 million, an increase in pre-tax other comprehensive income attributed to shareholders of $306 million ( $240 million post-tax), and a decrease in pre-tax other comprehensive income attributed to participating policyholders of $4 million ( $2 million post-tax). The actuarial review this year included valuation model enhancements and assumption updates for U.S. life insurance riders, morbidity and mortality assumptions in Vietnam, valuation model updates for participating products in Asia, lapse assumptions for investment-linked products in Singapore, mortality assumptions in our U.S. life insurance business, and other methodology refinements. Quarterly profitability Manulife’s net income attributed to shareholders was $1,424 million in the third quarter of 2026 (“ 3Q26 ”) compared with $1,799 million in the third quarter of 2025 (“ 3Q25 ”). Net income attributed to shareholders is comprised of core earnings (consisting of items we believe reflect the underlying earnings capacity of the business), which amounted to $2,125 million in 3Q26 compared with $2,035 million in 3Q25 , and items excluded from core earnings, which amounted to a net charge of $701 million in 3Q26 compared with a net charge of $ 236 million in 3Q25 . The effective tax rate on net income (loss) attributed to shareholders was 15% in 3Q26 compared with 14% in 3Q25 primarily due to differences in the jurisdictional mix of earnings and the impact of tax true-ups . Net income attributed to shareholders in 3Q26 decreased $375 million compared with 3Q25 , reflecting a larger net charge from market experience and a net charge from our Vietnam operations 1 , partially offset by growth in core earnings. Total market experience was a net charge of $ 388 million in 3Q26 , primarily from lower-than-expected returns on alternative long- duration assets (“ALDA”), mainly related to private equity, real estate and farmland investments, lower-than-expected returns on public equities and a net charge from derivatives and hedge accounting ineffectiveness . The increase in the net charge from market experience in 3Q26 compared with 3Q25 largely consisted of a net charge from lower-than-expected returns on public equities in 3Q26 compared to a net gain in 3Q25 and a higher net charge from derivatives and hedge accounting ineffectiveness, partially offset by a lower net charge from lower-than-expected returns on ALDA. Core earnings increased $90 million or 4% o n a constant exchange rate (“CER”) basis 2 compared with 3Q25 . The increase in our insurance business was driven by business growth , primarily in our Asia and U.S. segments , improved net insurance experience, and the net positive impact of 3Q26 updates to actuarial methods and assumptions, partially offset by an increase in the provision for expected credit losses (“ECL”) in 3Q26 compared with a release in 3Q25 3 . The improved net insurance experience reflects improved U.S. life insurance claims experience and more favourable Canada Individual Insurance claims experience, partially offset by unfavourable U.S. lapse experience and Canada Group Insurance claims experience in 3Q26 compared with favourable experience in 3Q25. Higher Manulife Bank earnings also contributed to the increase in core earnings. Global Wealth and Asset Management (“Global WAM”) core earnings decreased primarily due to the impact of the electronic Mandatory Provident Fund (“eMPF”) transition in Hong Kong, higher expenses and lower performance fees , partially offset by higher net fee income 4 and contributions from the Manulife | Comvest business 5 . These include sensitivities due to specific changes in market prices and interest rate levels projected using internal models as at a specific date, and are measured relative to a starting level reflecting the Company’s assets and liabilities at that date. The risk exposures measure the impact of changing one factor at a time and assume that all other factors remain unchanged. Actual results can differ materially from these estimates for a variety of reasons including the interaction among these factors when more than one changes; changes in liabilities from updates to non-economic assumptions, changes in business mix, effective tax rates and other market factors; and the general limitations of our internal models. For these reasons, the sensitivities should only be viewed as directional estimates of the underlying sensitivities for the respective factors based on the assumptions outlined below. Given the nature of these calculations, we cannot provide assurance that the actual impact on CSM net of NCI, net income attributed to shareholders, other comprehensive income attributed to shareholders, and total comprehensive income attributed to shareholders or on MLI’s LICAT ratio will be as indicated. Changes in public equity prices may impact other items including, but not limited to, asset-based fees earned on assets under management and administration or policyholder account value, and estimated profits and amortization of deferred policy acquisition and other costs. Core earnings (loss) is a financial measure which we believe aids investors in better understanding the long-term earnings capacity and valuation of the business. Core earnings allows investors to focus on the Company’s operating performance by excluding the impact of market-related gains or losses, and certain items such as the net impact of updates to actuarial methods and assumptions that flow directly through income as well as other items, outlined below, that we believe are material, but do not reflect the underlying earnings capacity of the business. For example, due to the long-term nature of our business, the mark-to-market movements in equity markets, interest rates including impacts on hedge accounting ineffectiveness, foreign currency exchange rates and commodity prices as well as the change in the fair value of ALDA from period-to-period can, and frequently do, have a substantial impact on the reported amounts of our assets, insurance contract liabilities and net income attributed to shareholders. These reported amounts may not be realized if markets move in the opposite direction in a subsequent period. This makes it very difficult for investors to evaluate how our businesses are performing from period-to-period and to compare our performance with other issuers. We believe that core earnings better reflect the underlying earnings capacity and valuation of our business. We use core earnings and core EPS as key metrics in our short-term incentive plans at the total Company and operating segment level. We also base our mid- and long-term strategic priorities on core earnings. Core earnings include the expected return on our invested assets and any other gains (charges) from market experience are included in net income but excluded from core earnings. The expected return for fixed income assets is based on the related book yields. For ALDA and public equities, the expected return reflects our long-term view of asset class performance. These returns for ALDA and public equities vary by asset class and range from 3.25% to 11.5%, leading to an average return of between 9.0% to 9.5% on these assets as of September 30, 2026 . While core earnings are relevant to how we manage our business and offer a consistent methodology, it is not insulated from macroeconomic factors which can have a significant impact.
