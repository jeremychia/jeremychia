It also reflects consistent execution of our strategic priorities with pace, precision and discipline. As we simplify HSBC, we are creating greater capacity to invest, grow and deliver strong returns for our shareholders.” Financial performance in 3Q26 – Profit before tax increased by $2.3bn or 32% to $9.6bn compared with 3Q25, primarily due to a net favourable impact from notable items of $1.4bn , which reflected the non-recurrence of legal provisions in 3Q25 of $1.4bn . The increase also reflected growth in revenue, mainly from banking net interest income (‘banking NII’) and lower expected credit losses and other credit impairment charges (‘ECL’). Profit after tax increased by $2.1bn or 38% to $7.6bn compared with 3Q25. – Constant currency profit before tax excluding notable items increased by $0.9bn to $10.0bn compared with 3Q25. – Annualised return on average tangible equity (‘RoTE‘) in 3Q26 was 17.7% compared with 12.3% in 3Q25. Excluding notable items, annualised RoTE in 3Q26 was 18.2% , a rise of 1.6 p ercentage points compared with 3Q25. – Revenue increased by $ 0.8 bn to $18.6bn compared with 3Q25, primarily reflecting growth in banking NII. F ee and other income also increased in our Corporate and Institutional Banking (‘CIB’) business in Wholesale Transaction Banking (‘WTB’) and in Debt and Equity Markets , supported by higher customer activity, and in Investment Distribution and Private Bank in Wealth. This was partly offset by valuation losses in Corporate Centre and t he non-recurrence of one-off favourable impacts in 3Q25 in Insurance . Constant currency revenue excluding notable items rose by $0.8bn to $18.7bn . – Net interest income (‘NII’) of $9.3bn increased by $0.5bn or 6% compared with 3Q25. The increase reflected deposit balance growth and the benefit of reinvestment of our structural hedge at higher yields, partly offset by lower NII from Markets Treasury and an adverse impact of $0.1bn from one-off items in 3Q26. Banking NII increased by $0.6bn to $11.7bn . Banking NII excludes insurance NII, which fell by $0.1bn, and the funding costs associated with the trading book, which remained broadly stable. – Net interest margin (‘NIM’) of 1.57% was stable compared with 3Q25. – ECL of $0.8bn decreased by $0.2bn or 17% compared with 3Q25, primarily reflecting a $0.1bn reduction in stage 3 charges related to the Hong Kong commercial real estate (‘CRE’) sector, as well as l ower charges in our UK business and International Wealth and Premier Banking (‘IWPB’) b usiness in Mexico. – Operating expenses of $8.8bn were $1.3bn or 12% lower compared with 3Q25. This included a favourable net impact of $1.4bn from notable items, which reflected the non-recurrence of legal provisions in 3Q25. Excluding notable items, the increase in operating expenses primarily reflected the impact of inflation, higher planned spend and investment in technology , and the phasing of the performance-related pay accrual relative to 3Q25, partly mitigated by the cost reduction benefits from our organisational simplification. Target basis operating expenses were $0.1bn or 1.4% higher than in 3Q25, including the impact of inflation and higher planned spend and investment in technology, partly offset by the cost reduction benefits from our organisational simplification. – Customer lending balances increased by $7.8bn compared with 2Q26 on a reported basis. On a constant currency basis, lending balances increased by $6.7bn . This primarily reflected growth as we supported our customers to participate in the India Foreign Currency Non-Resident Deposit scheme , together with growth in our U K and Hong Kong businesses. This was partly offset by the classification to held for sale of lending balances associated with the exit of our retail banking business in Australia in our IWPB business . – Customer accounts increased by $9.1bn compared with 2Q26 on a reported basis. On a constant currency basis, customer accounts increased by $6.5bn , reflecting continued momentum in deposit inflows in our Hong Kong business. In our IWPB business, deposit growth was mainly in India, as we facilitated customer participation i n the India Foreign Currency Non-Resident Deposit scheme. This growth was partly offset by retail deposit outflows following our announced exit of our retail banking business in Australia. In CIB, lower deposit balances reflected outflows of short-term balances . – Common equity tier 1 (‘CET1’) capital ratio of 13.9% decreased by 0.2 percentage points compared with 30 June 2026, reflecting an increase in risk-weighted assets (‘RWAs‘), together with a decrease in the fair value of hold-to-collect-and-sell debt instruments due to higher yields and the impact of the share buy-bac k announced at our interim results, partly offset by regulatory profits net of dividends. – The Board has approved a third interim dividend of $0.10 per share. On 28 September 2026, we completed the $1bn share buy-back announced at our i nterim results . We also intend to initiate a share buy-back of up to $1bn, which we expect to complete by our 2026 full- year results announcement. Outlook Group financial targets – We remain confident in achieving the targets we set out in February 2026, including a RoTE of 17% or better for 2026, 2027 and 2028, excluding notable items. – We continue to target year-on-year growth in revenue from 2026 to 2028, rising to 5% growth in 2028 compared with 2027, excluding notable items and on a constant currency basis. – We maintain our dividend payout ratio target basis of 50% for 2026, 2027 and 2028. Our target basis payout ratio is calculated as a percentage of earnings per share, excluding material notable items and related impacts. In respect of 2026 – We continue to expect banking NII of at least $46bn in 2026 . – We continue to expect an ECL charge as a percentage of average gross customer loans of around 45bps (including held for sale loan balances) for 2026. Over the medium term, we retain our planning range of 30–40bps. – The Group remains on track to deliver year-on-year growth in operating expenses of approximately 1% in 2026 on a target basis. As communicated at our 2026 interim results, should strong business performance continue, we may consider additional performance-related pay, which would increase 2026 target basis cost growth modestly. Our target basis operating expenses measure excludes notable items and includes the impact of simplification-related saves associated with our announced strategic reorganisation. Capital and regulatory developments – We continue to assess the anticipated impact of the final Basel 3.1 standards on our capital. We expect, based on our latest modelling, that the impact on the Group CET1 capital ratio a t 1 January 2027 will be a benefit of approximately 40–50bps. The final impact will depend on a number of factors, including model and data refinements, portfolio size and composition and supervisory permissions granted. Any related reductions to firm-specific capital requirements are not expected to be significant. – We intend to continue to manage our CET1 capital ratio within our medium-term target operating range of 14% to 14.5%. About HSBC HSBC Holdings plc, the parent company of HSBC, is headquartered in London. With assets of $ 3.4 tn at 30 September 2026 , HSBC is one of the world’s largest banking and financial services organisations. The Group‘s operating segments comprise of four businesses along with Corporate Centre: – Hong Kong – UK – Corporate and Institutional Banking – Internati onal Wealth and Prem ier Banking Our Hong Kong business comprises Retail Banking and Wealth and Commercial Banking of HSBC Hong Kong and Hang Seng Bank Limited (‘Hang Seng Bank‘). Our UK business comprises UK Retail Banking and Wealth (including first direct and M&S Bank) and UK Commercial Banking, including HSBC Innovation Bank. CIB integrates our Commercial Banking business (outside of the UK and Hong Kong) with our Global Banking and Markets business. IWPB comprises Premier banking outside of Hong Kong and the UK, our Private Bank, Asset Management and Insurance businesses. Corporate Centre results primarily comprise the financial impact from certain acquisitions and disposals and the share of profit, dilution and impairment loss impacts from interests in our associates and joint ventures . It also includes Central Treasury, stewardship costs and consolidation adjustments. Reshaping the Group for growth Privatisation of Hang Seng Bank On 26 January 2026, we completed our privatisation of Hang Seng Bank, following shareholder and Court approval. Hang Seng Bank is now a wholly-owned subsidiary of the HSBC Group and Hang Seng Bank shares have been withdrawn from the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. Through the privatisation of Hang Seng Bank, we expect to realise $0.5bn in annualised pre-tax revenue and cost synergies across both our brands in Hong Kong by the end of 2028. We expect to incur associated restructuring costs of $0.6bn, of which one-off income statement impacts would be reported as material notable items. We also have an ambition to generate further annualised revenue and cost opportunities of around $0.4bn by the end of 2028 across both our brands in Hong Kong. Organisational simplification At our 2024 full-year results we announced measures to simplify the Group, committing to deliver an annualised reduction of around $1.5bn in our cost base by the end of 2026, spending $1.8bn in restructuring and other related costs. At our 1H26 interim results, having already exceeded our savings commitment of $1.5bn, we revised our simplification savings commitment with an intention to action around $2bn of annualised savings by the end of 2026, spending up to our originally expected $1.8bn of restructuring costs. The additional saves we have brought into the programme generate capacity that will be used to support the growth of our four businesses. Since the start of 2025, we have identified and actioned annualised cost savings of approximately $1.9bn, spending associated restructuring and other related costs of $1.6bn. These actions have resulted in a reduction of around $0.6bn in operating expenses in the income statement in 2025, and a $0.8bn reduction in 9M26. Strategic transactions We remain focused on opportunities where we have a clear competitive advantage and accretive returns, and we aim to redeploy approximately $1.8bn of additional costs saved from non-strategic activities into these areas over the medium term. This includes the additional $0.3bn of costs saved from the synergies generated from our privatisation of Hang Seng Bank. During the third quarter of 2026, we completed the sale of our operations in Uruguay. In addition, we announced the sale of HSBC Life Singapore. In Australia, we announced the sale of our portfolio of home and personal loans , the planned wind-down of the remaining retail business and the transfer of our CIB, Private Banking and Asset Management businesses to the Sydney branch of The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation Limited, simplifying our entity footprint. In Egypt, we announced the sale of our retail banking business . The completion of announced disposals and related actions remains subject to relevant regulatory and other approvals. We have commenced a strategic review of our retail business and domestic-focused small and mid-sized corporate banking operations in Türkiye. Our international wholesale client business in Türkiye is unaffected by this review. Ñ For further details on business disposals, see page 15 . Progress in growth areas Wealth balances increased by 3% to $ 1.6 tn at 30 September 2026 compared with 31 December 2025. We attracted net new money (‘NNM‘) in 9M26 of $105bn, with $95bn booked in Asia, partly offset by the impact of classifications to held for sale and portfolio exits. Transaction banking continues to perform well as we leverage our network and capabilities to capture opportunities from changing trade and capital flows. In 9M26, fee and other income in WTB rose by 8% compared with 9M25, or by 6% on a constant currency basis, reflecting increases in Global Foreign Exchange, Securities Services, Global Trade Solutions (‘GTS‘) and Global Payment Solutions (‘GPS‘). Notable items and material notable items We separately disclose ‘notable items’, which are components of our income statement that management would consider as outside the normal course of business and generally non-recurring in nature. Certain notable items are classified as ‘material notable items’, which are a subset of notable items. Categorisation as a material notable item is dependent on the nature of each item in conjunction with the financial impact on the Group‘s income statement, and such items are excluded from our target basis dividend payout ratio calculation and earnings per share measure. Material notable items in 9M26 or relevant comparative periods relate to the operating expenses associated with actions to exit or wind down non-strategic businesses. Madoff Investment Securities LLC fraud in 9M25. Ñ The tables on pages 27 to 31 and pages 37 to 41 detail the effects of notable items on each of our business segments and legal entities. Impact of strategic transactions In addition to the items categorised as material notable items, the impacts of strategic transactions include the distorting impact observed between the periods of the operating income statement results related to acquisitions, disposals and wind-downs that affect period-on-period comparisons. Once a transaction has completed or a wind-down has commenced, the impact will include the operating income statement results of each business, which are not classified as notable items, in any comparative period if there are no results in the current period as a result of a transaction, or a reduction in revenue or costs has arisen from the wind-down of a business. We consider the monthly impact of distorting income statement results when calculating the impact of strategic transactions. In the case of wind-downs, or transactions that complete in phased tranches, there may be timing differences between the recognition of operating cost impacts and operating revenue impacts. These would arise in the event that there is a timing lag between the impact of cost actions and the resultant impact on operating revenue. Financial investments As part of our interest rate hedging strategy and liquidity management we hold a portfolio of debt instruments, reported within financial investments, which are classified as hold-to-collect-and-sell. As a result, the change in value of these instruments is recognised through ‘debt instruments at fair value through other comprehensive income’ in equity. At 3Q26, we recognised a pre-tax cumulative unrealised loss reserve through other comprehensive income of $5.0bn related to these hold-to-collect-and-sell positions, excluding investments held in our Insurance business. This compared with an unrealised loss of $2.4bn at 2Q26, and reflected a $2.6bn pre-tax loss in 3Q26, inclusive of movements on related fair value hedges. We also hold a portfolio of financial investments measured at amortised cost, which are classified as hold-to-collect. At 3Q26, the debt instruments within this portfolio had a cumulative unrecognised loss of $7.0bn, representing a $4.9bn deterioration during 3Q26. Risk-weighted assets RWAs of $ 922.4 bn increased by $ 16.0 bn compared with 30 June 2026, primarily due to asset size movements of $ 18.7 bn, driven primarily by IWPB due to increases in sovereign exposures arising from participation in the Foreign Currency Non-Resident Deposit scheme in India, lending in our CIB and UK businesses and financial commitments in our Hong Kong business.
