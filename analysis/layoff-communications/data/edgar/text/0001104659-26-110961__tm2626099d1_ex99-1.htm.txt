Business Overview Description of Business LuxExperience Group is a leading luxury multi-brand digital group for the global luxury consumer shopping worldwide. We offer one of the finest edits in luxury, curated from the world’s most coveted brands of womenswear, menswear, kidswear, lifestyle products and fine jewelry. Our story began over three decades ago with the opening of Theresa, in Munich, one of the first multi-brand luxury boutiques in Germany, followed by the launch of the digital platform Mytheresa in 2006. In April 2025, we completed the acquisition of YOOX Net-a-Porter Group S.p.A. (“YNAP”), with the heritage store brands NET-A- PORTER, the leading luxury fashion destination for women since 2000, and MR PORTER, the men’s luxury style destination since 2011, forming the luxury segment next to the Mytheresa luxury segment, and acquiring YOOX, the multi-brand digital platform for long-lasting fashion, design and art since 2000 and THE OUTNET in the Off-Price segment. We subsequently renamed the combined company “LuxExperience Group”. Our business model combines technology, luxury fashion and differentiated customer service on a global scale. The simplicity of our mobile-first websites and apps (“sites”) creates an efficient and user-friendly shopping experience for our time- constrained, global customers. Our sites offer advanced features, including the ability to personalize the customer experience and other highly attractive customer services. We have an efficient, repeatable playbook for localizing the customer experience through local language, currencies, payment methods, shipping services and marketing. On April 30, 2026, LuxExperience Group successfully sold the set of assets powering THE OUTNET platform to The O Group LLC (which has been renamed The Outnet Operations US, LLC). LuxExperience Group’s Transformation Plan We announced a transformation plan in May 2025, designed to restore growth and strengthen the financial performance of YNAP’s businesses after years of decline. As we execute this plan and integrate YNAP into LuxExperience Group, we expect to realize substantial synergies through a shared infrastructure and technology platform, as well as meaningful operational efficiency improvements. We remain on track in executing the transformation plan and delivering against its key objectives. Since the YNAP Acquisition, we have initiated targeted cost reduction measures across the legacy YNAP operations, including streamlining the global warehouse footprint, optimizing the customer service provider landscape, consolidating the Luxury Digital Production activities in Milan Morimondo, and renegotiating global carrier contracts. These initiatives are expected to deliver significant and sustainable savings over time while improving the scalability of our operations. We have also started migrating Luxury | NAP & MRP onto LuxExperience Group’s proprietary technology platform and simplifying the separate off-price technology environment, both of which are designed to enhance technological agility, efficiency, and innovation across all segments. During the year, we also made significant progress toward the implementation of our new ERP platform, further supporting the simplification and centralization of our technology landscape. In parallel, we have implemented a joint data analytics layer across the Company to leverage insights from differentiated data sources and support more advanced customer and operational analytics. Alongside these operational initiatives, we have advanced several strategic transformation programs to simplify the Group and strengthen our operating model. We successfully completed transaction of the assets powering THE OUTNET, an important milestone in streamlining the Group portfolio and structure. We established The Net-a-Porter Group Italy S.r.l. (the Luxury Italian NewCo), supporting the separation of the Luxury and YOOX businesses, and contributing to the simplification and reorganization of the former YNAP legal entity structure. 8 Finally, we continue to simplify and consolidate the corporate administrative structure while evolving the organization to better align capabilities with business priorities. This evolution will create a leaner, more transparent and effective operating model. Together, these initiatives are expected to improve organizational effectiveness, deliver further general and administrative expense savings, and support long-term profitability. Distinct Customer Profiles Across Our Platforms Each of our three segments has established and differentiated customer bases. Our luxury segments, Mytheresa and NAP & MRP, acquire and retain customers who are predominantly working professionals with significant spending power and limited time, shop frequently, seek luxury products that are not easily found elsewhere and demand superior customer service. These customers are high-net worth individuals that value quality over price and curation over assortment breadth and there is limited overlap of approximately 12% of our customers between our two luxury segments. Our YOOX off-price segment, caters to a different audience of global luxury shoppers seeking access to previous-season collections from the world’s leading designers. This customer base is clearly differentiated from that of our luxury segments, with only approximately 3% overlap with our luxury segments. Mytheresa, NET-A -PORTER, MR PORTER, and YOOX, have each built a strong reputation for innovation, inspiration, and exceptional customer service. Together, these storefronts span the full spectrum of the luxury market, offering distinct brand portfolios, customer profiles, and geographical focuses—yet united by a shared positioning toward high-end, discerning consumers. As LuxExperience Group, we bring these differentiated strengths together into one cohesive digital group. We deliver a unique experience that combines exclusive product and content offerings with best- in-class technology, advanced analytics, and premium customer service. More than an online retailer, LuxExperience Group is building a community for global luxury enthusiasts—creating desirability through distinctive digital engagement and curated physical experiences as a global leader in multi-brand luxury e-commerce. To reward and engage our most valued customers, our luxury segments offer benefits for our most valued customers. These benefits, such as first access to runway and exclusive pieces, previews of new season styles, dedicated personal shopping services and invitations to exclusive events and fashion shows as well as other “money-can’t-buy” experiences. The customers in these programs account for a substantial portion of our GMV across our three segments. NET-A-PORTER and MR PORTER generated around 49.1% of its GMV from approximately 4.3% of its customers who were part of its Top Customer program. The exclusive events, collections and campaigns that we create with our luxury brand partners highlight the innovation and creativity we bring to the luxury fashion world, underpin the strong relationships we have with these brands, and enable us to deepen connections with our most valued customers. Our Industry We operate at the intersection of luxury goods, technology and services. We see ourselves as a winner in an otherwise still tough market environment. We believe we are uniquely positioned to further capture market share as a result of our exclusive, highly curated product assortment, leading service offering and advanced technology. 9 Luxury Market The personal luxury goods market, inclusive of luxury apparel, accessories, beauty and hard goods, is expected to continue growing, reaching approximately €365 billion to €373 billion in 2026, according to Bain & Company and Altagamma’s Luxury Goods Worldwide Market Study, Spring 2026 (the “2026 Bain Study”). We believe luxury is one of the last attractive categories to expand online and is relatively underpenetrated compared to traditional apparel and footwear. Growth is expected to continue, with Boston Consulting Group and Altagamma’s True- Luxury Global Consumer Insights 2026 (the “2026 BCG- Altagamma Study”) forecasting growth of 2% to 5% in 2026 and compound annual growth of 4% to 7% from 2026 to 2029. Consumers generally approach the market in a borderless manner, often purchasing luxury goods across multiple continents, seeking an elevated shopping experience and anytime access wherever their travels take them. Wealthiest Consumers are Driving Growth and Resilient Demand The global luxury market continues to be supported by the sustained expansion of high-net-worth individuals (“HNWIs”)—those with investable assets of at least $1 million—who represent a key customer demographic with significant purchasing power in the luxury sector. According to Capgemini’s World Wealth Report 2026, the global HNWI population grew by 7.9% in 2025 to 25.3 million, while total HNWI wealth increased by 8.7% to approximately $98.3 trillion. The continued rise in both the number and wealth of HNWIs underscores the resilience of the global luxury customer base and its importance as a primary growth engine of the luxury industry. Components of our Results of Operations Net sales Net sales consist of revenues earned from sales of clothing, bags, shoes, accessories, fine jewelry and other categories through our sites and our two retail stores, as well as shipping revenue and delivery duties paid when applicable, net of promotional discounts and returns. The platform fees originating from the curated platform model, other commission fees and retail media revenues are also included in our net sales. Revenue is generally recognized upon delivery to the end customer. Changes in our reported net sales are mainly driven by changes in the number and quality of our active customers, changes in average order value, the total number of orders shipped and fees in relation to our curated platform model. Cost of sales, exclusive of depreciation and amortization Cost of sales, exclusive of depreciation and amortization includes the cost of merchandise sold, net of trade discounts, in addition to inventory write-offs and delivery costs of product from our brand partners. These costs fluctuate with changes in net sales and changes in inventory write-offs due to inventory aging. For CPM and other commission revenue, we do not incur cost of sales as the purchase price of the goods sold is borne by the CPM brand partner. 17 Gross Profit Gross profit equals our net sales reduced by cost of sales, exclusive of depreciation and amortization. Risk Management and Risk Factors 4.1 Maintaining effective internal risk management and control system 4.1.1 Introduction At LuxExperience Group, we place great importance on maintaining control over our operations and achieving our strategic goals. For this reason, we implement an internal risk management and control system to manage risks efficiently and effectively, safeguarding our strategy and objectives. This system is seamlessly embedded into our daily processes and activities. This section describes our internal risk management and control system, while also outlining the evaluation procedures performed throughout the year and their key outcomes. Our commitment has always been to maintain a robust internal risk management and control system aligned with our risk register. As a listed company in the United States, LuxExperience Group implemented an internal control system back in 2021, based on the requirements of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act and in line with the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (COSO) framework. With the adoption of the globally recognized industry-standard, LuxExperience Group was able to establish, evaluate, and strengthen their internal controls and enterprise risk management. By aligning governance, risk assessment, and control activities, it helps businesses prevent fraud, maintain financial integrity, and achieve strategic goals. 35 With this setup, LuxExperience Group included the scope of risk management and compliance, the coverage of financial and operational risks as well as the integration of the positive change report for full coverage of all sustainability/ESG ambitions. This is also exemplified by the introduction of the Risk Management Declaration (“Verklaring omtrent Risicobeheersing” or “VOR”) in the Dutch Corporate Governance Code. Based on the VOR, we encompass operational, compliance, and sustainability reporting risks. Our priority over the last years and in the coming years will be to enhance our management of these expanding risk categories where applicable. Although we devote much attention to the design, implementation and evaluation of the internal control system, there is no absolute assurance that these will avoid or mitigate every risk that LuxExperience Group faces. As a learning organization, we continuously evaluate and improve this system. This ongoing evolution allows us to respond effectively to new insights and developments. Should any shortcomings be identified during our review processes, we are committed to taking prompt and appropriate action to address them. To implement and oversee the VOR, LuxExperience Group established a steering group involving experts from the Management Board, Risk Management & Compliance, Financial Reporting, and LuxExperience Group Internal Audit. The key activities for the VOR were as follows: · Select appropriate international frameworks for the design and evaluation of internal risk management and control system · Define the certainty levels for compliance and operational risks based on the Management Board risk register (the required assurance levels for reporting risks are already predefined by the Code) · Discuss the draft approach, VOR statement, and related substantiations with the Management Board, the Supervisory Board and the Audit Committee · Identify the primary risks per category and document the key control measures implemented to manage these risks within the established risk register · Evaluate the effectiveness of these measures—ensuring risks are managed broadly in line with the risk register and required assurance or certainty levels—and define improvement actions In FY2026, the Management Board, Supervisory Board and Audit Committee were informed on a quarterly basis about progress of the activities and findings from both the external auditor and Internal Audit. Within the upcoming sections, LuxExperience Group included a VOR statement and the design and evaluation of the internal risk management and internal control system. 36 4.1.1.2 Annual Assessment Methodology for Internal Risk & Control Systems The Management Board and Supervisory Board, supported by its Audit Committee systematically evaluates the design and operating effectiveness of the internal risk management and control systems at least once per year. This evaluation follows a three-stage process managed by the Internal Audit team and is overseen by the Audit Committee: - Scoping & Internal Control Testing: All business units are in scope for the annual evaluation based on the COSO Internal Control Framework. This is done to evaluate key operational, compliance, financial, and sustainability control activities and risks - Data Aggregation & Gap Analysis: Control deficiencies, audit observations, and operational incidents collected across all lines of defense are aggregated into a centralized quarterly report by the Internal Audit team to the Management Board, Supervisory Board and Audit Committee - Supervisory Board Oversight/Management Board Review: The Management Board, Supervisory Board, and Audit Committee reviews the consolidated quarterly evaluation of the internal control system, establishes prioritized remediation plans and reviews the overall conclusion with the support of the Risk & SOX Compliance team This implemented process ensures that qualitative feedback and formal audit findings systematically inform the Management and Supervisory Board’s final declaration on control effectiveness. 4.1.2 Governance 4.1.2.1 Three lines model The Management Board and Supervisory Board are ultimately responsible for identifying, assessing, and managing the risks LuxExperience Group faces in executing our strategy and activities. They are also responsible for setting our risk register and for designing, implementing, and maintaining our internal risk management and control system, as well as monitoring their effectiveness. LuxExperience Group applies the so-called “three lines” model, which comprises: · First line: Business management is responsible for effectively identifying, assessing, and managing the company’s primary risks within the risk register set by the Management Board and Supervisory Board, in line with the steps outlined in this section. Business management also owns the risks, control frameworks, and other risk responses · Second line: SOX Compliance and Risk Management, IT Risk and Compliance, and the General Counsel/Compliance Officer are responsible for risk management, compliance, and security procedures. They are also responsible for supporting and challenging business management in their assessment and management of the main risks · Third line: LuxExperience Group Internal Audit performs independent reviews based on its approved audit plan and risk assessment. The internal risk management and control system will be assessed at least twice a year for the design and operating effectiveness, as well as testing controls for operational effectiveness. The primary results of both the risk assessments and the evaluation of the internal risk management and control system are shared with the Management Board and Supervisory Board and discussed with the Audit Committee as well as with the external auditor The Management Board and Supervisory Board, supported by its Audit Committee, oversees LuxExperience Group’s internal risk management and control system, including the company’s risk register and the design and operating effectiveness of this system. 4.1.2.2 Use of international frameworks For the generic design, implementation and evaluation of the internal risk management and control system we use guidance as described in the COSO Internal Control – Integrated Framework (2013). For specific risk categories and where relevant, additional internationally accepted frameworks are used to identify inherent risks and to design and evaluate control measures, such as ISO standards. 37 4.1.3 Risk Management and Internal Control System 4.1.3.1 Risk Management Process With the integrated internal risk management and control system to manage the primary risks that could hinder the achievement of the company’s objectives, LuxExperience Group ensures the alignment with the risk register. LuxExperience Group´s classification of the risk categories is as follows: 1) Financial Reporting Risks: Risks that could lead to material misstatements in LuxExperience Group’s financial statements 2) Sustainability Reporting Risks (Environmental, Health, and Safety): Risks that could lead to material misstatements in LuxExperience Group’s sustainability statement 3) Business Risks: Risks that could disrupt operational processes and negatively impact the quality of service delivered to customers 4) Legal/Compliance Risks: Risks that could result in the breach of applicable laws and regulations 5) IT Risks: Risks that could arise from the IT landscape (including systems and access to systems), and cybersecurity threats The following sections detail the internal risk management and control system, as well as the specific measures taken to mitigate risks across each respective category. Additionally, we outline the evaluation procedures used to assess the effectiveness of these systems. All results of the internal assessments and testing are shared quarterly with the Management Board and Supervisory Board to define remediation actions if necessary. 4.1.3.2 Risk Management Process and Control Environment The tone from the top of the Management Board and Supervisory Board, aims for clear and compliant business culture within the day to day business of LuxExperience Group. With the integration of the three lines model, a group wide code of conduct, regular communication and training programs on compliance and integrity issues, a whistleblower process, the implementation of the internal control system, and corporate policies and guidelines forms a strong and robust business culture. The base for all builds the internal control system with a broad set of entity level controls, process level controls, IT general controls, and IT application controls that apply across the entire organization and its subsidiaries. In order to manage risks in an effective and efficient way it is necessary to understand and assess each risk individually and to evaluate potential interdependencies between identified risks. Risk responses can be grouped into four option types: 40 Risk Response Objective and Procedures Avoidance Avoidance of the initiative/transaction/activity that potentially causes the risk. Based on the informed decision not to become involved in a risk situation, or to cease activities in a particular area, because the risk is assessed as too high (e.g. delisting of a good due to quality issues). Mitigation (or Reduction) Reduction of risks aims at decreasing of either the likelihood of occurrence or the impact (or both) of a risk to a tolerable level. This can be done by technical or organizational measures, or risk diversification (e.g. listing of multiple suppliers for one product). Transfer Transfer potential risk impact to a third party by changing the contractual terms (e.g. with a supplier or service provider), or to an insurance company by conclusion of a contract to cover the risk. Acceptance Informed decision to accept the probability and impact of a particular risk provided the level of risk falls within the risk appetite of the business. Risk acceptance is a viable strategy for small risks (low probability, low risk impact) where the costs of other alternatives are higher than the total risk impact sustained.
