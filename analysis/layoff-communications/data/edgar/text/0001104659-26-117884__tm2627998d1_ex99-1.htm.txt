Curragh Complex Curragh continued to advance its operational reset program during the September quarter, delivering further evidence of improvement in open cut mining performance, mine plan execution and operational flexibility. ROM production increased 7.9% quarter on quarter to 4.2Mt from 3.9Mt in the June quarter, representing the highest quarterly ROM production since the September 2020 quarter. The increase reflects improved mining productivity and successful execution of key southern mining area milestones under the revised mine plan. A reduction in strip ratio and consequent reduction in waste movement at Curragh South reflects ongoing implementation of the revised mine sequence and supports the Company’s view that the operation is beginning to realize the benefits associated with the reset strategy. These outcomes are expected to support improved operating efficiency and reduced capital intensity over time. Inventory levels continued to build throughout the quarter, improving operational flexibility and supporting customer commitments. Curragh ROM inventories increased to approximately 830Kt at quarter end, providing increased flexibility ahead of the wet season and a stronger platform for converting ROM into higher-value saleable tonnes as CHPP performance continues to improve. Product quality and product mix outcomes also improved during the period. The business continued reducing its loss-making thermal coal sales while increasing the proportion of higher-value Met Coal production. Although PCI production declined, the overall quality and margin profile of production improved relative to the June quarter. CHPP reliability and operating stability continued to be a primary focus during the quarter. Plant performance remains the principal operational constraint at Curragh, but ongoing improvements in reliability and runtime are increasingly allowing management to shift focus toward throughput optimisation, recovery improvement and conversion of existing ROM inventories into saleable tonnes. The Company believes improving throughput and recoveries represent the most significant near-term opportunity to enhance production performance and unlock additional value from the existing mining activities. Mammoth’s ramp-up remained below expectations during the September quarter and continues to be an important focus area. Underground mining operations have been particularly impacted by extensive faulting in two of the panels. The mine operator, Mammoth Underground Mine Management Pty Ltd (a subsidiary of Eclipse Mining Pty Ltd), is implementing an extensive improvement program as its understanding of the geology in this mine develops. While further work remains across Mammoth and CHPP performance, the Company believes the operational reset program is delivering measurable improvement across a range of key operating metrics and is establishing stronger foundations for sustainable earnings and cash generation. Buchanan Buchanan successfully progressed through a significant geological fault encountered within the southern longwall panel during the September quarter, and longwall advance rates have since largely returned to normal. The operation continues to benefit from the flexibility created through the expanded operating footprint established by the Buchanan expansion project. The northern longwall partially offset lost production during the period despite hard-cutting conditions, and demonstrated the operational resilience provided by the dual-longwall configuration. While temporary in nature, the geological conditions at Buchanan’s southern longwall represented the most significant adverse factor affecting Group performance during the quarter. The faulting reduced ROM production by approximately 0.7Mt relative to the June quarter and materially affected production of premium low-volatility Met Coal products. Additional production support requirements and void fill activities associated with safely progressing through the affected zone increased operating costs during the period. Combined with lower production and sales volumes, these factors resulted in a material increase in unit costs and significantly reduced earnings contribution from the operation. The disruption also affected operating leverage within the business, with lower production volumes reducing the ability to recover fixed operating costs across the production base. The Company does not view the geological conditions encountered during the quarter as indicative of any deterioration in the quality, capability or long-term value of the asset. The operation has successfully advanced through the faulted area and normal longwall advancement rates have subsequently been restored. Safety and Health As at 30 September 2026, the Group TRIR was 1.42, up 6% from 1.34 for the June quarter of 2026. The Company will continue to prioritise safety as its most important responsibility and remains committed to delivering long-term improvements that create a safer workplace for all employees and contractors. During the quarter, the Company continued the implementation of its leadership training program across all operations. The program is aimed at strengthening visible safety leadership, enhancing critical risk management practices and reinforcing frontline engagement to ensure safety standards are consistently understood, applied and maintained across the business. The program brings together leaders from all levels of the organisation and is designed to reinforce accountability, improve hazard identification and intervention capabilities and enhance the quality of safety interactions in the field. Through regular site engagements, leadership observations and workforce participation, the Company is seeking to further embed a proactive safety culture that encourages shared ownership of safety outcomes and improvement. The leadership program is supported by a range of ongoing initiatives, including critical control verification activities, targeted training programs, field leadership interactions and focused reviews of key operational risks. Together, these measures are intended to strengthen risk management, improve safety performance and support the Company's objective of preventing serious injuries and fatalities across all operations. Sales Group Sales volumes of 3.6Mt in the September quarter were 2% higher than the June quarter despite lower production, due to the drawdown of inventory built in prior periods. Sales volumes were constrained by port congestion (Curragh experienced 10–14 day loading delays alone), with approximately 630Kt of coal held at port awaiting shipment at quarter end across the Group. The delayed vessels represented approximately US$50M of revenue that is expected to be realised in the December quarter, with most vessels already having sailed in October. The Company has also seen some improvement in port congestion conditions since quarter end. The Group maintained a favourable sales mix during the period, with Met Coal continuing to represent the majority of sales volumes. Stronger realised pricing outcomes, supported by higher benchmark PLV HCC pricing, contributed positively to earnings performance during the quarter. Group realised Met Coal pricing increased to US$183.5/t, up from US$170.5/t in the June quarter, with an average Australian realised Met Coal price of US$186.3/t and an average U.S. realised Met Coal price of US$177.0/t. Australian realised Met Coal pricing improved to 75.1% of the PLV HCC FOB Australia benchmark, up from 72.1% in the June quarter, reflecting continued improvements in product mix, marketing initiatives and commercial execution. Benchmark PLV HCC FOB Australia pricing averaged approximately US$248.1/t during the quarter, an increase of 4.1% from the June quarter, providing strong support for Realised pricing and earnings across the Group. Inventory levels improved throughout the period, providing greater operational flexibility and partially offsetting the impact of lower saleable production. This allowed the Group to continue servicing customers while rebuilding operating momentum across both Curragh and Buchanan. METALLURGICAL COAL MARKETS The Met Coal market remained supportive during the September quarter due to ongoing supply constraints in China, resilient steel demand across key growth markets and continued discipline across global seaborne supply. Chinese domestic coal production remained below normal levels throughout the quarter as safety inspections and operational restrictions continued to limit output from a number of mines. These supply constraints helped support seaborne import demand despite weaker profitability across China's steel sector. Chinese steel production conditions remained mixed during the quarter. While seasonal steel demand improved ahead of national holidays and supported shipments, steel margins remained under pressure and a large proportion of steel QUARTERLY REPORT SEPTEMBER 2026 QUARTERLY REPORT: Q3, SEP 2026 5 mills continued to operate at limited profitability. Hot metal production softened modestly during the period as some blast furnaces reduced operating rates or entered maintenance programs. Outside China, demand conditions remained more supportive. India's steel market continued to demonstrate resilience, supported by infrastructure activity, automotive demand and expectations of stronger post-monsoon steel consumption. While buyers remained cautious in response to elevated Met Coal prices, improving steel market conditions are expected to support a gradual recovery in import demand following the monsoon season. On the supply side, global market conditions remained relatively balanced. Australian Met Coal production and exports continued to recover during the quarter, supported by favourable operating conditions and improved performance across several operations. At the same time, disruptions affecting Russian logistics and ongoing challenges in parts of North America continued to limit supply growth from certain exporting regions. Market sentiment softened towards the end of the quarter as expectations of gradual Chinese mine restarts, recovering Mongolian supply and weaker steel margins moderated demand expectations. Nevertheless, seaborne Met Coal markets continued to benefit from constrained supply growth, supportive demand across major steel-producing economies and the strategic importance of high-quality metallurgical coal in blast furnace steel production. FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE Financial performance strengthened during the September quarter, resulting in significantly higher earnings compared to the prior quarter, driven by higher realised pricing, lower total mining costs at Curragh, an improved production mix and the build of unsold coal into inventory. Coal revenues increased to approximately $558M during the quarter (June 2026 quarter: $506M) while Group Realised pricing increased to US$183.5/t, benefiting from stronger benchmark pricing and continued commercial optimisation initiatives. Available liquidity increased by $57M to $155M at 30 September 2026, mainly supported by the Glencore prepayment financing arrangement. Cash flow during the quarter was affected by timing items, principally customer receipts moved into October by port congestion, the build of ROM and port inventories, and the senior secured notes interest and insurance payments, together with the impact of geological conditions on Buchanan production, partly offset by supplier payment deferrals that are expected to reverse in the December quarter. Adjusting for these items, and excluding the prepayment arrangements, the underlying business was broadly cash neutral for the quarter, supported by the significant improvement in earnings, stronger pricing and lower mining cash costs at Curragh. Net debt was $553M at 30 September 2026 (compared to $606M at 30 June 2026). Inventory build represented a significant use of cash during the quarter. The Curragh ROM build was partly deliberate, to prepare for the wet season and to support plant feed continuity and customer commitments, and partly the result of CHPP constraints and port congestion. These inventories are expected to convert to sales and cash progressively as plant performance and port conditions improve. The largest adverse impact on earnings and cash flow was the geological conditions encountered at Buchanan's southern longwall panel. The temporary reduction in production volumes, lost Met Coal tonnes and additional operating activities required to manage the faulted zone materially impacted profitability during the period. Improvements across Curragh open cut performance, product mix optimisation, inventory positioning and operational efficiency initiatives provide support for the Company’s view that the underlying cash-generating capability of the business is improving. Completion of the Logan divestment in July 2026 further simplified the portfolio and removed a loss-making operation from the Group. The Company remains focused on converting improving operational performance, stronger inventory levels, better product mix outcomes and increasing production consistency into sustainable free cash flow generation. While meaningful progress has been achieved through the Curragh operational reset program, improved inventory positioning, advancement of the Southern open cut mining operations and a range of productivity and cost improvement initiatives, the timing of expected improvements in CHPP performance has been slower than originally anticipated, and production was affected by geological conditions at Buchanan and the slower Mammoth ramp-up. As a result, the Company now expects full year Saleable production of 14.0Mt to 14.5Mt. The revision reflects lost production at Buchanan arising from the south longwall geological conditions, the slower Mammoth ramp-up and slower than expected improvement in Curragh CHPP throughput. Consistent with the reset program's focus on margin over volume and on building inventory buffers to stabilise the production system, the Company has chosen not to bypass ROM coal as low-value thermal product, which could have increased reported volumes. Curragh expects to end the year with approximately 1.5Mt of ROM coal on stockpiles, equivalent to approximately 1.1Mt of Saleable production at a 75% yield, which we expect will be processed into higher-value products in 2027.
