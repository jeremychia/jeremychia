Multiple scenario approach Model description Under IFRS 9, allowances for credit losses are based on reasonable and supportable information available without undue cost or effort, which take into consideration past events, current conditions and forecasts of future economic conditions. From the third quarter of 2026 onwards, Deutsche Bank uses four distinct scenarios as the regular basis for forward looking credit loss projections. These scenarios are designed to assess various alternatives of plausible trajectories for the economy and financial markets over the next three calendar years, with stable trends in years four and five of the scenario horizon. The scenarios utilized consist of a central baseline, an optimistic baseline, a pessimistic baseline and a stress scenario as described below. They are designed to support the robustness of ECL calculations against a wider set of potential economic dynamics. The Group's Risk and Finance Credit Loss Provision Forum monitors the scenario designs and their weights on a quarterly basis and recommends changes to the standard setup as necessitated by macroeconomic or geo-political events, which are approved by senior management in Risk. Bloomberg consensus forecasts remain the basis for the central baseline as well as the optimistic and pessimistic baseline scenarios. For all scenarios, projections of additional macro-financial variables which are required for the ECL calculations but are not available from the consensus forecasts, market-implied curves, or the assumed shocks in case of the stress scenario are generated applying a statistical scenario expansion model. The stress scenario describes a severe deterioration of the macroeconomic and financial market outlook over the forecast horizon and is typically derived from a recent scenario used for Group-wide Stress Testing (GwST). 34 Deutsche Bank Risk information Earnings Report as of September 30, 2026 Scenario assumptions and weighting Deutsche Bank established the multiple scenario approach using a standard set of scenarios and weights as a starting point, subject to review and potential adjustment. Each scenario receives a weight which is considered in the ECL calculation. The standard setup of weights features the following split: The central baseline scenario receives a 70% weight, reflecting the tendency of forecasts to cluster around the central estimate, with more than two-thirds of observations typically deviating by less than one standard deviation. Assuming broadly balanced risks, the remaining probability is evenly split across the upside and downside scenarios, hence the optimistic baseline receives a standard weight of 15%. The pessimistic baseline and the stress scenario combined are allocated the remaining 15%, with the pessimistic baseline receiving a weight of 10% and the stress scenario a weight of 5%. Weights and scenarios can be modified to express shifts in the outlook or events that are not appropriately reflected in the standard setup; for instance, by giving more weight to pessimistic scenarios, adjusting relevant scenario features, or adding a new scenario with appropriate weight which captures economic dynamics outside the standard set. Changes to the scenarios or weights will be proposed, reviewed and challenged in the Group's Risk and Finance Credit Loss Provision Forum and approved by senior management in Risk. For purposes of the Group’s primary financial reporting outside the United States, the Group prepares its consolidated financial statements in accordance with IFRS as endorsed by the EU. For purposes of the Group’s consolidated financial statements prepared in accordance with IFRS as endorsed by the EU, the Group applies fair value hedge accounting for portfolio hedges of interest rate risk (fair value macro hedges) in accordance with the EU carve out version of IAS 39. The purpose of applying the EU carve out version of IAS 39, is to align the Group’s hedge accounting approach with its risk management practice and the accounting practice of its major European peers. Under the EU carve out version of IAS 39 fair value macro hedge accounting may be applied to core deposits. In addition, the EU carve out version of IAS 39 hedge ineffectiveness is only recognized when the revised estimate of the amount of cash flows in scheduled time buckets falls below the original designated amount of that bucket. If the revised amount of cash flows in scheduled time buckets is more than the original designated amount, then there is no hedge ineffectiveness. Under IFRS as issued by the IASB, hedge accounting for fair value macro hedges cannot be applied to core deposits. In addition, under IFRS as issued by the IASB, hedge ineffectiveness arises for all fair value macro hedge accounting relationships whenever the revised estimate of the amount of cash flows in scheduled time buckets is either more or less than the original designated amount of that bucket. The EU carve out version of IAS 39 also removes the prohibition on identifying a benchmark risk component in a financial instrument priced at sub–benchmark. This may arise when financial instruments carry a negative spread such that the identified non–contractually specified risk component is larger than the interest carry on the contract itself.
