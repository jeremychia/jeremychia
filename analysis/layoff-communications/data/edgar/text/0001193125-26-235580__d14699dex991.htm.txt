Algoma continued to advance its strategic priorities during the twelve-month period ended December 31, 2025 (“ Fiscal 2025 ”). The transition represents a fundamental shift in Algoma’s operating model and positions the Company as a sustainable steel producer. For Fiscal 2025, Algoma continued to advance its efforts toward a lower-carbon future, aligning with Canada’s Paris Agreement commitments and helping our customers meet their own sustainability and climate related goals through the decarbonization of our operations. Fiscal 2025 presented extraordinary challenges for the Canadian steel industry. US trade measures – unprecedented in scope – designed to eliminate Canadian steel imports into the United States, fundamentally decoupled previously integrated North American supply chains. Compounding this, as the United States moved to curtail global steel imports, displaced tons 2026 MANAGEMENT INFORMATION CIRCULAR ALGOMA STEEL GROUP INC. i sought alternative markets, with a significant volume finding their way into Canada and further depressing domestic Canadian pricing. The resulting pricing volatility and broad market disruption made clear that the traditional integrated steelmaking model was not viable for Algoma. In this environment, a measured response was not sufficient. Algoma’s board and management took decisive action – accelerating the Company’s strategic transformation and repositioning Algoma for long-term competitiveness. Against this backdrop, the Company remained focused on the disciplined execution of the EAF transformation while maintaining safe and reliable operations throughout the year. That decisiveness culminated in a defining milestone subsequent to year-end, when in January 2026 Algoma commenced the decommissioning of its legacy integrated ironmaking assets and transitioned to producing steel exclusively through EAF. This transition is expected to significantly reduce the Company’s greenhouse gas (“ GHG ”) emissions and establish Algoma as a producer of lower-carbon steel well-positioned to serve North American markets going forward. Algoma continues to advance its sustainability strategy, aligning with Canada’s climate commitments and supporting customers in achieving their own emissions reduction objectives. Our Performance for Fiscal 2025 Fiscal 2025 presented an extraordinarily challenging operating environment for the North American steel industry, shaped by a confluence of trade disruption, import pressure, and weak demand. US trade measures – unprecedented in scope – were designed to eliminate Canadian steel imports into the United States, including through the imposition of 50% tariffs on imported steel products and derivative steel products. These measures fundamentally decoupled previously integrated North American supply chains. Compounding this, as the United States moved to curtail global steel imports, displaced tons sought alternative markets, with a significant volume finding their way into Canada and further depressing domestic Canadian pricing. Canada responded with measures of its own to support the domestic steel sector, including tariff rate quotas on certain imported steel products and a 25% surtax on certain steel imports from China. Anti-dumping and countervailing duty measures also remained in place across a range of products and jurisdictions. Notwithstanding these measures, import pressure within the Canadian market remained elevated, contributing to broader supply imbalances across North America. Demand conditions offered little relief. In the United States, elevated interest rates maintained to combat inflation constrained manufacturing activity and suppressed metal consumption. Across North America, the combination of persistent weak demand and rising import volumes created oversupply conditions that weighed heavily on steel prices and compressed margins for producers throughout the year. Taken together, these conditions made clear that the traditional integrated steelmaking model faced structural, not cyclical, headwinds – and that decisive action was required. On September 28, 2025, having concluded that the continuation of integrated ironmaking operations was no longer viable in the trade-impacted environment, the Board approved a plan to wind down and decommission the Company’s blast furnace and coke oven operations and accelerate the full transition to EAF steelmaking. Blast furnace production ceased shortly after December 31, 2025, and decommissioning of the Company’s integrated ironmaking and coke-making assets commenced in January 2026. Algoma now operates exclusively as an EAF steel producer. Variable Compensation Incentive Plan Payout for Fiscal 2025 The Variable Compensation Incentive Plan (“ VCIP ”) is our annual performance-based cash bonus program designed to reward employees for their contributions to the Company’s success. For Fiscal 2025, the VCIP calculation for executives resulted in an overall payout score of 20% of target for the period January 1, 2025 to March 31, 2025 and 34.4% of target for the period April 1, 2025 to December 31, 2025. Clawback Policy The Company has adopted the Clawback Policy in accordance with Nasdaq Rules, Rule 10D-1 under the Exchange Act and Section 304 of the U.S. Sarbanes-Oxley Act, which applies to our current and former executive officers. Under the Clawback Policy, we are required to recoup the amount of any erroneously awarded compensation on a pre-tax basis within a specified lookback period in the event of any accounting Restatement (as defined in the Clawback Policy) or where an executive officer engages in fraud, criminal misconduct, gross negligence or other intentional and serious misconduct that results in an accounting Restatement, subject to limited impracticability exceptions. Restatements include both a restatement to correct an error that is material to previously issued financial statements or that would result in a material misstatement if the error were corrected in the current period or left uncorrected in the current period. The amount required to be recovered is the excess of the amount of incentive-based compensation received over the amount that otherwise would have been received had it been determined based on the restated financial measure and excludes base annual salary received by the executive. The Clawback Policy is overseen and administered by the Board.
