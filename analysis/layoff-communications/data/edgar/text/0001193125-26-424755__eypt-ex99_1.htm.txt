(Nasdaq: EYPT), a company committed to developing and commercializing innovative therapeutics to improve the lives of patients with serious retinal diseases, today announced a strategic prioritization designed to reduce operating expenses and focus resources on its late-stage program for DURAVYU™ in diabetic macular edema (DME). This strategic realignment follows today’s topline readout from the Phase 3 LUCIA study of DURAVYU in wet AMD, which did not meet its primary endpoint of non-inferiority, despite vision improvements, and despite meeting key secondary endpoints including reduction in treatment burden, supplement-free rates, anatomic control, and safety with redosing. To preserve capital and maximize shareholder value, the Board of Directors has approved a plan to reduce the Company's workforce by approximately 53% of employees and to conclude the pivotal Phase 3 wet AMD trials early, without full completion of the second year of each trial. EyePoint plans to prioritize the development of DURAVYU for the potential treatment of DME, which is expected to read out pivotal Phase 3 topline data in the fourth quarter of 2027. EyePoint thanks its employees affected by these measures for their dedication and contributions to the Company’s mission. Although today’s clinical setback highlights the challenges of drug development, it does not diminish the team’s achievements or the Company’s commitment to improving the lives of patients with serious retinal diseases. About the Phase 3 DME Program The Phase 3 DME program consists of COMO and CAPRI, two global, randomized, double-masked, aflibercept-controlled, non-inferiority trials evaluating the safety and efficacy of DURAVYU 2.7 mg in patients with active DME. The trials enrolled 551 patients in total, including approximately 75% treatment-naïve and 25% previously treated patients. Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive either DURAVYU 2.7 mg every six months or on-label aflibercept every 8 weeks as control. The primary endpoint is non-inferiority in the average change from baseline in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) at the blended Weeks 52 and 56. Secondary endpoints include safety, superiority in reduction in treatment burden, percentage of eyes free of supplemental aflibercept injections, and anatomical outcomes as measured by optical coherence tomography (OCT). More information is available at www.clinicaltrials.gov (COMO: NCT07449936; CAPRI: NCT07449923). About Diabetic Macular Edema Diabetic macular edema (DME) is the leading cause of vision loss in people with type 1 and type 2 diabetes. DME results when damaged retinal blood vessels leak fluid into the macula, the central portion of the retina responsible for sharp, detailed vision. The resulting retinal swelling can cause blurred vision and may lead to severe vision loss or even legal blindness. The pathophysiology of DME is multifactorial, involving vascular permeability and inflammation mediated by pathways including VEGF, PDGF and interleukin-6 (IL-6). DME is estimated to affect approximately 28 million people worldwide, and its prevalence is expected to increase as the global diabetes population grows. Current treatments for patients experiencing DME include short-acting intravitreal anti-VEGF biologics, corticosteroids and laser photocoagulation. Since DME is a chronic disease in an active population, the need for frequent treatment can create a substantial burden for patients, caregivers, and physicians and can contribute to undertreatment in clinical practice. About DURAVYU TM DURAVYU ™ (vorolanib intravitreal insert), is an investigational sustained-delivery treatment for patients suffering from serious retinal diseases. DURAVYU combines vorolanib, a selective and patent-protected tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), with EyePoint’s proprietary next-generation bioerodible Durasert E ™ , a best-in-class IVT delivery technology designed to deliver sustained release of drug for at least six months without free-floating drug particles. DURAVYU brings a potential new multi-mechanism of action and treatment paradigm for retinal diseases beyond existing anti-VEGF large molecule ligand blocking therapies, as vorolanib acts intracellularly to suppress angiogenesis through the inhibition of all VEGF receptors and PDGFR, while also suppressing inflammation through the inhibition of interleukin 6 (IL-6)/JAK1 signaling. In addition, vorolanib has demonstrated neuroprotective activity in an in vivo model of retinal detachment. DURAVYU has been evaluated across multiple clinical trials in wet AMD and DME, including the Phase 2 DAVIO 2 and Phase 3 LUGANO and LUCIA trials in wet AMD, and Phase 2 VERONA trial in DME, and is in ongoing Phase 3 COMO and CAPRI trials in DME. No drug-related safety concerns were observed in over 630 patients across six completed clinical trials, including three Phase 2 trials and 2 Phase 3 trials. The Phase 2 VERONA trial in DME met the primary and secondary endpoints and demonstrated a rapid and sustained improvement in vision and anatomy and a continued favorable safety and tolerability profile with superior dosing intervals to standard of care. Pivotal data from the Phase 3 DME program is anticipated to be reported in the fourth quarter of 2027. About EyePoint EyePoint, Inc.
