This motion addresses the urgent need to approve a debt arrangement for an Israeli company that possesses groundbreaking technology, intellectual property, and unique expertise in the automotive sector, but which has encountered financial difficulties and is unable to continue its operations or repay its debts without a debt arrangement. The Arrangement submitted for creditors’ approval in this motion is intended to preserve the Company as a “going concern” and the value inherent in its operations, rather than realizing its assets in liquidation, which would result in the destruction of value and negligible repayment of the debt, if any. This motion is now being filed following protracted negotiations—conducted day and night—which culminated just before the filing of this motion in agreements with the investors Kukac LLC and Elljay Limited (hereinafter collectively referred to as “the Investors”) regarding a new investment by them totaling 4 million U.S. dollars (US$2 million each) in the Company for the purpose of injecting working capital and restructuring its debts and equity, subject to the terms of the Arrangement and the Equity Commitment Letter, which constitutes Appendix “1” to the Arrangement and is an integral part thereof (hereinafter: “the Commitment Letter”). As detailed below, the Arrangement may result in a potential recovery of up to approximately 94% of the Company’s debts (gross): a) Injection into the Arrangement Fund of the Company’s available cash and expected receipts generated during the operating period, up to a total amount of 3 million U.S. dollars. This collection, which serves as the source of the Arrangement, is possible only due to the Company’s continued operation as a going concern, thanks to the investors’ injection of working capital into the Company to enable its continued operations, as stated above and below; b) The Investors are converting debt totaling 20 million NIS into shares in the Company, thereby significantly reducing the amount of outstanding debt and, accordingly, increasing the effective distribution rate to creditors. In addition, Mr. Daniel Barel and Mr. Sardes are also converting debt owed to them by the Company—arising from loans they provided in the millions of NIS—into equity (rather than receiving a cash repayment), which further increases the distribution rate to creditors. 3 c) Furthermore, the Arrangement grants eligible creditors a Contingent Value Right (CVR) in an aggregate amount of up to an additional 3 million U.S. dollars, subject to the qualifying conditions (an exit event). The purpose of the Arrangement is to enable the repayment of a significant portion of the Company’s debts while maintaining its operations, continuing to employ its workers, and serving its customers. Maintaining the Company as a “going concern” will, on the one hand, enable the collection of funds that will allow for optimal distribution to creditors, and on the other hand, preserve the technology, know-how, and intellectual property it has accumulated, complete ongoing projects, and maximize collection sources (which otherwise could not be collected), and, in the process, allow creditors to benefit from the potential for the Company’s future rehabilitation by participating in possible future upside through the CVR. In light of the tight schedule, and considering that a delay in convening the meetings could thwart the investment and the debt arrangement, we request that an accelerated schedule be set for approval of the Arrangement and that the stay of proceedings order be extended, as necessary and subject to law, as requested in the preamble. Accelerating the proceedings is also intended to allow sufficient time for the conditions precedent to be met and for the necessary closing actions to be completed. Attached to this motion as Appendix “2” is the opinion of CPA Yaakov Pasi, which shows that approval of the Arrangement is significantly preferable to a scenario of liquidation. The Company is an Israeli technology company incorporated in 2011, with its offices and development center located in Galil Yam, Herzliya. Its primary value lies in software-defined vehicle (SDV) technology, electronic vehicle control systems, and the intellectual property and expertise of its development team. The company has operations and commercial ties in Israel and abroad, including a project for a defense client whose identity is confidential. 4 3. The Company’s primary asset is its owned intellectual property, which includes patents, patent applications, and trademarks in its fields of activity. However, the value of this asset is not limited to the registered rights alone. It encompasses the professional knowledge accumulated over years of development, the expertise of the staff, the regulatory approvals obtained, customer relationships, and the ability to continue developing the technology and integrating it into commercial projects. Preserving the business and the professional team is therefore an essential component in maintaining the Company’s value and its ability to derive value from its assets. 4. As detailed at length in the application for a stay of proceedings, the Company fell into a financial crisis due to a lack of funding sources. The deterioration of the Company’s financial situation stemmed from a combination of business, financial, and macroeconomic factors. Among other things, research, development, production, and commercialization activities entailed high costs, while the volume of commercial revenue and the pace at which it was realized were insufficient to finance operations. The Company therefore required ongoing capital investments and relied significantly on capital raising and business partners in a competitive market dominated by major international players. 5. During 2025, the difficulties intensified, in part due to U.S. tariff policies and their impact on the profitability of the Company’s vehicle production and its supply chain. The resulting rise in costs and uncertainty compelled the Company to review and realign its business model. 6. At the same time, as detailed in the application for a stay of proceedings, the Company’s ability to raise additional funding was impaired due to deteriorating capital-raising conditions for technology and growth companies—particularly those in the electric vehicle sector. Compounding these challenges were difficulties stemming from the security situation in Israel and its impact on advancing deals and negotiations with international parties. This combination of circumstances reduced the Company’s available sources of financing, precisely at a time when it needed resources to complete the transformation of its business model and to continue developing and commercializing its technology. 7. In parallel with the extensive streamlining measures the Company implemented, it worked for an extended period to identify an investor, acquirer, or strategic partner and to explore various financing options. As part of this process, the Company approached dozens of entities, including automakers, automotive industry suppliers, technology companies, and international investors. The Company’s management conducted direct negotiations with business and strategic entities, and various transaction structures were examined with the aim of enabling the Company to continue its operations and utilize the technology it had developed. However, these efforts did not, at that time, result in a transaction that would meet the Company’s needs. 5 8. Against this backdrop, and after exhausting all alternatives, the Company filed an application with the Honorable Court for a stay of proceedings. The goal was to enable the Company to continue operating within a protected and supervised framework, to preserve its assets and operations, and to formulate a Debt Arrangement that would address its obligations and ensure its continued existence. The Company believed, and continues to believe, that it is possible to formulate a Debt Arrangement that will save the Company from the difficult situation in which it finds itself—and the Debt Arrangement it has formulated is the optimal solution for the Company, its employees, and its creditors. 9. On July 2, 2026, an application for a stay of proceedings was filed together with an initial outline for an arrangement. A temporary order was issued that same day. On July 12, 2026, following a hearing, a temporary stay of proceedings was granted, which has been extended from time to time. The stay of proceedings is currently in effect until October 30, 2026. The Proposed Method for Continuing the Corporation’s Operations 25. The Arrangement combines a distribution from the Company’s resources, an investment of $4 million in working capital intended to enable the Company to continue its operations, and a debt-to-equity conversion. 26. The main advantage of the Arrangement is that it provides a practical alternative to liquidation, while preserving the sources of repayment and the possibility of allowing creditors to share in future appreciation. 27. The Company will continue its operations as a “going concern” subject to the approval and completion of the Arrangement, rather than through the separate realization of its assets. Pursuant to the Debt Arrangement, after the Effective Date, the Company will operate as a private company through its corporate organs, under the supervision of the Arrangement Administrator regarding the implementation of the Arrangement and within the scope of the powers expressly granted to him therein. 28. The Company’s continued operation as a going concern will enable the collection of receivables from its customers, which, had the Company not continued to operate as a going concern, would likely have been collectible only under uncertain circumstances. Contingent Value Right (CVR) : 32.1. In addition to the basic distribution, the Arrangement grants eligible creditors a Contingent Value Right (hereinafter: “CVR”) from the Company, contingent upon an exit transaction by September 30, 2029 (if such a transaction materializes, and there is no commitment to do so) - This refers to a transaction or a series of related transactions in which a person or group of persons acquires, directly or indirectly, more than 50% of the Company’s issued share capital; or in which the Company sells, transfers, or otherwise disposes of all or substantially all of its business, operations, or assets. The calculation shall be based on the total amount of consideration, both in cash and non-cash (including shares and/or securities), received or to be received in connection with the transaction, including deferred consideration, excluding amounts required to settle or repay the Company’s debts and transaction costs, fees, and expenses incurred in connection therewith. 32.2. If the consideration, as defined in the Arrangement, exceeds 50 million U.S. dollars, the CVR amount will be 2 million U.S. dollars in aggregate for all eligible parties; if it exceeds US$150 million, US$1 million will be added, up to a cumulative total of US$3 million for all eligible parties. If no exit transaction entitling the holders to payment occurs by September 30, 2029, the right will expire; however, pursuant to Section 5.7.4 of the Arrangement, any right to payment that has accrued by that date will remain unaffected, including with respect to deferred consideration received thereafter. This preserves the opportunity to participate in the success of the rehabilitation and the enhancement of the Company, without making the basic distribution contingent on an exit event; it should be noted that this is not a guaranteed payment nor are these funds currently available for distribution. 11 32.3. The Company will ensure that the amount due to eligible creditors is deducted from the proceeds of the exit transaction prior to any distribution to shareholders and transferred directly to a designated account managed by the Arrangement Administrator, for distribution to the creditors. The Company will notify the Arrangement Administrator of the execution of a binding agreement to carry out an exit transaction prior to its completion, provide details regarding the expected proceeds, and report on the completion and the final calculation, accompanied by the required supporting documentation. This mechanism allows for continued oversight of the exercise of the right even after the Arrangement is completed. 33. The debts of holders of convertible loans who have filed claims will be converted into equity on the date of completion of the Arrangement. Loans provided by Messrs. The new allocations and conversions will be made in accordance with the entitlements set forth in the Arrangement and the Commitment Letter, and not by virtue of the continued validity of old equity rights. The starting point, established by law and case law, is that in an insolvent company, shareholders are in a subordinate and deferred position relative to creditors . Once the Company’s residual value is exhausted, the value of its shares is reduced to zero . In this situation, shareholders are not entitled to any consideration by virtue of their status as shareholders, and their rights expire. The Court’s remarks in Insolvency Case (Tel Aviv-Jaffa District Court) 27222-09-21 Israel Petrochemical Industries Ltd. v. the Commissioner of Insolvency Proceedings and Economic Rehabilitation (Nevo, September 5, 2022) are relevant in this regard. The courts have recognized this principle even in cases where a company undergoing an arrangement canceled its shares and allocated new shares to a creditor representing 100% of its issued capital, just as in the present case. See, for example: Insolvency Proceedings (Tel Aviv-Jaffa District Court) 25064-10-19 Africa Israel Investments Ltd. v. the Commissioner of Insolvency Proceedings and Economic Rehabilitation (Nevo, November 27, 2019); and LCA 8417/11 Nami Trusts Ltd. v. Adv. Bergerzon (Nevo, May 7, 2013). The same applies in this case: Under the current circumstances, the existing shareholders are left with no residual value whatsoever, and therefore their existing equity rights will expire and be extinguished as part of the Arrangement. Consequently, there is also no need to convene a shareholders’ meeting, as the conditions set forth in Section 322(b) of the Insolvency and Economic Rehabilitation Law have not been met. 63. The Arrangement—General Provisions 3.1 Subject to satisfaction of the conditions precedent for the Arrangement to take effect, and as of the Closing Date, this Arrangement shall define and set forth exclusively and definitively the relationships, rights, and obligations among the Company, the Company’s creditors, Existing Shareholders, Existing Rights Holders, and the Investors, and shall exhaustively settle and regulate the status of all Past Debts. 3.2 As of the Closing Date, and subject to the Court’s approval of this Arrangement and the payment obligations set forth herein, all Past Debts shall be finally and completely settled and discharged, and the Company and anyone acting on its behalf shall have no remaining liability in respect thereof, except for its express obligations under this Arrangement. With respect to Past Debts, no person, including the Company’s creditors, shareholders, and all Rights Holders, shall be entitled to file a claim and/or demand against the Company and/or anyone acting on its behalf except in accordance with this Arrangement. As of the Closing Date, the Company’s creditors shall be entitled solely to the rights granted to them under this Arrangement. Nothing herein shall derogate from the right of eligible creditors to enforce the Company’s obligations under this Arrangement. 3.3 After the Closing Date, the Company will continue to operate as a going concern as a private company, through its corporate organs and in accordance with the capital structure and corporate governance set forth in this Arrangement. Terms of the Proposed Settlement for Creditors 5.1 The Arrangement Fund will consist of the Company’s funds, in a total amount of up to 3,000,000 U.S. dollars, from available cash on hand and proceeds expected to be received by the Company. This is based on the Company’s existing contracts and projects during the operational period, including proceeds for milestones not yet completed or deliverables not yet fully delivered as of the Closing Date, which are expected to be received after the Closing Date, all in accordance with the revenue sources included in the financial forecast upon which this arrangement is based. It is clarified that the foregoing amount constitutes a ceiling and not a commitment to provide a minimum amount, and that the transfer of proceeds to the Arrangement Fund is contingent upon their actual receipt by the Company. If all or part of the expected proceeds are not actually received, the Company shall not be obligated to make up the amounts not received from other sources, and their non-receipt shall not in itself constitute a breach of the Arrangement, provided that the Company acts with reasonable commercial diligence to fulfill its relevant obligations and collect the proceeds. 5.2 With respect to proceeds arising from agreements that involve activities both before and after the Closing Date, the Arrangement Fund shall include the proceeds specified in the financial forecast on which this Arrangement is based, including proceeds for milestones to be completed or deliverables to be provided after the Closing Date, subject to their actual receipt by the Company and in accordance with Section 5.1 above. The funds will be transferred by the Company to a designated account managed by the Arrangement Administrator and will be used to pay the Company’s eligible creditors and the costs of the proceedings, in accordance with this Arrangement and the Court’s decisions. The Company shall transfer to the Arrangement Fund the available cash intended for distribution on the Closing Date, and the balance of funds from proceeds received by it after the Closing Date within 14 business days after their receipt in the Company’s account, up to an aggregate cap of US$3,000,000, based on funds actually received. To secure this obligation, a lien in favor of the insolvency estate shall be registered on proceeds received from customers in respect of these payments, up to the cap. The Company shall provide the Arrangement Administrator with periodic reports on the proceeds received and the amounts transferred to the Arrangement Fund, including, if any, expected proceeds that have not yet been received. Appendix 1 - 9 5.8 Right to Contingent Consideration – Creditors’ Participation in an Exit Transaction The Company’s creditors who are eligible pursuant to the provisions of this Arrangement and the rulings on the debt claims shall be entitled to a conditional right to consideration (CVR) in an aggregate amount for all eligible creditors, subject to the terms set forth below: 5.8.1 The right shall be contingent upon the completion of an Exit Transaction by September 30, 2029. 5.8.2 If the consideration in the Exit Transaction exceeds US$50,000,000, the amount of the contingent consideration shall be US$2,000,000 in aggregate for all eligible creditors; 5.8.3 If the consideration in the Exit Transaction exceeds US$150,000,000, an additional US$1,000,000 will be added, so that the cumulative contingent consideration will total US$3,000,000 for all eligible creditors; 5.8.4 The right to contingent consideration is an economic right in addition to the creditors’ rights from the Arrangement Fund. If no Exit Transaction entitling the holders to payment occurs by September 30, 2029, the right to contingent consideration will expire. Nothing herein shall derogate from any right to payment that has accrued by that date, if any, including with respect to deferred consideration received thereafter. 5.8.5 Upon an Exit Transaction that entitles holders to payment under the right to contingent consideration, the Company shall ensure that the amount due to eligible creditors is deducted from the proceeds of the Exit Transaction before any distribution of those proceeds to shareholders and transferred directly to a designated account managed by the Arrangement Administrator for distribution to the eligible creditors in accordance with this Arrangement. 5.8.6 The Company shall notify the Arrangement Administrator in writing, shortly after entering into a binding agreement to carry out an Exit Transaction and, in any event, prior to its completion, of the expected completion date, the expected consideration for the Exit Transaction, and the expected amount of the contingent consideration to be paid. The Company shall also notify the Arrangement Administrator of the transaction’s completion and provide him with the final calculation of the contingent consideration amount, together with reasonable documents and supporting evidence required by him to verify the amount. 6. D. Purpose of the Opinion and Key Conclusions 1. The purpose of this opinion is to examine whether, given the company’s current financial position, the resources at its disposal, and the risks associated with continuing its operations, the expected financial return to creditors under the updated arrangement is preferable to the return they would receive under the liquidation alternative. 2. The question is not whether the arrangement guarantees full repayment, but rather which of the practical alternatives maximizes the return to creditors. 3. The original motion presented a proposal assuming full debt repayment to all creditors other than holders of convertible loans. Since then, two changes have occurred. First , some of the investors on whose investment the proposal was based decided not to inject funds; two investors committed to injecting $4 million into the company. Second , the total amount of debt claims filed (approximately 47.1 million NIS) exceeded the company’s estimate of its known debt obligations (approximately 38.7 million NIS), including the convertible loans. 4. The key conclusion is that even in its updated form, the proposed debt arrangement is clearly preferable to the liquidation alternative for each creditor group.
