Overview We are not a Chinese operating company but rather a holding company incorporated in Cayman Islands. As a holding company with no material operation of our own, we conduct a substantial majority of our operation through our wholly owned subsidiary, Beijing Zhongchao Zhongxing Technology Limited, a People’s Republic of China (“PRC”) company (“Zhongchao WFOE”) and a variable interest entity in China, Zhongchao Medical Technology (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. (“Zhongchao VIE”) and its subsidiaries (collectively, the “PRC operating entities”). Due to the existing VIE agreements between Zhongchao WFOE and Zhongchao VIE, we are able to consolidate the financial results of Zhongchao VIE under the U.S. GAAP, however, we do not hold equity interest in Zhongchao VIE. We are a provider of healthcare information, education, and training services to healthcare professionals and the public in China. We offer a wide range of online and onsite health information services, healthcare education programs, and healthcare training products, consisting primarily of clinical practice training, open classes of popular medical topics, interactive case studies, academic conferences and workshops, continuing education courses, and articles and short videos with educational healthcare content to healthcare professionals as well as the public. We have also been engaged by certain customers on a project basis to establish individual columns on our online platform to provide training and knowledge of certain drug treatment for healthcare professionals and patients. We also plug in supplemental features to manage the drug treatment, including reviewing patients’ applications, tracking their usage of drugs, and collecting related information, or the patient-aid projects. We discontinued our patented drug sales operations in June 2025. Since 2025, the Chinese healthcare information service industry has experienced intensified market competition, and downstream customers have placed increasingly stringent demands on service quality and efficiency, exerting sustained downward pressure on the gross margins of our business lines. In response to these market changes, we are implementing a strategic optimization initiative designed to gradually scale down our existing business portfolio and better align it with our long-term development strategy. Subsequent events On September 18, 2026, our shareholders approved the proposed 1-for-2 share consolidation of our ordinary shares of $0.744 par value each into $1.488 par value each (the “1-for-2 Share Consolidation”). The shareholders also approved that the authorized share capital of us be reduced and reorganized from $10,000,000,000 divided into 6,048,387,097 Class A ordinary shares of par value $1.488 each and 672,043,010 Class B ordinary shares of par value $1.488 each to $67,204 divided into 6,048,387,097 Class A ordinary shares of par value $0.00001 each and 672,043,010 Class B ordinary shares of par value $0.00001 each (the “Capital Reduction”). The 1-for-2 Share Consolidation and Capital Reduction were effective on September 28, 2026. Results of Operations The following table sets forth a summary of our condensed consolidated results of operations for the periods presented. This information should be read together with our unaudited condensed consolidated financial statements included elsewhere in this report. The results of operations in any period are not necessarily indicative of our future trends. 1 Revenues We generate revenues from pharmaceutical enterprise customers and not-for-profit organizations (“NFP”) from design and production of online medical courses, organizing offline medical training services, consulting and academic support services, patient management services for patient-aid projects, and sales of patented drugs. Revenues decreased by approximately $4.8 million, or 85.0%, from approximately $5.6 million for the six months ended June 30, 2025 to approximately $0.8 million for the six months ended June 30, 2026. The overall revenue decline is mainly from an approximately $2.1 million decrease in revenue from medical training and education services and an approximately $2.0 million decrease in revenues from patient-aid projects. This decline was primarily attributable to intensifying competition and increasingly stringent customer requirements for service. As a result, we are making strategic adjustments to gradually scale down our existing operations. Additionally, sales of drugs decreased by approximately $0.7 million for the six months ended June 30, 2026 as compared to the same period of the last year. Over the past year or two, intensified competition among similar pharmaceutical products, coupled with the impact of centralized procurement in China, has made imported drugs uncompetitive in pricing. We discontinued our patented drug sales operations in June 2025. Cost of revenues Cost of revenues was comprised of direct related costs incurred for preparation of online medical training courses and offline education seminars and patient management services in patient-aid projects and cost of patented drugs. Cost of revenues incurred for preparation of online medical training courses and offline education seminars and patient management services in patient-aid projects was comprised of direct related costs incurred for preparation of online medical training courses and offline education seminars and patient management services in patient-aid projects, including expenses of travelling and accommodation, seminar site-rental, video production and backdrop production, professional service fees charged by experts who provide online and offline seminars, and salary and welfare expenses incurred by the key members of the editorial, design and production team and patient-aid projects, as well as outsourced labor cost in patient-aid projects. The travelling and accommodation expenses, including but not limited to the transportation expenses and hotel accommodation expenses, represented the costs arising from lecturers’ attendance and participation of the offline seminars. Other travelling expenses were incurred by the Company’s medical department for videos production, live streaming of the offline seminars, and materials collection to create online courses. These travelling and accommodation expenses are well budgeted before any agreements entered into by the Company and the customers. Therefore, such expenses are well covered by the customers under those agreements. The Company is not reimbursed by the customers separately. Cost of revenues incurred for patented drugs was primarily comprised of purchase cost of drugs. Cost of revenues decreased by approximately $2.1 million, or 72.9%, from approximately $2.9 million for the six months ended June 30, 2025 to approximately $0.8 million for the six months ended June 30, 2026. This decrease was primarily due to a reduction of approximately $1.5 million in the costs of medical training and education services and patient-aid projects, as the business volume was reduced, leading to a corresponding decrease in related expenditures. In addition, the cost of selling patented drugs decreased by approximately $0.6 million, which was consistent with the decline in revenue from patented drugs sales. Gross Profit Gross profit decreased by approximately $2.7 million, or 97.9%, from approximately $2.7 million for the six months ended June 30, 2025 to approximately $60,000 for the six months ended June 30, 2026. For the six months ended June 30, 2026 and 2025, our overall gross margin was 6.6% and 48.4%, respectively. As mentioned above, the primary reason for the decline in gross margin stemmed from our gradual wind-down of our existing operations, leading to reduced revenue, while labor costs and other inputs could not be reduced at the same pace. General and administrative expenses General and administrative expenses increased by approximately $1.7 million, or 61.4%, to approximately $4.4 million for the six months ended June 30, 2026 from approximately $2.7 million for the same period in 2025. The increase in general and administrative expenses was primarily attributable to the following factors: (i) an increase of approximately $1.7 million in share-based compensation expense resulting from the issuance of ordinary shares to management and consultants as compensations; (ii) an increase of approximately $0.3 million in professional service expenses mostly related to business development; and (iii) an increase of approximately $0.2 million in salary and welfare expenses due to one-time severance costs. The increase was partially offset by a decrease of approximately $0.4 million in credit loss expense related to overdue receivables. 2 Selling and marketing expenses Selling and marketing expenses decreased by approximately $0.8 million, or 89.4%, to approximately $0.1 million for the six months ended June 30, 2026 from approximately $0.9 million for the same period in 2025. The decrease was primarily attributable to (i) a decrease of approximately $0.4 million in salary and welfare expenses, resulting from the workforce reduction in the sales team, and (ii) a decrease of approximately $0.3 million in advertising and promotional expenses, due to the reduced business promotion activities. Research and development expenses Research and development (“R&D”) expenses decreased by approximately $8,000, or 100.0%, to nil for the six months ended June 30, 2026 from approximately $8,000 for the same period in 2025. The decrease was primarily attributable to our termination of our R&D projects, resulting in no expenditures incurred in the current period. Other income (expenses) Our total other (expenses) income, net, decreased by approximately $1.2 million, or 519.9%, to other expenses, net of approximately $1.0 million for the six months ended June 30, 2026 from other income, net, of approximately $0.2 million for the six months ended June 30, 2025. Other income (expenses) mainly includes interest income, other (expenses) income, and change in fair value of derivative liabilities and loss from issuance. The change was primarily attributable to a gain of approximately $3.5 million from changes in fair value of derivative liabilities and a loss of approximately $4.6 million from the issuance of such instruments. The common warrants issued in our private placement in June 2026 were classified as liability instruments. They were initially measured at fair value and were subsequently remeasured at fair value at each reporting date, with fair value computed using the Monte Carlo simulation model. As of June 30, 2026, the common warrants were fully exercised. Income tax expenses We had income tax expense of approximately $41,000 for the six months ended June 30, 2026, as compared to income tax expense of approximately $0.2 million for the six months ended June 30, 2025. The change was mainly due to a decrease in deferred income tax expense, resulting from the combined effects of a decrease in unbilled revenue and a decrease in the applicable tax rate for our subsidiary. Net loss As a result of the foregoing, we had net loss of approximately $5.4 million for the six months ended June 30, 2026 as compared with net loss of approximately $0.9 million for the six months ended June 30, 2025. Current foreign exchange and other regulations in the PRC may restrict our PRC subsidiary, Zhongchao VIE and its subsidiaries in their ability to transfer their net assets to the Company and its subsidiaries in Cayman Islands, and Hong Kong. However, these restrictions have no impact on the ability of the PRC entities to transfer funds to us as we have no present plans to declare dividend which we plan to retain our retained earnings to continue to grow our business. In addition, these restrictions have no impact on the ability for us to meet our cash obligations as all of our current cash obligations are due within the PRC. Our current revenue is denominated in Renminbi. Under existing PRC foreign exchange regulations, payments of current account items, including profit distributions, interest payments and trade and service-related foreign exchange transactions, can be made in foreign currencies without prior SAFE approval as long as certain routine procedural requirements are fulfilled. Therefore, the PRC entities are allowed to pay dividends in foreign currencies to us without prior SAFE approval by following certain routine procedural requirements. However, approval from or registration with competent government authorities is required where the Renminbi is to be converted into foreign currency and remitted out of China to pay capital expenses such as the repayment of loans denominated in foreign currencies. The PRC government may at its discretion restrict access to foreign currencies for current account transactions in the future. Based on this assessment, management expects that we will maintain sufficient liquidity to meet our obligations as they fall due for at least twelve months from the issuance date of these unaudited condensed consolidated financial statements. This expectation is based on our minimal operating expense structure, the absence of material debt obligations, and the significant liquidity enhancement from the private placement completed in July and August 2026.
