In a more challenging economic environment, we made additional fine-tuning adjustments to our credit models, pricing and reduced our exposure to more volatile client clusters. At the same time, our diversified revenue base, including treasury, investment banking, brokerage and custody services, consortia, and insurance operations, combined with rigorous expenses management while maintaining investments in our transformation journey, supported our performance. As a result, we have improved further our cost to income ratio. Looking ahead, we remain cautious and maintain a moderate risk appetite, with a firm commitment to continuing implementing our transformation plan at an accelerated pace. The macroeconomic environment continues to deteriorate, as noted in recent reports released by the Central Bank of Brazil. Interest rates remain high, and the economy is slowing down. The debt service-to-income ratio of individuals is high, and financial conditions are tight for both companies and individuals. Against this challenging macroeconomic backdrop, a slowdown in credit-related revenues is to be expected, with our commercial teams more focused on a cautious credit mix and on expanding non-credit revenue streams. This was the case in 3Q26. Two positive standouts in quarterly growth were fee income and market NII, with strong performance from the structured products trading desks serving clients and ALM management. Regarding the loan portfolio mix, the share of real estate, auto financing, sureties and guarantees, and securities expanded. As a result, there was a further increase in the share of secured loans in our portfolio. Delinquency indicators hinted at a slight increase in 3Q26. Consistent with the trend seen in recent quarters, delinquencies of more than 90 days increased in the MSME segment, particularly in working capital loans backed by Federal Government guarantees. Delinquencies in this product account for almost half of the increase in total delinquencies. In addition, there were seasonal delays in John Deere Bank's operations, which placed pressure on the over90 NPL for individuals. Overall, the macro environment continues to put pressure on delinquency ratios, which encourages us to make further fine-tuning adjustments to models, prices and reduce our exposure to more volatile client clusters. The cost of risk increased slightly in the quarter, as a result of the reassessment of the risk of older vintages. Coverage of Stage 3 movements stood slightly below 100%. Excluding working capital lines backed by the FGI and FGO funds, coverage would have been above 100% in 3Q26 and in every quarter of the year. The net interest income rose during the quarter. Of particular note was market NII ¡V once again reflecting strong risk management and treasury's capacity to drive recurring value ¡V primarily from the client marketrisk desk. The client NII also grew, reflecting the expansion of the loan portfolio. Fee and commission income accelerated in 3Q26, with a particular emphasis on card fees, asset management, consortia and capital markets & financial advisory services. Income from insurance sustained ROAE at a healthy 22%. Operating expenses remained controlled, and the claims ratio evolved in line with seasonal patterns for the period. Premiums grew on a quarterly and annual basis. Despite the significant investments we are making in our transformation plan, we have been able to keep tight control over spending. Key cost lines, such as personnel and administrative expenses, increased at a rate below inflation for the period. Our efficiency ratio (cost-to-income ratio) continues to trend downward. We stand firm in our commitment to further enhancing the Organization¡¦s efficiency. We allocated R$4.1 billion in interest on capital to shareholders in 3Q26. We successfully completed our R$10 billion capital increase and have already obtained regulatory approval from the Central Bank of Brazil for the transaction. As a result, our capital ratios have risen and are well above regulatory and management levels. Our transformation plan is moving forward at an accelerated pace. We have made significant progress in the new client segments and all client channels. Regarding MSMEs, we continue to raise deposits and change the way we serve the segment, including greater use of the new App. Our high-net-worth segment, Principal, expanded to 93 offices and crossed the 500 thousand client mark as of September. We have more than 45 million fully digital clients, gained further efficiency in digital sales, and continued to adjust our footprint. On the technology front, we continued to scale our agile operating model by expanding the number of tribes, launched new digital journeys, and increased efficiency through broader adoption of AI. We have increased the amount of tech talents and the number of agentic squads, expanding our capacity for transformation. We remain committed to financing sustainable businesses and supporting our clients in the transition to a greener and more inclusive economy. As of September 2026, we had reached 95.6% of our target of directing R$450 billion to this purpose by the end of 2026. We completed the allocation of R$3.6 billion to sustainable projects under the first Eco Invest Brasil auction. This amount comprises R$500 million in catalytic capital and R$3.1 billion in private resources mobilized for initiatives in energy transition, circular economy, bioeconomy, sanitation and infrastructure. These operations involve a range of derivatives, such as interest rate and currency swaps, futures, options, forward contracts, credit derivatives and exchange contracts with immediate and future settlement, accounted for and disclosed as derivatives, in accordance with CMN Resolution No. 4,966/2021. Bradesco¡¦s risk management policy is based on the utilization of derivative financial instruments mainly to mitigate the risks from operations carried out by the Bank and its subsidiaries. Derivative financial instruments are measured at fair value and classified in the fair value through profit or loss (FVTPL) category as shown in the consolidated financial statements. Fair value is generally determined based on market quotations or prices applicable to assets or liabilities with similar characteristics. When such quotations are not available, fair value is estimated based on information from market operators, pricing models, discounted cash flows, or other similar techniques. In these cases, determining fair value may require significant judgment or estimation by Management. The fair value of swaps is determined using discounted cash flow modeling techniques, utilizing yield curves that reflect appropriate risk factors. These curves are applied to the pricing of currency swaps, interest rate swaps, and swaps with other risk factors. The information used to construct yield curves is obtained primarily from B3 and the domestic and international secondary markets. The fair value of futures and forward contracts is determined based on market price quotations for exchange-traded derivatives or using methodologies similar to those used in pricing swaps. The fair value of options is determined based on mathematical models, such as Black & Scholes, using yield curves, implied volatilities and the fair value of the underlying assets. The fair value of credit derivatives is determined based on market quotations or obtained from specialized entities. Current market prices are used to calculate volatility. According to CMN Resolution No. 4,966/2021, foreign exchange portfolio transactions must be treated as derivatives. In this sense, the amounts recorded as rights and BRADESCO | Consolidated Financial Statements 76 Consolidated Financial Statements | Notes to the Consolidated Financial Statements obligations of the foreign exchange portfolio (notional value) are recorded in clearing accounts, and the variation of the fair value in income statements. To estimate the fair value of over-the-counter derivatives, the credit quality of each counterparty is taken into account, thus associating an expected loss for each derivatives portfolio (Credit valuation adjustment). The derivative financial instruments held by Bradesco in Brazil primarily consist of swaps, options and futures, and are registered with B3. Derivatives carried out abroad refer to swaps, forwards, options, credit derivatives and futures transactions carried out, substantially, on the Chicago and New York Stock Exchanges, as well as on the overthe- counter market. Macro strategies are defined for the Trading (proprietary) and Banking portfolios. Trading Portfolio transactions, including derivatives, seek gains from directional movements in prices and/or rates, arbitrage, hedge and market-maker strategies that may be fully or partially settled before the originally stipulated maturity date. The Banking Portfolio focuses on commercial transactions and their hedges. Portfolio risk is controlled using information consolidated by risk factor; effective portfolio risk management requires joint use of derivatives with other instruments, including stocks and bonds. I) Credit Default Swap (CDS) In general, these represent bilateral agreements in which one of the parties purchases protection against the credit risk of a certain financial instrument (the risk is transferred). In the case of a default, the purchasing counterparty will receive a payment to offset the loss incurred on the financial instrument. In this case, the selling counterparty usually receives the underlying asset of the agreement in exchange for the payment. The hedge effectiveness may be impacted primarily when, during the hedging relationship period, changes occur in the market risk environment or in the counterparty¡¦s credit risk. Hedge of investments abroad - The financial instruments classified in this category, have the objective of reducing the exposure to foreign exchange variation of investments abroad, whose functional currency is different from the Brazilian real, which impacts the net income of the Organization. For foreign investment hedge strategies, prospective effectiveness is assessed by demonstrating the existence of an economic relationship between the hedged item and the hedging instrument, including tests based on stress scenarios. Retrospective effectiveness is assessed by comparing the foreign exchange variation of the investment with the foreign exchange variation of the derivative, taking into account the related tax effects. The hedge ratio is determined by dividing the foreign exchange variation of the hedged item by the foreign exchange variation of the hedging instrument. Economic offset arises because both are exposed to the same risk factor (foreign exchange risk), with the effective portion recognized in equity (Other Comprehensive Income - OCI) and the ineffective portion recognized immediately in profit or loss. Both strategies are designed to achieve an effective level of economic offset, reflecting the risk management strategy adopted by Management.
