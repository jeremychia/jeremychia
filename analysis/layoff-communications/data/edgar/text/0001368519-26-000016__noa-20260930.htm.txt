In the third quarter of 2026, we delivered strong top-line growth, recording combined revenue of $438.1 million, a 12% increase compared to 2025 Q3. This $47.3 million increase was driven primarily by the addition of IMC, contributing $74.2 million of combined revenue. Our Canadian operations were up, increasing $15.5 million, driven by increased operational support scopes at the Syncrude and Millennium mines. When excluding the 797 haul truck fleet from 2025 Q3, which were disposed in 2025 Q4, this increase of over 20% on a like-for-like basis reflects the strong demand and improved equipment utilization in the oil sands region. Offsetting these increases was a decrease in our share of revenue from investments in affiliates and joint ventures of $48.7 million, mostly attributable to a joint venture relating to the Fargo-Moorhead Flood Diversion Project (the "Fargo Project") which is discussed in detail below. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 22.8%, compared to 25.3% in the prior period, which includes IMC results, a less capital-intensive business than our Queensland and Canadian heavy construction and mining operations. IMC contributed $8.2 million of adjusted EBITDA in the quarter which correlates to adjusted EBITDA margin of 11.2%. Excluding IMC, adjusted EBITDA margin for the quarter was 25.2%, consistent with 2025 Q3. Reported gross profit margin was 11.4%, compared to 15.6% in the prior year period. Combined gross profit margin decreased to 11.8% from 14.5% in 2025 Q3 but increased from 10.9% in 2026 Q2. Margin performance benefitted from improving equipment utilization in Canada but was impacted by additional costs incurred in Australia related to customer fleet availability issues at our largest site in Queensland and operational challenges from a pit wall failure in Western Australia. For clarity, this margin excludes the Fargo Project write-down, which is discussed below. Free cash flow was an outflow of $28.3 million in 2026 Q3, compared to an inflow of $45.7 million in 2025 Q3, as the consistent cash generated from EBITDA was more than offset by sustaining capital spending of $76.5 million, cash interest of $20.5 million and a temporary working capital impact of $23.6 million in our core businesses. Australia sustaining capital spending of $40.8 million was consistent with 2026 Q2 and primarily incurred on major maintenance rebuilds and replacement fleet purchases for MacKellar. Sustaining capital in Canada of $35.7 million, Management's Discussion and Analysis September 30, 2026 M-2 North American Construction Group Ltd. up notably from 2026 Q2, was incurred in response to strong demand in the oil sands region and in direct support of achieving our equipment utilization targets. Heavy Equipment - Australia commentary The Heavy Equipment – Australia segment recorded revenue of $267.8 million in 2026 Q3, up $79.3 million, or approximately 42%, from $188.5 million in 2025 Q3. The majority of this increase reflects the addition of IMC, acquired in 2026 Q2, which contributed $73.4 million of reported revenue in the quarter. Gross profit decreased to $32.0 million, from $36.8 million in 2025 Q3, a decrease of $4.7 million, or approximately 13%. Gross profit margin declined to 12.0% from 19.5%, primarily due to additional costs incurred to maintain customer fleet availability at a thermal coal mine in Queensland and operational inefficiencies at a lithium mine in Western Australia caused by a pit wall failure which occurred during the quarter. Heavy Equipment - Canada commentary The Heavy Equipment – Canada segment reported revenue of $141.2 million in 2026 Q3, compared to $125.7 million in 2025 Q3, an increase of $15.5 million, or approximately 12%. The primary driver of the increase was higher demand for operational support at Syncrude and the Millennium mine. Gross profit increased to $13.5 million from $11.4 million in 2025 Q3, an increase of $2.1 million, or approximately 18%. Gross profit margin increased to 9.5% from 9.0%, primarily reflecting an increase in utilization of the fleet generating more output from a materially leaner fleet. Depreciation as a percentage of revenue was generally consistent at 19.1% compared to 20.7% in the prior period. Fargo-Moorhead Flood Diversion commentary In early October, ASN Constructors (“ASN”), in which we hold a 30% ownership interest and which is responsible for construction of the Fargo Project, provided us with financial information as at September 30, 2026, which were premised on a recently revised project forecast. The revised forecast increased estimated total project costs by approximately $150.0 million on a 100%-basis, or $45.0 million attributable to our 30% interest, and represented a net increase of 9.0% over the previous estimate, resulting in a contract loss position. The forecast was approved by ASN’s executive committee and deemed the final construction estimate, as the project is in excess of 98% physically complete. The cost increases were the primary contributors to the $49.9 million project loss incurred in the third quarter. This adjustment was offset by the reversal of previously accrued general and administrative charges from the joint venture partners of $29.6 million within ASN's results. This reversal has been reflected as a corresponding increase in our reported general and administrative expenses. ASN, together with the Red River Valley Alliance, is engaged in discussions with the customer, the Metro Flood Diversion Authority, regarding mutually beneficial remedies to address the cost increases. No amounts in respect of these potential remedies have been reflected in our financial statements. We are committed to pursuing all legal and commercial avenues to recover the cost increases that have been incurred in completing this project. For the third quarter of 2026, we have excluded the non-recurring, non-cash project loss from adjusted earnings given it primarily is the result of activities outside of our direction and control. The project is considered legacy in nature as it is outside of our core operations and is in excess of 98% physically complete. In addition, we will only enter future joint venture arrangements which provide operational control. Consolidated commentary Combined revenue for the quarter was $438.1 million, representing an increase of $47.3 million (12%) compared to $390.8 million in 2025 Q3. The year-over-year growth was mostly attributable to the acquisition of IMC which contributed $74.2 million of combined revenue in the quarter. Excluding this contribution from IMC, combined revenue from Australia increased 3% and Canada combined revenue increased 12% as discussed above. Our share of revenue from equity-consolidated joint ventures decreased by a net $48.7 million, with the primary driver of this decline being the legacy Fargo Project. Combined gross profit for the quarter was $51.6 million, down from $56.8 million in 2025 Q3, with the combined gross profit margin decreasing to 11.8% from 14.5% in the prior year period. The year-over-year decrease was driven mainly by the impact from the Fargo Project. General and administrative expenses, excluding stock-based compensation, were $49.8 million in 2026 Q3, up from $13.0 million in 2025 Q3. The $36.8 million year-over-year increase was driven by the reversal of $29.6 million of general and administrative reimbursements from ASN due to the impacts of the loss incurred on the Fargo Project as well as acquisition-related costs. Normalizing for these costs, G&A expenses as a percentage of revenue was 4.6% for the quarter. Adjusted EBITDA for the third quarter of 2026 totaled $100.1 million, up from $99.0 million in the third quarter of 2025. Adjusted EBITDA margin declined from 25.3% in 2025 Q3 to 22.8% in 2026 Q3 reflecting the impact of IMC's 11.2% adjusted EBITDA margin. Excluding the contribution from IMC, our business maintained a margin consistent with the prior period. Depreciation expense for 2026 Q3 was $66.2 million, or 16.0% of revenue, compared to $49.9 million, or 15.7% of revenue, in 2025 Q3. The $16.4 million increase was attributable to the inclusion of IMC, which contributed $4.8 million of depreciation in the quarter, and higher depreciation of major equipment components at MacKellar. Adjusted net earnings for 2026 Q3 were $6.6 million, or $0.25 of adjusted EPS, a decrease compared to $19.6 million, or $0.66 of adjusted EPS, in 2025 Q3. The year-over-year decrease was primarily driven by consistent gross profit being offset with higher general and administrative costs from the increased scale of the business and increased interest expense from acquisition-related debt. Non-GAAP financial measures We believe that the below non-GAAP financial measures are all meaningful measures of business performance because they include or exclude items that are or are not directly related to the operating performance of our business. "Adjusted EBIT" is defined as adjusted net earnings before the effects of interest expense, income taxes and equity earnings in affiliates and joint ventures, but including the equity investment EBIT from our affiliates and joint ventures accounted for using the equity method, as well as the economic benefit generated by IMC for the period from January 1, 2026, through the acquisition completion date of April 7, 2026. As adjusted EBIT, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted net earnings and adjusted EPS are non-GAAP financial measures, our computations may vary from others in our industry. "Backlog" is a measure of the amount of secured work we have outstanding and, as such, is an indicator of a base level of future revenue potential. Significant accounting policies Basis of presentation These interim consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with United States generally accepted accounting principles ("US GAAP"). These interim consolidated financial statements include the accounts of the Company and its wholly-owned incorporated subsidiaries in Canada, the United States, and Australia. All significant intercompany transactions and balances are eliminated upon consolidation. The Company also holds ownership interests in other corporations, partnerships, and joint ventures. The Company's full year results are not likely to be a direct multiple of any particular quarter or combination of quarters due to seasonality with variability in weather at times having a direct impact on revenue. In the Queensland and New South Wales regions of Australia, production-related mine support and rental revenue can be impacted by the rainy cyclone season from November through March. During this period, heavy rains can temporarily suspend mining operations from both the direct impacts to the mine itself as well as flooding that can damage perimeter roads required for critical supplies and parts. In Western Australia, the winter months, typically June to August, can bring consistent rain which can hamper equipment utilization.
