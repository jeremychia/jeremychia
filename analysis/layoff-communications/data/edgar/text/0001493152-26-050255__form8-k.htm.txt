Description of Business Overview Following the Closing, the Company conducts its operations through PGS, in which the Company holds an indirect eighty percent (80%) membership interest through Acquisition Sub, with the remaining twenty percent (20%) membership interest held by Mr. Wanke. PGS, which also does business as A1 Glass Coating, is a Texas limited liability company formed in 2021 and headquartered in Universal City, Texas, in the San Antonio metropolitan area. PGS is the North American distributor of C-Bond products and markets, sells and installs nanotechnology-based glass strengthening and ballistic-resistant window film systems that are designed to protect building occupants and property against ballistic attack, forced entry, blast, severe windstorms and flying debris. PGS sells and installs these products directly, and also sells product to a nationwide network of more than 35 authorized dealers and distributors, serving educational institutions, general contractors, federal, state and local government agencies, airports, law enforcement agencies, commercial and industrial property owners and residential customers, principally in the United States. C-Bond products have been installed in more than 150 schools and at major facilities including Los Angeles International Airport, George Bush Intercontinental Airport, the Federal Bureau of Investigation, the Los Angeles Police Department and the Houston Fire Department. PGS’s revenues are derived primarily from commercial film installation and, to a lesser extent, retail automotive film installation. Products and Services PGS’s principal products are C-Bond Secure, a glass strengthening primer and window film mounting solution, and C-Bond BRS, a ballistic-resistant film system. PGS also offers solar, decorative, reflective and other specialty window films. PGS delivers these products principally through direct sales to end users, in which PGS both supplies the product and performs the installation, and to a lesser extent through sales to authorized dealers and distributors, which purchase product from PGS and perform installation for their own customers. PGS also derives revenue from repeat maintenance, warranty and upgrade work for existing customers. C-Bond Secure. C-Bond Secure is a patented, water-based, non-toxic nanotechnology solution that is applied to glass to increase its strength and flexibility, improve the adhesion of window film to glass, reduce film curing time, and modify the failure pattern of glass in the event of breakage. C-Bond Secure may be used as the mounting solution for any type of window or glass film in place of conventional soap and water, without additional labor cost. The patent-protected formula uses a Silane Coupling Agent, Acrylate Epoxy Adhesive and Self-Assembling Mechanism developed in part in conjunction with Rice University. C-Bond Secure has been tested by independent laboratories, including at Rice University in accordance with ASTM C-1499 (glass strength, validated to provide a 55% improvement) and ASTM C-3330 (film peel strength), at Stork Laboratories in accordance with CPSC 1201.4 (forced entry impact), at the Miami-Dade Hurricane Testing Center in accordance with applicable ASTM wind and storm standards, and at ABS Consulting in accordance with ASTM F1642-04 and GSA TS01-2003 (blast mitigation). C-Bond BRS. C-Bond BRS is a ballistic-resistant film system consisting of a proprietary multi-layer security film applied to specified glass configurations using C-Bond Secure as the mounting solution. C-Bond BRS has been validated by independent laboratories to provide ballistic protection at National Institute of Justice (“NIJ”) Levels I, II and IIA and under Underwriters Laboratories standard UL 752 by H.P. White Laboratories, and at NIJ Level III by Element U.S. Space & Defense, covering munitions ranging from .22 long rifle and .38 magnum to 9mm, .357 magnum and 5.56x45mm with appropriate glass and film configurations. Because C-Bond BRS is applied to existing glass, it can be installed or retrofitted into existing framing systems without structural changes to the building, and it is substantially lighter and less expensive than traditional ballistic glass or polycarbonate alternatives. PGS believes C-Bond BRS is the only film-to-glass system validated to these ballistic standards and the only such system offering one-way ballistic capability, which is designed to stop incoming projectiles while permitting return fire from the protected side. Customers and Suppliers Customers. PGS’s customers include public school districts, general contractors, colleges and universities, federal, state and local government agencies, airports, law enforcement and emergency services agencies, healthcare facilities, commercial and industrial property owners and residential customers. C-Bond products have been installed in more than 150 schools as well as at major airports including Los Angeles International Airport and George Bush Intercontinental Airport, law enforcement agencies including the Los Angeles Police Department and the Federal Bureau of Investigation, and the Houston Fire Department. For the year ended December 31, 2025, no customer accounted for more than ten percent (10%) of PGS’s sales. For the year ended December 31, 2024, one customer accounted for 23.0% of PGS’s sales. Because PGS’s revenues are project-based, the customers that account for a significant portion of sales vary from period to period. PGS’s accounts receivable are more concentrated than its sales: at December 31, 2025, three customers accounted for 60.5% of the total accounts receivable balance, representing 24.3%, 23.4% and 12.8%, respectively, and at December 31, 2024, two customers accounted for 37.8% of that balance, representing 21.4% and 16.4%, respectively. Suppliers. PGS does not manufacture the products it sells. PGS obtains C-Bond Secure from CB Nanoshield under the Distributor Agreement, which was assigned to PGS in connection with the Closing and under which CB Nanoshield manufactures and supplies product against purchase orders at a fixed price, neither party is obligated to purchase or sell any minimum quantity, and either party may terminate on sixty (60) days’ notice prior to the annual anniversary date. CB Nanoshield manufactures that product under a patent and know-how license granted by C-Bond Systems LLC, as described under “ Intellectual Property ” below. PGS purchases substantially all of its inventory from four suppliers, and the loss of those suppliers could have a material adverse effect on its results of operations and financial condition. PGS does not own the formulations for, or the right to manufacture, the C-Bond Secure and C-Bond BRS products. PGS believes that, if necessary, alternate vendors could supply similar products in quantities adequate to avoid material disruption to its operations, although an alternative product may not carry the ballistic, forced entry, blast and safety certifications described above. Sales and Marketing PGS sells its products primarily through direct sales to end users, including as a subcontractor to general contractors, and to a lesser extent through a network of authorized dealers and distributors located throughout the United States. PGS’s dealer and distributor agreements are entered into on a standard form under which the dealer or distributor submits purchase orders at prices quoted by PGS, PGS may accept or reject any order in its sole discretion, and PGS may establish and change credit terms and limits. Those agreements provide that PGS will not change prices without ten (10) days’ prior written notice, that risk of loss passes to the dealer or distributor upon loading at PGS’s facility, that PGS retains a purchase money security interest in the products sold until payment, that dealers and distributors may resell only to end users except as expressly permitted, and that PGS’s liability is limited to the purchase price paid and excludes incidental, special and consequential damages, with any action required to be commenced within one year after the cause of action accrues. The agreements are non-exclusive, do not obligate the dealer or distributor to purchase or PGS to sell, and may be terminated by either party at any time in its sole discretion. PGS markets its products by emphasizing the independent third-party validation of its product performance, the cost and weight advantages of a film retrofit relative to ballistic glass and polycarbonate alternatives, and its installed base of referenceable public-sector and institutional customers. The Company has identified additional acquisition candidates in adjacent verticals but has not entered into any definitive agreement with respect to any such acquisition. The Company’s ability to implement this strategy will depend on its ability to obtain additional financing on acceptable terms, and there can be no assurance that the Company will do so or that any element of this strategy will be successfully implemented. Competition The markets in which we operate are highly competitive and fragmented. PGS competes principally against (i) manufacturers and installers of traditional laminated ballistic glass and polycarbonate glazing systems, which generally require full glass or frame replacement, (ii) other suppliers and installers of safety, security and solar window films, many of which are affiliated with large diversified manufacturers with substantially greater financial, technical and marketing resources than the Company, and (iii) regional and local window film installation contractors. PGS competes on the basis of the independently validated ballistic and safety performance of its products, the ability to retrofit existing glass without structural modification, the lower installed cost and weight of its systems relative to ballistic glass and polycarbonate alternatives, optical clarity, its installed base of public-sector and institutional references, and the reach of its dealer network. Some competitors offer broader product lines, longer operating histories and greater brand recognition, and PGS’s competitive position depends in part on its continued access to the C-Bond Secure and C-Bond BRS products under the Distributor Agreement. Risks Related to Legal and Regulatory Compliance Our products are marketed to protect against ballistic and forced-entry threats, and any failure of a product to perform as expected could expose us to substantial product liability and warranty claims. The material terms of the Series J Preferred Stock, as designated and amended, are summarized below. Par Value and Authorized Shares. The Series J Preferred Stock has a par value of $0.001 per share and a stated value of $100 per share. Immediately prior to the Closing, 110,424 shares of Series J Preferred Stock were issued and outstanding, and following the issuance of the 47,500 shares of Series J Preferred Stock constituting the Merger Consideration and the 3,518 shares of Series J Preferred Stock issued in satisfaction of the Satisfied Obligations, 161,442 shares of Series J Preferred Stock are issued and outstanding. Ranking. With respect to preferences as to dividends, distributions and payments upon our liquidation, dissolution and winding up, the Series J Preferred Stock ranks senior to all shares of our capital stock that were outstanding on the date the shares of Series J Preferred Stock were issued, including our common stock and our Series H Preferred Stock. Dividend Rights. Beginning on June 1, 2025 and on each successive six-month anniversary, holders of Series J Preferred Stock are entitled to receive dividends, in either cash or stock at our option, equal to ten percent (10%) of the aggregate stated value of the shares held by each holder. Those dividends accrue and compound daily on the basis of a 360-day year. In addition, if we declare or make any dividend or other distribution of our assets, or rights to acquire our assets, to holders of common stock, by way of return of capital or otherwise, each holder of Series J Preferred Stock is entitled to participate in that distribution to the same extent as if the holder had held the number of shares of common stock acquirable upon complete conversion of its Series J Preferred Stock, without regard to any limitation on conversion, immediately before the applicable record date. Voting Rights. Holders of Series J Preferred Stock are entitled to vote on all matters on which holders of our common stock are entitled to vote, voting together with the common stock as a single class on an as-converted basis, which assumes that each holder has converted its shares of Series J Preferred Stock into shares of common stock. In addition, so long as any shares of Series J Preferred Stock are outstanding, we may not, without the affirmative vote of the holders of a majority of the then-outstanding shares of Series J Preferred Stock voting as a separate class, (i) alter or change adversely the powers, preferences or rights given to the Series J Preferred Stock or alter or amend the Series J Certificate of Designation, (ii) amend our articles of incorporation or any other charter document in any manner that adversely affects any rights of the Series J Preferred Stock, or (iii) enter into any agreement with respect to any of the foregoing. Conversion Rights and Mechanics. At any time from and after the date of issuance, a holder may convert all or any part of its outstanding shares of Series J Preferred Stock, at the holder’s option, into shares of common stock. The conversion price is $5.00 per share, subject to proportional adjustment upon any stock split, stock dividend, stock combination or similar transaction, and each share of Series J Preferred Stock is accordingly convertible into 20 shares of common stock. We have the right, at any time and with the written consent of the holders of a majority of the outstanding shares of Series J Preferred Stock, to lower the conversion price to any amount, subject to any applicable rules and regulations of the trading market on which our common stock is then quoted or listed. Beneficial Ownership Limitation. A holder of Series J Preferred Stock may not convert its shares if, after giving effect to the issuance of the conversion shares, the holder together with its affiliates would beneficially own more than 4.99% of the outstanding common stock. A holder may increase that limitation to 9.99% upon notice to the Company, with the increase becoming effective on the 61st day after the notice is delivered. In addition, a holder may not convert its shares of Series J Preferred Stock if the conversion would result in the issuance to that holder of a number of shares of common stock equal to more than ten percent (10%) of the trading volume of the common stock, provided that this limitation does not apply if the conversion price at the time of conversion is greater than $0.40 per share. Liquidation Preference. In the event of any liquidation, dissolution or winding up of the Company, each holder of Series J Preferred Stock is entitled to receive an amount in cash equal to one hundred twenty percent (120%) of the aggregate stated value of the shares of Series J Preferred Stock held by that holder, together with any accrued and unpaid dividends. That preference ranks senior to our common stock and to our Series H Preferred Stock and is payable before any distribution is made to the holders of those classes. Based on the 161,442 shares of Series J Preferred Stock outstanding following the Closing, the aggregate stated value of the Series J Preferred Stock is $16,144,200 and the aggregate liquidation preference, exclusive of accrued and unpaid dividends, is $19,373,040. Redemption Rights. The shares of Series J Preferred Stock are redeemable upon the occurrence of certain triggering events. Upon such a triggering event, holders of Series J Preferred Stock have the option to cause the Company to redeem all or part of their shares at a price per share equal to one hundred ten percent (110%) of the stated value of those shares. The Certificate of Amendment filed on September 5, 2025 amended the definition of a triggering event to exclude events, facts or circumstances that occurred prior to, or were in existence as of, the date of the certificate of amendment. The amendment also increased the indebtedness default threshold from $50,000 to $250,000. Because these redemption rights are outside our control and are subject to the occurrence of uncertain future events, the Series J Preferred Stock is presented at redemption value as temporary equity, outside of the stockholders’ deficit section of our consolidated balance sheets. Anti-Dilution Protections. The conversion price of the Series J Preferred Stock is subject to proportional adjustment upon the occurrence of any stock split, stock dividend, stock combination or similar transaction. The Series J Certificate of Designation provides for an adjustment to the conversion price upon an issuance or deemed issuance of common stock or common stock equivalents at a price below the then-applicable conversion price (a “dilutive issuance”). Upon a dilutive issuance, the conversion price is reduced based on a formula that multiplies the conversion price by a fraction, the numerator of which is the number of shares of common stock outstanding immediately before such dilutive offering and the denominator of which is the number of shares of common stock outstanding immediately after such dilutive offering. The Company may also lower the conversion price with the written consent of the holders of a majority of the outstanding shares of Series J Preferred Stock. Transfer Restrictions. The shares of Series J Preferred Stock issued as the Merger Consideration were issued without registration under the Securities Act and constitute restricted securities. Indemnification of Directors and Officers We are incorporated under the laws of the State of Nevada. Sections 78.7502 and 78.751 of the Nevada Revised Statutes permit a Nevada corporation to indemnify any person who is or was a director, officer, employee or agent of the corporation, or who serves or served in any such capacity at the corporation’s request for another entity, against expenses, including attorneys’ fees, judgments, fines and amounts paid in settlement actually and reasonably incurred in connection with any threatened, pending or completed action, suit or proceeding brought by reason of that position, if the person is not liable under Section 78.138 of the Nevada Revised Statutes or acted in good faith and in a manner the person reasonably believed to be in or not opposed to the best interests of the corporation and, with respect to any criminal proceeding, had no reasonable cause to believe the conduct was unlawful. Indemnification of expenses is required to the extent the person has been successful on the merits or otherwise, and expenses may be paid as incurred and in advance of final disposition upon an undertaking by a current director or officer to repay the amounts advanced if it is ultimately determined that the person is not entitled to indemnification. In an action by or in the right of the corporation, indemnification may not be made for any claim as to which the person has been adjudged liable to the corporation, except to the extent a court determines that the person is fairly and reasonably entitled to indemnity for expenses. These rights are not exclusive of any other rights to which an indemnified person may be entitled. Article V of our Amended and Restated Bylaws requires us to indemnify and hold harmless, to the fullest extent permitted by Nevada law, each person who is or was a director or officer of the Company, or who serves or served at our request as a director, officer, manager or trustee of another entity, against all costs, charges, expenses, liabilities and losses (including attorney’s fees, judgments, fines, ERISA excise taxes or penalties and amounts paid in settlement) incurred in connection with any such proceeding. That right continues after the person ceases to serve, inures to the benefit of the person’s heirs, executors and administrators, and is a contract right that includes the right to advancement of defense expenses upon delivery of the undertaking described above. If a claim is not paid in full within thirty days after a written claim is received, the claimant may bring suit to recover the unpaid amount and, if successful, the expense of prosecuting that claim. Article V also permits us to maintain insurance on behalf of our directors, officers, employees and agents against any liability, whether or not we would have the power to indemnify against that liability, and we maintain directors’ and officers’ liability insurance. We tendered the complaint in the stockholder derivative action described under “Legal Proceedings” above, in which two of our current directors are named as defendants, to our directors’ and officers’ liability carrier for defense and indemnity purposes, and that coverage is subject to a $250,000 self-insured retention.
