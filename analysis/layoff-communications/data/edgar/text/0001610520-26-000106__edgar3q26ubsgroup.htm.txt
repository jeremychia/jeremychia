UBS Group third quarter 2026 report | Recent developments 4 Recent developments Management report Integration of Credit Suisse We are progressing according to plan in the final phase of the integration and remain firmly on track to achieve substantial completion by year-end 2026. During the third quarter of 2026, our primary focus was on the continued decommissioning of legacy IT infrastructure. As of 30 September 2026, approximately 98% of legacy Credit Suisse IT applications in scope for decommissioning were no longer in use and around 90% of such applications had been fully decommissioned. In the third quarter of 2026, we realized additional gross cost savings of USD 0.6bn, bringing our cumulative gross cost savings at the end of September 2026 to USD 13.2bn compared with the 2022 combined cost base of UBS and Credit Suisse. Reflecting this progress, we now expect to deliver total gross cost savings of around USD 13.7bn by the end of 2026, above our stated ambition of around USD 13.5bn. Cumulative integration-related expenses incurred by the end of September 2026 amounted to USD 14.6bn, and we expect to have incurred around USD 15.1bn by the end of this year, assuming in both cases constant foreign- exchange rates compared with 30 September 2023. The wind-down of our Non-core and Legacy division is nearly complete, and as of 30 September 2026 the division had achieved a 91% reduction in underlying operating expenses (excluding litigation) compared with the 2022 baseline. In addition, a 68% reduction in risk-weighted assets (RWA) has been achieved since the second quarter of 2023 and a reduction of credit and market risk RWA to USD 3bn, ahead of our year-end 2026 ambition of around USD 4bn. Regulatory and legal developments Banking regulation in Switzerland In August 2026, the Swiss Federal Council launched two public consultations on further measures under the too- big-to-fail reform package, with one consultation covering proposed legislative measures and the other covering proposed amendments to the Swiss Liquidity Ordinance. The proposed legislative measures, to be submitted to the Swiss Parliament for enactment, include additional requirements for the recovery and resolution of systemically important banks (SIBs), add measures to enhance banks’ ability to obtain liquidity from the Swiss National Bank, introduce a Senior Managers Regime for banks with more complex organizational structures, and provide expanded supervisory powers for the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA). This consultation will conclude in November 2026, and the Swiss Federal Council is expected to submit its final legislative proposal to the Swiss Parliament in the first half of 2027. The entry into force of these changes is expected no earlier than the beginning of 2029. The proposed amendments to the Swiss Liquidity Ordinance, including quantitative minimum requirements for borrowing capacity for emergency liquidity assistance, are subject to public consultation until November 2026. Related amendments are expected to enter into force no earlier than the beginning of 2029, with an expected phase in over a period of four years. In September 2026, the Council of States (the upper house of the Swiss Parliament) supported a proposal requiring SIBs to deduct 90% of the carrying value of foreign participations from regulatory capital, with the deduction to be made entirely from common equity tier 1 (CET1) capital. This proposal is now subject to the deliberations of the National Council (the lower house of the Swiss Parliament). If enacted in the form supported by the Council of States, the proposal is expected to be phased in commencing in 2028, at the earliest, and would require UBS AG to hold approximately USD 16bn of additional CET1 capital. This would come in addition to around USD 2bn of additional CET1 capital required at UBS AG as a result of the ordinance-level measures announced earlier this year. UBS AG must also hold the previously disclosed CET1 capital of around USD 15bn under existing regulations following the acquisition of Credit Suisse. This amount includes around USD 9bn related to regulatory concessions granted to Credit Suisse and around USD 6bn to meet the current progressive requirements due to the increased size and market share of the combined business. Other developments Repurchase of legacy Credit Suisse debt In September 2026, UBS repurchased USD 9.8bn aggregate principal amount of legacy Credit Suisse senior debt instruments for an aggregate acquisition cost of around USD 9.9bn. UBS recognized a net loss of USD 346m in the third quarter of 2026 on the retirement of these instruments, including the release of purchase price allocation adjustments of USD 241m. The net loss has been recorded in Other income and is expected to be offset in future periods by lower ongoing interest expense. Settlements of legacy legal matters In the third quarter of 2026, UBS resolved and settled certain legacy legal matters recorded in Non-core and Legacy. The settlements resulted in a net gain of USD 697m, which has been recorded in Other income and included in underlying results. Changes to fund administration businesses In October 2026, UBS reached an agreement to extend its long-standing fund administration relationship with Northern Trust. As part of this agreement, UBS will transfer its former Credit Suisse traditional and alternatives fund administration businesses based in Luxembourg and Switzerland to Northern Trust, subject to regulatory approvals. In addition, UBS’s Swiss and Luxembourg management companies will extend their existing agreement to include investment funds that are currently serviced by UBS. The transaction is expected to close in the second quarter of 2027, and UBS does not expect to recognize a material profit or loss. Acquisition of an equity stake in 360 ONE WAM Ltd On 22 October 2026, UBS exercised all of the warrants that it had previously subscribed for pursuant to an agreement entered into in the second quarter of 2025 with 360 ONE WAM Ltd (360 ONE), one of India’s largest independent wealth and asset management firms. Following the exercise, UBS holds 20.5 million shares in 360 ONE, representing an equity stake of approximately 4.8% in the company. The exercise of the warrants and the acquisition of the equity stake did not result in a material profit or loss. Capital returns In the third quarter of 2026 we repurchased USD 1,807m of shares under our 2026 share repurchase programs. We expect to complete our current USD 3bn share repurchase program by the time of the publication of our fourth- quarter 2026 results. The amount and pace of share repurchases will remain subject to our short-term financial performance and outlook, maintaining a CET1 capital ratio of around 14% and further visibility on the deliberations by the Swiss Parliament on the capitalization of foreign subsidiaries. Underlying results In addition to reporting our results in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards, we report underlying results that exclude items of profit or loss that management believes are not representative of the underlying performance. In the third quarter of 2026, underlying revenues excluded purchase price allocation (PPA) effects and other integration items of negative USD 38m, including a net loss of USD 346m from the repurchase of legacy Credit Suisse debt instruments. PPA effects mainly consisted of PPA adjustments on financial instruments measured at amortized cost, including off-balance sheet positions, arising from the acquisition of the Credit Suisse Group. Accretion of PPA adjustments on financial instruments is accelerated when the related financial instrument is derecognized before its contractual maturity. No adjustment is made for accretion of PPA on financial instruments within Non-core and Legacy, due to the nature of its business model. Results: 3Q26 vs 3Q25 Reported operating profit before tax increased by USD 759m, or 27%, to USD 3,587m, mainly reflecting an increase in total revenues and a decrease in operating expenses. Total revenues increased by USD 590m, or 5%, to USD 13,350m, which included a decrease of USD 739m in PPA effects and other integration items. The increase in total revenues was primarily driven by an increase of USD 326m in other income, largely reflecting a net gain of USD 697m related to settlements of certain legacy legal matters in Non-core and Legacy, partly offset by a net loss of USD 346m from the repurchase of legacy Credit Suisse debt instruments, and also driven by an increase of USD 286m in net fee and commission income. Operating expenses decreased by USD 183m, or 2%, to USD 9,648m, which included an USD 871m decrease in integration-related expenses and PPA effects. Outlook As we enter the fourth quarter, investor sentiment remains measured amid elevated macroeconomic volatility, ongoing geopolitical tensions and political uncertainty, with consequential elections, including in the US. In Global Markets, the strong prior-year performance, which included a gain on disposal, may weigh on the year-on-year comparison. In Global Banking, we expect revenues to increase sequentially, based on the current pipeline and assuming constructive markets. Net interest income is expected to increase by a low-single- digit percentage sequentially in Global Wealth Management and increase slightly in Personal & Corporate Banking in Swiss franc terms. UBS remains strategically committed to its diversified business model and its global and regional footprint. As the political process on future regulatory requirements in Switzerland progresses, we will continue to engage constructively in the hope that Parliament will reach a solution that supports the country’s financial stability without imposing undue costs on UBS’s shareholders, clients and employees and without impacting its long-term competitive position.
