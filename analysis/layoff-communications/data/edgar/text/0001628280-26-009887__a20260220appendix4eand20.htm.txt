I will also take this as an opportunity to reflect on what I believe makes Telix such a dynamic and innovative company and the drivers behind its substantial growth and continued performance. Leading a new frontier of therapeutic innovation At our core, we exist to create products that seek to improve the quality of life for people living with cancer and rare diseases. Telix is now a global radiopharmaceutical company with a specialist commercial organization and fully integrated radiopharma company, assisted by our acquisition of RLS Radiopharmacies during the year. Our broad pipeline is enabling us to build depth in our focus areas of urologic oncology, neurologic oncology and other cancer types to maximize the full potential of targeted radionuclide therapy across various stages of disease burden. Commercial performance provides a strong foundation Telix has clearly demonstrated its ability to commercialize and gain meaningful market share in the competitive prostate cancer imaging market. Importantly this success has provided the foundation to advance and launch future precision medicine and therapeutic products globally and provides the Company with a growing revenue stream. During the 2025 financial year we secured U.S. Food and Drug Administration ("FDA") approval and reimbursement for Gozellix®, launched new clinical programs, and delivered clinical data across multiple therapeutic programs. There have been several significant challenges, specifically the Complete Response Letters received for TLX250-Px (Zircaix®1) and TLX101-Px (Pixclara®1). These events should be expected in the complex environment of drug development, and reflect the vanguard nature of the products we are developing. We remain focused on remediation with the expectation that we will ultimately obtain the marketing authorizations for both of these imaging candidates. Our intention is to continue to reinvest earnings to see our first therapeutic products through to commercialization. Ultimately, we believe that this will deliver a significant impact to patients and pave the way for value creation for shareholders. Investing in manufacturing infrastructure to support long-term growth To be clinically and commercially successful, particularly in a field with such complexity and time sensitivity as radiopharmaceuticals, we must have in place the infrastructure and capabilities to evolve manufacturing and deliver doses to patients globally. The strategic investments we have made over the past 24 months across manufacturing, research and development ("R&D") and pipeline expansion support our long-term growth strategy as a vertically integrated radiopharmaceutical company. Our manufacturing and supply chain has continued to mature and today we are executing at scale, delivering more than 2.9 million commercial and clinical doses on an annual basis across the Group. Our commitment to governance and sustainability Your Board has continued to focus on upholding good governance and effective risk management. We are pleased to present the Company’s first Sustainability report aligned to AASB S22 principles and compliant with Australian regulations. This follows the completion of our first Double Materiality Assessment, which identified the key sustainability factors that are material to Telix. While the external pressures of a dynamic operating environment and regulatory hurdles created challenges, they also underscore the resilience of our business. Telix has continued to deliver against its strategic priorities and reinforce our differentiated position as a leader in precision oncology. Our strategy remains clear: Deliver our late-stage pipeline, grow our precision medicine business, build the next generation of products, and expand our global infrastructure. We have deployed our financial resources and talent effectively to deliver on this strategy. Amid the inherent complexity of pioneering new molecular imaging and therapeutic pathways, we continue to build deep expertise across clinical development, manufacturing, and commercial delivery - laying the foundation for long-term growth. An established track record in commercial delivery Telix achieved record revenue of US$803.8M, a 56% increase year-over-year. This is significantly driven by the acquisition of RLS, continued success of Illuccix® and the FDA approval and CMS reimbursement of Gozellix®, our next generation PSMA-PET1 imaging agent. Together, these products position us uniquely in the growing prostate cancer imaging market, offering clinicians choice based on workflow and reimbursement needs. Illuccix is now also available across global markets, including Australia, Brazil, Canada, Europe, and the United Kingdom, with a new drug application filed and accepted for review in China. Building a global presence is part of our long-term strategy, differentiates us from many of our peers and is aligned with our patient-centric values. Innovation remains central to Telix, and our investment in lifecycle management and platforms such as AlFluor™ is creating new opportunities to grow the market and increase our share. In 2025, we launched BiPASS™2, a first-of-its-kind registrational trial combining MRI3 with Illuccix and Gozellix PET4 imaging for prostate cancer diagnosis. This initiative could add 800,000 scans annually to the addressable market, significantly advancing early disease detection. Following receipt of FDA Complete Response Letters for TLX250-Px (Zircaix®5) and TLX101-Px (Pixclara®5), our teams acted swiftly to engage with regulators and determine a pathway for resubmission. These experiences underscore the complexity of pioneering novel therapies; progress is rarely linear, but the lessons learned will enable resubmission and strengthen future regulatory filings. Advancing our pipeline We now have three active therapeutic pivotal trials in prostate, kidney, and brain cancer. The progress made this year sets the stage for the delivery of future clinical milestones and inflection points to realize the value in our theranostic pipeline and firmly position Telix as a therapeutics company. In prostate cancer, recruitment into Part 2 of the ProstACT Global6 Phase 3 study of TLX591-Tx is underway across Australia, New Zealand, and Canada. The study is also approved to commence in China, Japan7, Singapore, South Korea, Türkiye and the United Kingdom, with U.S. and European expansion pending regulatory clearance. Our prostate cancer portfolio exemplifies our approach to building clinical and commercial depth in our focus areas. Alongside TLX591-Tx, our lead therapy candidate, we are advancing TLX592-Tx (alpha therapy) and TLX090-Tx an investigational treatment for pain from bone metastases, which includes but is not limited to prostate cancer. These therapeutic candidates are complemented by our commercial precision diagnostics. Stronger environment Climate‑related financial risks and opportunities Identification and assessment process Telix identifies climate‑related risks and opportunities through a structured process aligned with AASB S2, focusing on matters that could reasonably be expected to affect the Group’s financial position, financial performance, cash flows, or access to capital over the short-, medium-, and long-term. Identification of climate‑related risks and opportunities The identification process draws on two complementary inputs: 1. TCFD baseline assessment and executive workshops In October 2024, Telix commenced a Task Force on Climate‑Related Financial Disclosures ("TCFD") baseline assessment, including a structured review of existing disclosures and peer benchmarks. This assessment was supplemented by two executive‑level workshops to identify potential climate‑related risks and opportunities across multiple time horizons. These sessions considered transition risks and opportunities - including evolving regulatory requirements, carbon pricing, market dynamics, and supply chain exposures - as well as physical climate-related risks arising from acute and chronic weather‑related events. Climate‑related opportunities associated with innovation, energy efficiency improvements, and strategic positioning in a lower‑carbon economy were also identified. 2. Financial materiality determination All identified climate‑related risks and opportunities were assessed solely on the basis of financial materiality, consistent with AASB S2. The financial materiality determination drew on the DMA methodology, which provided a structured, evidence‑based assessment of the likelihood and magnitude of potential financial effects across Telix’s value chain. The assessment considered: • the likelihood of occurrence, and • the magnitude of potential financial effects on revenue, operating costs, capital expenditure, asset values, cash flows, and access to capital. Risks and opportunities were classified as materially affecting enterprise value where their assessed likelihood and financial magnitude exceeded defined financial materiality thresholds. Assessments were conducted on a gross basis, without taking into account existing mitigation or adaptation measures, to provide transparency regarding inherent financial exposures. In assessing likelihood and financial magnitude, Telix applied a combination of quantitative and qualitative inputs. These included company‑specific information on operations, geographic footprint, value chains, asset base, and product development activities, together with external data reflecting climate‑related policy, market, and physical risk drivers relevant to the jurisdictions in which the Group operates. Judgments and assumptions were based on management’s best estimates as of the reporting date, informed by observable market trends, regulatory developments, and relevant climate‑related scenario variables. Climate‑related scenario analysis was applied to validate and deepen the assessment of financial materiality for the risks and opportunities identified through the TCFD workshops and financial materiality determination, but it was not used to identify new risks or opportunities. Outcomes and integration The process resulted in a prioritized list of climate‑related risks and opportunities reflecting those with the greatest potential financial relevance to Telix. These outcomes inform the Group’s enterprise risk management activities, strategic planning, and the climate‑related disclosures presented in this report. 22 Financially material climate-related risks identified Physical: Acute and chronic climate hazards Telix's own operations Long-term Telix is exposed to acute physical climate risks due to its operations, which rely on highly time‑sensitive, temperature‑controlled delivery of radiopharmaceuticals with short half‑lives. Unlike many industries that can stockpile inventory or tolerate short delays, Telix’s products must be produced, shipped, and delivered to hospitals within tightly defined windows. Its expanding footprint, including a large U.S. radiopharmacy network and key manufacturing assets in Belgium, Japan and Australia, creates multi‑regional exposure to storms, flooding, heatwaves, and power outages. Extreme weather events can disrupt production cycles, impede last‑mile delivery, and jeopardize product viability. Transition: Energy Telix's own operations Medium- term Telix’s operations depend on continued electricity supply to power cyclotrons, cold‑chain logistics, radiopharmacy production, and laboratory environments. As jurisdictions introduce carbon pricing or as wholesale markets shift during the low‑carbon transition, Telix faces the risk of rising energy costs, particularly in Belgium, Australia, Japan, and the United States where it has major facilities. As radiopharmaceutical manufacturing cannot easily reduce energy demand, Telix has less flexibility to adapt quickly to energy‑transition pressures than companies with more discretionary energy loads. Climate-related risks - Analysis Scenario framework Telix conducted the climate scenario analysis in 2025. To ensure consistency across all climate‑related risk assessments in this report, Telix applied a single, common set of climate‑related scenarios to evaluate both transition and physical risks. Two internationally recognized pathways were used for all analyses: a ~1.5°C lower‑warming scenario aligned with the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) AR6 SSP1‑1.19 pathway, which reflects rapid global decarbonization consistent with the temperature‑stabilization objective of the Paris Agreement; and a ~2.5+°C higher‑warming scenario aligned with the IPCC AR6 SSP2‑4.5 pathway, representing delayed global policy action, continued dependence on fossil fuels, and significantly heightened physical‑hazard intensification. These scenarios draw on widely used global reference frameworks, including IPCC assessments and comparable datasets used in regulatory and financial‑sector scenario analysis. Together, they bracket a plausible range of future climate conditions through 2050 and provide a consistent basis for assessing Telix’s resilience, anticipated financial effects, and adaptive capacity across all identified climate‑related risks. For the purposes of anticipated financial impacts, Telix applies a business‑as‑usual ("BAU") operating scenario, reflecting current regulatory settings, observed historical disruption patterns, and management’s forward view of expected operating conditions across the short-, medium- and long-term. This BAU scenario represents Telix’s best estimate of expected conditions absent accelerated global climate‑policy change and is therefore separate from, aligned with IPCC AR6 SSP5-8.5. Such events may lead to production delays, emergency logistics costs, and potential lost revenue if Telix is unable to supply hospitals and patients on time. The DMA provides a structured and evidence‑based foundation for Telix’s sustainability strategy, enterprise risk management, and voluntary disclosures, supporting long‑term value creation and resilience. This assessment considers both how Telix’s activities may impact people and the environment and how sustainability‑related matters may, over time, influence the Company’s business model, operating context, and enterprise value. The DMA does not determine materiality for mandatory AASB S2 disclosures. Methodology The DMA was conducted using a science‑based methodology developed in collaboration with sustainability adviser Upright. The methodology integrates peer‑reviewed scientific research and large‑scale benchmarking across more than 50,000 companies, supplemented by Telix‑specific operational, financial, and geographic data. The assessment evaluates sustainability topics across two distinct but complementary dimensions: • Impact materiality: the nature and significance of Telix’s positive and negative impacts on the environment and society. • Financial materiality: the potential for sustainability‑related risks and opportunities, including dependencies on natural, human, and social capital, to affect enterprise value through impacts on revenue, costs, capital expenditure, cash flows, asset values, or access to capital. To ensure a comprehensive assessment, sustainability topics were evaluated through four analytical perspectives: • Product‑based assessment: reflecting impacts and dependencies associated with Telix’s products and services and their value chains. • Industry‑based assessment: capturing sector‑wide sustainability patterns and risks. • Geographic assessment: considering the locations of Telix’s operations, suppliers, and customers. • Data point‑based assessment: incorporating company‑specific sustainability indicators where relevant. Each topic was assessed using defined criteria for scale, scope, irremediability (for negative impacts only), and likelihood, and then positioned along axes of impact and financial materiality. Scoring, thresholds and governance Sustainability topics were quantified using Upright’s established scoring methodology: • Impact materiality is determined by multiplying scale, scope, irremediability, and likelihood, with individual impacts considered material where scores meet or exceed defined thresholds. • Financial risks and opportunities are assessed based on the likelihood and magnitude of potential financial effects, with explicit thresholds applied to identify financially relevant risks and opportunities. All assessments were conducted on a gross basis, without considering existing mitigation measures, to provide a transparent view of inherent impacts and exposures. 44 The DMA was carried out over a six‑month period and included interviews and workshops with stakeholders across eight functional areas, including People & Culture, Legal, Environment, Health & Safety, Finance, and Risk. Results were independently calibrated using Upright’s data model and validated by Telix’s SWG, with findings reviewed by members of the ARC. Telix intends to refresh the DMA every three years to reflect changes in operations, regulation, and stakeholder expectations. Stakeholder engagement Stakeholder input was incorporated to enhance the relevance and robustness of the assessment. Engagement activities included: • Internal stakeholders: interviews and surveys across key functional areas to capture operational insights and emerging risks. • External stakeholders: engagement with institutional investors, suppliers, and B2B customers to understand perspectives on sustainability‑related risks, governance expectations, and value‑chain considerations. Stakeholder views were used to validate and, where necessary, challenge the science‑based results, ensuring that the DMA reflects both objective evidence and real‑world business context. In December 2025 we participated in a Type A meeting with the FDA to align on plans to address CMC deficiencies cited, and in January 2026 we participated in an additional Type A meeting to align on plans to address comparability between the clinical drug product and that intended for commercial use. We face competition with respect to our Commercial Products and will face competition with respect to any product candidates that we are developing and may seek to commercialize in the future, from major pharmaceutical companies, specialty pharmaceutical companies, biotechnology companies, academic institutions and governmental agencies as well as public and private research institutions worldwide, many of which have significantly greater financial resources and expertise in research and development, manufacturing, preclinical studies, conducting clinical trials, obtaining regulatory approvals and marketing approved products than we do. There are a number of major pharmaceutical, specialty pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies that currently market and sell diagnostics and therapies and/or are pursuing the development of diagnostics and therapies for the treatment of cancer and the other disease indications for which we are developing our products and product candidates.
