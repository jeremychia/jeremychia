EX-99.1 2 ex991q326pressrelease.htm EX-99.1 Document Neighborhood Intelligence Reports Core E-Commerce Growth of 9%, Led by 75% Growth at Overstock.com; Total Company Revenue Increases 98% Driven by Acquisitions Gross Margin Performance Positioned for Improvement; Significant Progress on Cost Reductions and Merger Synergies, With Another $40 Million in Annualized Savings Identified NASHVILLE, Tennessee - November 5, 2026 - Neighborhood Intelligence, Inc. (NASDAQ:NXH), formerly Bed Bath & Beyond, Inc., today reported third quarter 2026 revenue of $509 million, an increase of 98% year over year, primarily driven by the acquisitions of Kirkland’s and The Container Store. Core e-commerce revenue increased approximately 9%, led by 75% year-over-year growth at Overstock.com. Gross profit was $173 million, representing a reported gross margin of approximately 34%. The quarter included a planned $14 million purchase price accounting benefit associated with inventory conditions identified and factored into the acquisitions before closing. This benefit was substantially offset by the Company’s planned elimination of dated, obsolete and underperforming inventory. Management sees a path to 37% through improved sourcing, assortment, inventory discipline and higher-margin home services. Year to date, the Company has eliminated more than $50 million in annualized SG&A costs and merger synergies. Management has identified more than $40 million in additional annualized operational and merger savings through supply chain consolidation, elimination of organizational redundancies and optimization of the store fleet. The Company expects the full benefits of integration and merger synergies to develop over approximately six to nine months. Management believes if it achieves mid-single-digit organic revenue growth, improved operating efficiencies and expansion of higher-margin home services the Company should be positioned to achieve an enterprise-wide positive cash flow inflection by the second half of 2027. Inclusive of all closed transactions, retail store fleet rationalization and modest organic growth, the Company currently expects consolidated revenue of $2.0 billion to $2.1 billion in 2027, with gross margins ranging from 33% to 34%. “Our legacy e-commerce business has achieved two important milestones worth noting: sustained revenue growth and adjusted EBITDA neutrality. We have accomplished what we set out to do in the underlying legacy business, and our focus now is on realizing the full potential of the enterprise we have assembled,” said Marcus A. Lemonis, Executive Chairman and Chief Executive Officer of Neighborhood Intelligence. “While our baseline for 2027 is deliberately measured and assumes a continued depressed housing market, we are moving quickly to prepare for a more robust return to normalized housing activity. Our decision to accelerate the integration of our acquisitions, recalibrate inventory and simplify our operating structure reflects that conviction. We believe we can compound growth without a housing recovery, but even a modest improvement in housing activity could create meaningful upside and operating leverage.” Third Quarter 2026 Highlights Net revenue was $509 million, an increase of 98.0% year over year. Of the year-over-year increase, $229 million was attributable to acquired businesses, reflecting the integration of The Brand House Collective, including the Kirkland’s and Kirkland’s Home brands, and The Container Store, which was acquired during 1 the quarter. The remaining $23 million increase was driven by continued strength in the core online e-commerce business and an improved assortment. Active customers increased to 9.0 million, up 117% year over year, and orders delivered increased to 4.0 million, up 217% year over year, reflecting growth in the base business and the inclusion of acquired brands. Orders per active customer increased to 2.0 from 1.3 in the prior-year period, an increase of 53%. Gross profit was $173 million, or approximately 34% of net revenue. Of the year-over-year increase, $97 million was attributable to acquired businesses. The quarter included the planned purchase price accounting benefit and related inventory recalibration described above. Sales and marketing expense was $54 million, or 10.7% of net revenue, an improvement of 330 basis points year over year. Technology and general and administrative expense was $165 million compared with $33 million in the prior-year period, primarily due to acquired entities, with the remaining change attributable to various items, including professional fees related to acquisitions. Net loss was $26 million, compared with a net loss of $5 million in the prior-year period. The current period includes approximately $20 million of special items, primarily acquisition-related costs, restructuring costs, and non-cash store-closure impairments. Adjusted EBITDA (non-GAAP) was ($26) million, compared with ($5) million in the prior year period. The Company's available sources of liquidity include, but are not limited to $127 million of cash, cash equivalents and restricted cash, $228 million in inventory and real estate owned free of borrowings of $26M. Equity in inventory at September 30, 2026 was $110 million, net of ABL borrowings of $118M. Longer-Term Growth Opportunity The Company’s 2027 baseline assumes continued weakness in housing starts and existing-home sales, with existing-home sales remaining near levels last seen during the Great Recession. Existing-home sales in 2025 were below 2009 levels and approximately 25% below the 2015–2019 average. Management believes approximately 5% annual organic revenue growth is achievable from its 2027 base over the following three to four years without requiring a housing recovery. We see additional upside from 3 additional primary drivers. High Margin, High Average Order Home Services, Namely Elfa Systems being restocked with rebuilt sales funnel and lead management system; Full integration of Bed Bath & Beyond traditional product assortment and brand across 75+ store fleet (product being fully stocked now with signage and branding complete by Q1 2027); any potential improvement in the housing market currently recording both housing starts and existing home sales below 2009 Great Recession levels. Even a modest improvement in housing activity could provide additional upside. A gradual return toward mid-cycle levels, still below those experienced during 2016–2019, could create meaningful operating leverage. Management believes it is able to generate approximately $30 million in incremental operating contribution for every additional $100 million of organic revenue. “We have a clear plan to drive organic growth, strengthen margins and improve operating efficiency across our business,” said Amy Sullivan, President of Neighborhood Intelligence, Inc. “We’re leveraging the brands, sourcing capabilities and home services infrastructure we’ve acquired to create new opportunities for growth, while remaining disciplined in how we manage costs and deploy capital. About Neighborhood Intelligence, Inc. Neighborhood Intelligence, Inc. (NASDAQ: NXH), formerly Bed Bath & Beyond, Inc., is a data and technology company organized around three interconnected pillars: Omni-Channel Retail, Home Services and Home Ownership. Its portfolio includes Bed Bath & Beyond, Overstock, buybuy BABY, Kirkland's, The Container Store, Elfa and Closet Works, along with its expanding Home Services and Home Ownership businesses. The Company also maintains a portfolio of blockchain-related investments, including tZERO, GrainChain and other assets. The Company's mission is to make home ownership simpler and more affordable. We believe these metrics provide investors with additional information regarding our financial results and provide key performance indicators to track our progress. These indicators include changes in customer order patterns and the mix of products purchased by our customers. Active customers represent the total number of unique customers who have made at least one purchase during the prior twelve-month period. This metric captures both the inflow of new customers and the outflow of existing customers who have not made a purchase during the prior twelve-month period. Last twelve months (LTM) net revenue per active customer represents total net revenue in a twelve-month period divided by the total number of active customers for the same twelve-month period. Orders delivered represents the total number of orders delivered in any given period, including orders that may eventually be returned. As we ship a large volume of packages through multiple carriers, actual delivery dates may not always be available, and in those circumstances, we estimate delivery dates based on historical data. Average order value is defined as total net revenue in any given period divided by the total number of orders delivered in that period. Orders per active customer is defined as orders delivered in a twelve-month period divided by active customers for the same twelve-month period. We use these non-GAAP measures internally in analyzing our financial results and we believe they are useful to investors, as a supplement to GAAP measures, in evaluating our ongoing operational performance and, in the case of free cash flow, our liquidity position, in the same manner as our management and board of directors. We have provided reconciliations of these non-GAAP financial measures to the most directly comparable GAAP measures in this earnings release. These non-GAAP financial measures should be used in addition to and in conjunction with the results presented in accordance with GAAP and should not be relied upon to the exclusion of GAAP financial measures. 9 Adjusted diluted net loss per share is a non-GAAP financial measure that is calculated as net income (net loss) less the income or losses recognized from our equity method securities and the gains on derecognition of loan commitment. We believe that these adjustments to our net income (net loss) before calculating per share amounts provide meaningful supplemental information for investors by excluding items that are not reflective of our core operations and that introduce significant volatility into GAAP results. We believe excluding these adjustments enhances the comparability of our financial results across reporting periods and offers greater and supplemental insight into the performance of our core operations when presented in conjunction with our GAAP disclosures. More specifically, the income or loss from equity method securities relates to our blockchain asset portfolio, which includes passive investments in early-stage blockchain technology companies. These investments are ancillary to our retail business and are not part of our strategic or operational planning for the retail segment. Additionally, the market value and performance of these investments are subject to material volatility driven by external factors unrelated to our retail business. Similarly, the gain on derecognition of loan commitment arises from non-operating investments where we do not participate in day-to-day operations, capital allocation, or economically significant decision-making. These gains or losses are all non-cash, and the Company believes their inclusion in GAAP net income (net loss) can, in the absence of additional context, distort the comparability of our operating performance across periods. Adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP financial measure that is calculated as net income (net loss) before depreciation and amortization, stock-based compensation, interest and other income (expense), provision (benefit) for income taxes, and special items. We believe the exclusion of certain benefits and expenses in calculating adjusted EBITDA facilitates operating performance comparisons on a period-to-period basis. Exclusion of items in the non-GAAP presentation should not be construed as an inference that these items are unusual, infrequent or non-recurring. Free cash flow is a non-GAAP financial measure that is calculated as net cash provided by or used in operating activities reduced by expenditures for property and equipment. We believe free cash flow is a useful measure to evaluate the cash impact of the operations of the business including purchases of property and equipment which are a necessary component of our ongoing operations.
