Termination of a Material Definitive Agreement. As previously disclosed in the Annual Report on Form 10-K of Premier Air Charter Holdings Inc. (the “Company”) for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, the Company’s wholly owned operating subsidiary, Premier Air Charter, Inc. (“Premier”), operated two Cessna Citation CJ3 aircraft (together, the “CJ3 Aircraft”), under (i) an Exclusive Aircraft Lease Agreement dated December 18, 2024 between AB Jet Co I, LLC and Premier, as amended by Amendment No. 1 to Exclusive Aircraft Lease Agreement dated June 5, 2026 (as so amended, the “N795KF Lease”), and (ii) an Exclusive Aircraft Lease Agreement dated December 27, 2024 between AB Jet Co VI, LLC and Premier, as amended by Amendment No. 1 to Exclusive Aircraft Lease Agreement dated June 5, 2026 (as so amended, the “N973CG Lease” and, together with the N795KF Lease, the “CJ3 Leases”). AB Jet Co I, LLC and AB Jet Co VI, LLC are referred to together as the “Lessors.” The CJ3 Leases provided for monthly rent, and the June 5, 2026 amendments provided for, among other things, an adjusted monthly rent, the repayment by Premier of a principal balance of previously accrued and unpaid amounts (the “Principal Balance”) in installments under an agreed payment schedule, a forbearance by the Lessors with respect to then-existing defaults, and a covenant by Premier to keep the engine maintenance programs applicable to the CJ3 Aircraft current and in good standing. On September 23, 2026, each Lessor delivered to Premier a written notice of default under the applicable CJ3 Lease, asserting that Premier had failed to make certain required payments of rent and of installments of the Principal Balance and had failed to keep the applicable engine maintenance programs current, and affording Premier five business days from receipt to cure. The notices asserted that Premier owed AB Jet Co I, LLC approximately $249,416 and owed AB Jet Co VI, LLC approximately $265,141. The notices further asserted that approximately $129,797 was owed to Williams International and Textron Aviation under the engine and parts programs applicable to N795KF and approximately $137,044 was owed to Williams International under the engine program applicable to N973CG. Premier did not cure the asserted defaults within the cure period. On October 1, 2026, the Lessors terminated the CJ3 Leases and retook possession of the CJ3 Aircraft Premier and the Lessors agreed to terminate the CJ3 Leases and Premier returned the CJ3 Aircraft to the Lessors, and the CJ3 Aircraft were removed from Premier’s FAA Part 135 air carrier certificate effective October 1, 2026. Termination of the CJ3 Leases does not relieve Premier of its obligation to pay the amounts described above, and the Lessors have reserved all rights and remedies under the CJ3 Leases, including the right to declare the entire remaining Principal Balance, together with accrued and unpaid interest, immediately due and payable. See Item 2.04 below. Other than as described in this Item 1.02 and in Item 2.04, no early termination penalties were incurred by the Company in connection with the termination of the CJ3 Leases. The Company is in discussions with the Lessors regarding the resolution of the outstanding amounts, and no assurance can be given that those discussions will result in an agreement. Item 2.04. Triggering Events That Accelerate or Increase a Direct Financial Obligation or an Obligation under an Off-Balance Sheet Arrangement. The information set forth in Item 1.02 of this Current Report on Form 8-K is incorporated by reference into this Item 2.04. Under Section 5 of each of the June 5, 2026 amendments to the CJ3 Leases, Premier’s failure to cure an amendment default or repayment default within the five business day cure period permits the applicable Lessor, without further notice, to declare the forbearance terminated, to treat the previously existing defaults as immediately enforceable, to declare the entire Principal Balance, together with accrued and unpaid interest, immediately due and payable, to terminate the applicable CJ3 Lease, and to repossess the applicable aircraft. As of the date of this report, the aggregate amount claimed by the Lessors to be due and payable under the CJ3 Leases, including the accelerated Principal Balance, accrued interest and the unpaid rent described in Item 1.02, is approximately $514,557, consisting of approximately $249,416 under the N795KF Lease and approximately $265,141 under the N973CG Lease. These amounts do not include the approximately $266,842 asserted by the Lessors to be owed by Premier to Williams International and Textron Aviation under the engine and parts programs applicable to the CJ3 Aircraft, which the Company disputes in part. The Company had previously recorded the Principal Balance as a liability on its balance sheet. The Company’s obligations under the CJ3 Leases are not guaranteed by the Company and are not cross-defaulted to the Company’s other aircraft leases or indebtedness. Item 2.05. Costs Associated with Exit or Disposal Activities. As a result of the return of the CJ3 Aircraft and the termination of the management arrangement for the CJ2 aircraft described in Item 8.01 below, Premier no longer operates any Cessna Citation CJ-series aircraft. On October 2, 2026, management of the Company committed to a reduction in force under which five of Premier’s ten Citation CJ-rated pilots will be separated from employment and the remaining five will be retrained and transitioned to other aircraft types in Premier’s fleet. The reduction in force reduces the Company’s workforce from 42 to 37 employees, or approximately 12%. The separations are expected to be completed by October 2, 2026. The Company currently estimates that it will not incur any material pre-tax charges of in connection with the reduction in force. The Company does not expect to record any material impairment charges as a result of the return of the CJ3 Aircraft, because the CJ3 Aircraft were leased on a month-to-month basis and no long-lived assets of the Company are associated with them.
