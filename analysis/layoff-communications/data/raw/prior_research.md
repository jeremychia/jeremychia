# Prior research on layoff communications and layoff totals (batch G findings)

## Layoffs.fyi totals by year (tech; secondary reports, layoffs.fyi itself not machine-readable via fetch)
- 2022: ~165,000 (165,269; ~1,064 companies)
- 2023: ~263,000 (262,735 to 265,660; ~1,194 companies) - peak year
- 2024: ~153,000 (152,922; 551 companies, NerdWallet)
- 2025: ~122,000 (122,549, 257 companies per NerdWallet; another list 122,606 / 278)
- Counts shift by snapshot date; verify at https://layoffs.fyi
- Sources: https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/tech-layoffs ; https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/layoffs-2024 ; https://blog.kowatek.com/2025/02/06/a-comprehensive-list-of-2024-and-2025-tech-layoffs/

- Secondhand summary: WaPo reviewed 48 publicly available memos from tech companies (startups with >=$50M raised up to trillion-dollar firms) since summer 2022; sentences split programmatically, tagged by keyword, manually vetted, multi-category allowed.

## Academic / other studies
- CUNY master's thesis (2024): thematic analysis of Meta, Google, Microsoft 2022-23 layoff memos vs 2009 memos; finds more passive/distancing language. https://academicworks.cuny.edu/bb_etds/175/
- Zdaniuk & Chhinzer (2019), Journal of Organizational Change Management: 388 Canadian downsizing announcements 2006-2015; explanation types (excuses/justifications/apologies/denials) and CEO presence vs shareholder reaction. https://www.econbiz.de/Record/the-effect-of-explanations-and-ceo-presence-on-stock-market-reactions-to-downsizing-zdaniuk-agnes/10012076371 ; summary https://news.uoguelph.ca/2019/11/tone-messenger-of-layoff-announcements-impact-shareholder-reaction-u-of-g-study-finds
- Scheuerlein & Chladkova: 594 shareholder letters from 104 CEOs 2006-2011 (not layoff-specific): first-person plural use rose in crisis. https://instituteforpr.org/an-analysis-of-leadership-letters-to-shareholders-during-the-2008-financial-crisis
