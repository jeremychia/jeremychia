Hearst Newspapers LogoSkip to main contentTechAmid Trump tariff worries, Bay Area tech giant HP lays off 2,000 workersBy Aidin Vaziri, Staff WriterUpdated Feb 28, 2025 8:25 a.m.Add UsHP has announced 2,000 job cuts as tariff concerns are expected to impact second-quarter earnings.

HP announced Thursday that it will cut up to 2,000 jobs as part of an ongoing restructuring plan.

The Bay Area tech giant expects to save $300 million by the end of its fiscal year in October 2025 due to the cuts, though it will incur $150 million in restructuring costs, mainly due to labor expenses.

HP employs around 58,000 workers across 59 countries, according to the Wall Street Journal, which first reported the cuts.

The restructuring plan, called “Future Now,” was introduced in November 2022, with the initial goal of cutting 7,000 jobs. The latest layoffs will bring the total number of job cuts under the plan to 9,000.

After this new round of cuts, HP expects to save $1.9 billion through the plan, with total restructuring charges reaching about $1.2 billion.

The announcement follows the Palo Alto company’s stronger-than-expected financial results for the first quarter. HP saw increased demand for its new technologies, particularly AI-powered PCs, with Chief Executive Officer Enrique Lores noting a 25% growth in the PC market.

However, HP warned that tariffs will affect its second-quarter earnings, resulting in lower-than-expected results.

“The company has made significant progress building a globally diverse supply chain, and by the end of fiscal year 2025, expects more than 90 percent of HP products sold in North America will be built outside of China,” the company said in its earnings report. “China will continue to be an important manufacturing hub for the rest of the world.”

For the first quarter, HP posted a profit of $565 million, down from $622 million a year earlier. Revenue for the quarter increased by 2.4% to $13.5 billion, with personal systems revenue rising by 5%. However, printing revenue fell by 2%.
