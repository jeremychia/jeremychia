description: Deliverables carry no trace of their own drafting — no earlier passes, no self-correction, no "you asked"

---

**Write every document as though the reader is seeing it for the first time.** No reference
to an earlier pass, no correction of a previous draft, no note about what I got wrong or
invented, no "you asked for this."

What gets cut:

- **self-correction as content.** "I got this wrong in an earlier pass. I read X and
  concluded Y. That conflated two things" → just state the distinction: "how you deliver
  and what drives you are two separate things."
- **my own authorship as a subject.** "I invented the number" → "use a number you know."
  "I wrote these as if they were facts about you" → "these four details are unverified."
  The instruction survives; the confession goes.
- **the draft's own history.** "this was missing from every draft", "the strongest idea
  available, and I'd left it out."
- **the request that produced the section.** "you asked. here they are honestly."
- **the failure being attributed back to the reader's earlier words.** "it's the specific
  failure you said you wanted to avoid" → "it's the failure most worth avoiding."

**Why:** the reader uses the document, they do not audit how it was built. Drafting history
makes them re-read for my benefit, and it undercuts the content it is attached to — a
verified instruction next to an apology reads as unreliable. Where something did go wrong
in an earlier pass, the learning belongs in this memory directory, not in the deliverable.

**How to apply:** after drafting, grep the document for first-person authorial voice — "I
invented", "I got this wrong", "earlier", "draft", "you asked", "as I said". Keep the
instruction or fact each one was carrying and delete the frame around it. Same rule as
[[one-line-comments-and-docstrings]] applied to prose: the artefact carries what is true
now, the history goes elsewhere. Length rules still apply on top —
[[writing-concise-and-lowercase]] and [[plain-language-in-writeups]].
