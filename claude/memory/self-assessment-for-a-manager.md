**Build a self-assessment as a titled document in four numbered parts, in this order: impact summary, framework assessment, growth actions, weekly log.** The assessment comes first because it is what the manager acts on. The log comes last because it is the evidence.

Jeremy rewrote my version as the model. The content was the same. The shape was not:

- **A title block:** the doc's title, the review period, and a link to the primary artifact, each on its own line.
- **Highlights before any table.** Four bullets, each with a bold label and its **bold number**: "**Financial integrity:** kept **~€2.7m** of cost out of the wrong period". The detailed impact table follows under its own sub-heading.
- **Framework table columns:** dimension (bold), expectation, my evidence, current level, gap to the next level.
  - **Expectation:** phrase it as a contrast, "design complex architectures alone (IC3) vs. enable others to do so (IC4)".
  - **Current level:** bold, in one fixed format: `IC3`, `IC3 (IC4 in part)`, `IC3 in part`, `Met` or `Gap`.
  - **Gap cell:** never "—". A strong dimension still names its next increment.
  - **Split every gap in two.** Use one column for what my own behaviour can close, and one for what needs an opportunity: a scope, a role, a mentee, a seat in planning, or a business need. The framework says IC4 needs a business need, so a gap in that column is not mine to close alone. It is a question for the manager.
- **The framework's definition goes in a blockquote** above the table. One or two sentences.
- **Growth actions are a numbered list, not a wide table.** Each one gets a named title in bold ("Enable, rather than do") and its dimensions in italics. Mark each as **Behaviour** (I act) or **Opportunity** (ask the manager). Group the opportunities into one action to discuss. Under it go three labelled lines: **Action**, **Done when**, **Target**. A six-column table crams the action into a cell nobody can read.
- **Weekly log: one heading per week, with bullets under it**, not table rows. Each bullet starts with a past-tense verb ("Converted", "Patched", "Traced"). Value tags go at the end in brackets, e.g. `[Ownership / Co-create]`.
- **Standard capitalisation:** sentences start with a capital letter, and acronyms keep theirs (GB, VAT, MEC, CI). This doc is read by a manager outside code review, so the lowercase rule in [[writing-concise-and-lowercase]] does not apply to it. Headings stay in sentence case, per [[readme-built-for-skimming]].

Don't copy that example's defects:

- **links replaced by code-formatted labels.** Keep every reference a working link, per [[links-carry-where-it-was-said]].
- **figures whose meaning drifted.** "Lines filed as GB instead of UK" became "wrongly classified as standard UK transactions". "Models at risk of the limit" became "exceeding the limit". "The third hand-run request" became "only 3 requests". An open fix became "corrected".
- **LaTeX (`$\le 2$`)**, which a doc editor shows as raw text.
- **"above and beyond" dropped.** He asks for it, so keep it as a tag.

**Why:** a manager reads this to decide on a level and on next steps. Leading with the verdict and the gaps, rather than the chronology, puts that decision first. The log stays as the evidence behind it.

**How to apply:** when writing a brag list, self-assessment or promotion case, build the four parts first, then fill them. Before restyling a rewrite, re-check every figure against the source per [[verify-before-asserting]]. Short sentences and plain words still apply, per [[plain-language-in-writeups]].
