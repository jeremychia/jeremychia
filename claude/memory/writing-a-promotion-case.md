**Open a promotion case with the ask, not the story.** Put one pattern sentence first: the contribution that repeats across the wins. Then give four lines: the ask (the level, and the number), why it matters, why now, and the next steps with dates. The evidence goes underneath. Don't use investigation order (joined, learned, found, tried, result), because it buries the ask.

- **Test the top:** the manager should be able to repeat the ask and its reason in one sentence from the top section alone. If they can't, fix the structure. More evidence won't help.
- **The number is the author's to write.** Leave a marked prompt for it. Keep a salary figure out of any public repo.

**Translate each win in three steps:** what I built (8 words at most), what changed because of it, then the business driver: revenue, cost, risk or time.

- **The third step is someone else's behaviour or trust.** "Pipeline failures down 80%" stops at step two. "The board report went out on time, so finance stopped keeping a parallel spreadsheet" reaches step three.
- **Never invent money for step three.** Use a € figure only when the source states it. "Finance stopped submitting the wrong numbers" is stronger than an estimate that won't survive a question.
- **"Helped with" is worth nothing.** Name what I did and what changed.

**Make judgment visible with a decision log.** For each important piece of work, record the alternatives I rejected and why, the request I redirected and what I said, and the trade-off I made and what it cost. Judgment leaves no trace in metrics or output, so a reviewer only sees it if it is written down. Count saying no, cross-team alignment and mentoring as wins too.

**Write down every 1:1 promise with its date and owner.** A spoken promise leaves with the manager who made it. A written case survives a reorg or a new manager.

**Ask for criteria that can't shift.** Ask "what do you expect someone at my level to do?" Push "be more strategic" down to a project, a behaviour or a measurable outcome. Treat "not yet" as a diagnosis, and ask which specific gaps remain. Start the conversation 3–6 months before the change is due.

Source: [Data Gibberish, career pillar](https://www.datagibberish.com/t/pillar-career), especially the Design Your Promotion series (Sep 2026), "Your Judgment Is Invisible" and "No Formal Review Process". Most of the series is paywalled; these tips come from the free parts.

**Why:** Jeremy asked for these tips to be kept for his brag docs. The person who decides a promotion rarely saw the work. They read a compressed version in a few minutes and understand money, time, risk and speed.

**How to apply:** when building a brag list, self-assessment or promotion case, apply these on top of the shape in [[self-assessment-for-a-manager]]. Quote every figure from its source, per [[verify-before-asserting]]. Write the "so what changed" clause only where the evidence shows another person or team acting differently. Otherwise mark it "to confirm".
