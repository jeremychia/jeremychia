# Rejection Feedback — Analytics Engineer @ Distribusion

**Date received:** 2026-06-12
**Stage reached:** Final round (recruiter → hiring manager → VP Engineering → PM)

---

## What they said

> There were several strengths that stood out during the process. We noted your strong business and finance background, your focus on measurable impact and business outcomes, and your ability to think strategically about complex problems.
>
> Ultimately, our decision was driven by a few areas where we felt there was not the strongest match for the needs of this particular role and team. In particular, we saw opportunities for more concise and structured communication, especially when discussing complex topics. In particular, we were looking for stronger evidence of comfort and effectiveness in situations where priorities shift frequently, multiple urgent requests compete for attention, and ambiguity is a regular part of the day-to-day work.

## Honest analysis

### On the communication criticism — take it

This is credible and fixable. The prep docs for this process were sharp and specific, but the tendency is to be thorough at the cost of brevity. In a fast-paced PM-heavy environment (Kilian came from Capgemini BA and thinks in requirements; Ilya comes from billing systems where precision matters), over-explaining reads as uncertainty rather than rigour. The feedback is: land the point, stop, wait. Don't fill silence with elaboration.

**What to change:** In every interview story, treat the result sentence as the end. Don't add context after the number. If they want more, they'll ask.

### On ambiguity — partially fair, partially a role mismatch

The "shifting priorities" criticism has two components worth separating:

**The fair part:** The prep framing leaned heavily on "I don't wait for tickets / I fix things proactively." That's a story about initiative in a stable environment — not comfort with genuine chaos. Kilian's notes flagged he wanted someone who follows up every 2 days unprompted, operates under a new data PM coordinating a chaotic backlog, and doesn't need structure to function. If stories were all about building systematic solutions, it may have read as someone who *creates* structure rather than someone who *thrives without it*.

**The mismatch part:** The role had structural tension that wasn't fully resolved before hiring. Dmitry wanted a stakeholder-heavy senior AE replacement. Evgeny wanted data platform investment. Kilian wanted a short-cycle, proactive request-handler. These are three different profiles. The "ambiguity" feedback may partly reflect that the interviewers themselves hadn't fully aligned on what they needed — and you didn't fit the union of all three.

### On the company — red flags noted in real-time but not fully interrogated

The Kilian interview note read: *"big partner projects - okr / refinements, it is scaling a lot, and it needs to fit into the overall project. and it may not actively commit"* followed by *"red flag."* The flag was noticed but not acted on. That passive observation in the room may itself have been visible — not probing when something important surfaces is exactly the opposite of the "proactive" operating mode they wanted.

The €1,000/day AI spend with no guardrails is a symptom, not just a priority. A team that ships that fast without cost controls operates differently from someone who builds reconciliation checks and catches billing discrepancies before they hit the books. The values were probably not as aligned as the technical preparation assumed.

**Three of four senior people (Ilya, Evgeny, Dmitry) have Russian Big Tech backgrounds** (Badoo, OZON-scale systems). That culture tends to be high-context, results-at-all-costs, and treats ambiguity as a permanent operating condition rather than a problem to resolve. The profile here — high standards, explicit structure, results through systems — can chafe in that environment even when the technical skills are a strong match.

---

## What to carry forward

| Observation | Action |
|---|---|
| Communication brevity | In stories: result sentence = full stop. Don't elaborate unprompted. |
| Ambiguity framing | Add stories that show *functioning in noise*, not just *converting noise to structure*. |
| Red flag moments | When something surfaces in an interview that signals misalignment, name it — don't just note it internally. |
| Culture fit read | Before final rounds: explicitly ask how priorities are set and how often they change. Use the answer to calibrate, not just to seem curious. |
| Prep vs performance gap | The prep was thorough. The gap was execution under pressure. Practice delivering stories at 70% of the detail you think is needed. |
