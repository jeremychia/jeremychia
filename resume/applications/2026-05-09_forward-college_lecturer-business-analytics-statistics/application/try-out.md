Lovett and Greenhouse (2000):
(1) optimal learning occurs when students practice and perform autonomously,
(2) knowledge is contextually bound to where it was learned,
(3) real-time feedback supports learning efficiency,
(4) learning is based on the integration of existing and new knowledge, and, lastly, 
(5) mental overload decreases learning efficiency.

# how i would structure the class:

example: using this topic:
2. Univariate data visualisation and descriptive statistics.  

pre-reading:

[Albright, S. and W. Winston Business Analytics: Data Analysis and Decision Making, (South-Western, 2016) sixth edition [ISBN 9781305947542].](https://archive.org/details/businessanalytic0000albr_6ed/page/n9/mode/2up)

Part I Exploring Data - Chapter 2 Describing the Distribution of a Single Variable (Pg. 17-78)

* [Descriptive Statistics [Simply explained]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FzujIYo9GYo)
* [Descriptive Statistics: FULL Tutorial - Mean, Median, Mode, Variance & SD (With Examples)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SplCk-t1BeA)

pre-task:

complete a tutorial which covers concepts which are required (i.e. they have a numerical dataset, and they're supposed to compute the dataset)

students should find a dataset with numerical column, make a submission of it on the online portal
explain the significance of why they've chosen it. this should be something they know something about.

some websites which they can consider:
* [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/)
* [berlin open data](https://daten.berlin.de/)
* [paris data](https://opendata.paris.fr/pages/home/)
* [open data portal of the portuguese public administration](https://dados.gov.pt/)

in-class:

| activity | time |
|-|-|
| mini-quiz to test understanding | 5 minutes |
| classmates will present the tutorial solutions, students can ask questions | 30 minutes | 
| pair-work: pick a dataset (not your own), do an analysis of it (maybe using jupyter notebooks), write briefly what they can learn from it. find more context about why the statistics appear the way they are | 15 minutes |
| mini-discussion: present the dataset and the statistical summary. the person who submitted the dataset can give more inputs and feedback on their observations - 3 minutes per pair | 20 minutes (buffer 30 minutes) |
| debrief from instructor, commentary on how it links to the next topic: finding relationships between variables | 10 minutes |

post-class:

reflection post (linkedin style, or instagram reel) about some of the datasets they've shared and what they've learnt about how to describe datasets (max 30 minutes on it)

[Critical Thinking Tutorial: Statistical Misrepresentation](https://libguides.usask.ca/CriticalThinkingTutorial/HowReasoningFails/StatisticalMisrepresentation)
