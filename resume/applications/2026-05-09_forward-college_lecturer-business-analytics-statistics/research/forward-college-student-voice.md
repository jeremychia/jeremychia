# Forward College: What Students Actually Say

Research drawn from blog posts on [forward-college.eu/the-forward-blog/](https://forward-college.eu/the-forward-blog/), fetched June 2026. All quotes are from named students unless otherwise noted.

---

## Actionable Points for a Prospective Lecturer

These are the patterns that repeat across student voices. Each has a practical implication for how to teach well at Forward.

### 1. Small class sizes are load-bearing — students notice and expect it

> "I appreciate the small class sizes and the close relationship with professors, who truly support both our academic growth and emotional intelligence."
> — Mathilde Fraisse (first-year, Economics & Management + Business & Leadership) | [Meet Mathilde Fraisse](https://forward-college.eu/blog/meet-mathilde-fraisse/), Jan 2026

> "The friendliness of the staff and teachers, who are passionate and caring towards their students."
> — Bruno De Martino (first-year, Politics & IR) | [Meet Bruno De Martino](https://forward-college.eu/blog/student-story-interview-bruno-de-martino/), Dec 2024

**Implication:** Students arrive having been told the professor-student relationship will be personal. They will notice immediately if it isn't. Know names, remember academic interests, track who is struggling. This is baseline, not a bonus.

---

### 2. The flipped classroom is already internalised by students who chose Forward

> "The flipped classroom method fits my personality and programme, as I believe that communication, collaboration, and comparison are key elements for developing knowledge."
> — Bruno De Martino | [Meet Bruno De Martino](https://forward-college.eu/blog/student-story-interview-bruno-de-martino/), Dec 2024

> "[Class time is for] debating ideas, discussing concepts, and sharing perspectives" [rather than passive reception]
> — Anonymous French student | [Beyond Parcoursup](https://forward-college.eu/blog/beyond-parcoursup-why-i-traded-the-standard-french-uni-route-for-3-cities-in-3-years/), May 2026

**Implication:** Students self-select for this model. They will come prepared if they trust the format. Hold the line on pre-reading expectations — seminars that fall back to content delivery will disappoint the students most engaged in the programme.

---

### 3. One-on-one sessions are where trust is built

> "One-to-one sessions are genuinely supportive rather than evaluative, with faculty providing tailored advice based on individual interests and career goals."
> — Anonymous French student | [Beyond Parcoursup](https://forward-college.eu/blog/beyond-parcoursup-why-i-traded-the-standard-french-uni-route-for-3-cities-in-3-years/), May 2026

**Implication:** Tutorial time is not for content catch-up. Students come wanting to talk about their trajectory — connect coursework to their interests, goals, and (in Year 3) professional plans. Come prepared with observations about the specific student, not a generic progress review.

---

### 4. Project-based learning has real external credibility — students use it in interviews

> "I always felt lucky that I could draw on my Project-Based Learning examples, which led me to have very solid responses."
> — Isaac (gained acceptances to Columbia, Georgia Tech, UCLA, Duke, Carnegie Mellon, Boston University; waitlisted at MIT) | [From Forward College to Columbia and beyond](https://forward-college.eu/blog/from-forward-college-to-columbia-and-beyond-isaacs-road-to-top-american-universities/), Jun 2026

> "Forward gave me the best of both worlds — the opportunity to grow academically while exploring my curiosity."
> — Isaac | ibid.

**Implication:** Design projects with a story students can tell. What was the real-world problem? What did they decide and why? What would they do differently? These become interview currency at Masters programmes and employers. Debriefs and reflection built into the project cycle matter.

---

### 5. The multi-city structure builds genuine adaptability — not just a talking point

> "Forward prepared me especially through the 'three years, three cities' journey and the double bachelor's programme. It helped me not only in getting the internship, but also in adapting quickly to new environments."
> — Tassilo (internship at Austria's UN Mission in New York; now MSc Economics & Governance, Leiden) | [From the classroom to the UN](https://forward-college.eu/blog/from-the-classroom-to-the-un-how-forward-college-opened-the-door-to-a-global-internship/), Jun 2026

> "For me, it is the value of stepping into unfamiliar situations...those experiences have helped me understand both myself and others much better."
> — Henriette Kjelaas (second-year, Psychology and Business & Leadership) | [Experiencing Growth Beyond Borders](https://forward-college.eu/blog/experiencing-growth-beyond-borders-a-conversation-with-henriette/), Apr 2026

**Implication:** Students are already practising cultural adaptability as lived experience. Statistics and analytics examples drawn from cross-country or cross-cultural data land better here than at a typical domestic university. Frame technical skills as portable across contexts.

---

### 6. Personal Development isn't soft — students find it confronting and genuinely useful

> "It has been tremendously helpful for me to realise, session after session, in our open discussions, that others struggle just as much as I do."
> — Amelie Josten (second-year, Politics & IR, Paris) | [Personal Development at Forward College](https://forward-college.eu/blog/personal-development-at-forward-college/), Mar 2026

> "It makes you reflect on your actions and how you perceive others and I found it helped me with rationalizing communication patterns."
> — Amelie Josten | ibid.

> "What PD teaches us is that the different ways in which everyone processes and adapts to challenges do not mean that some of us are shielded from feeling the struggle."
> — Amelie Josten | ibid.

**Implication:** The PD programme creates an unusually high baseline of emotional literacy. Students are accustomed to talking about difficulty and uncertainty. A statistics or analytics lecturer who acknowledges what is genuinely hard (not just technically) will land well. So will connecting quantitative reasoning to real decisions under uncertainty — which mirrors what PD explores emotionally.

---

### 7. The cohort is the product — peer relationships are pedagogically central

> "Invest in your friends at Forward as they can serve as a safety net...It's about the people!"
> — Emirhan (now MSc Politics & Communications, LSE) | [Planning for the future](https://forward-college.eu/blog/planning-for-the-future-insights-from-forward-college-graduates/), May 2024

> "It also helped to have Forward friends like Caro and Flora doing internships in New York at the same time which meant I had a support network from the very beginning."
> — Tassilo | [From the classroom to the UN](https://forward-college.eu/blog/from-the-classroom-to-the-un-how-forward-college-opened-the-door-to-a-global-internship/), Jun 2026

> "Forward provided space for you to grow beyond exams by giving you an international journey, a close network of inspiring people and the chance to create things hands-on instead of only learning theory."
> — Tassilo | ibid.

**Implication:** Design group work to strengthen cohort bonds, not just to produce deliverables. Teams with deliberate diversity (mixing programmes, nationalities) reinforce what makes Forward distinctive. Students who build strong cohort ties go further — the network is a tangible graduate outcome.

---

### 8. Students arrive in Year 1 managing real emotional difficulty

> "The unfamiliarity of it all was both daunting and exhilarating at once."
> — Sawera Ali (first-year, Psychology, from Karachi, Pakistan) | [Meet Sawera Ali](https://forward-college.eu/blog/student-story-interview-sawera/), Jan 2025

> "The first week proved most difficult emotionally." [Sawera's experience arriving 9,000+ km from home for the first time]
> — Reported summary | ibid.

> "Nervous about workload was outweighed by excitement, but nervousness about workload was real."
> — Amelie Josten | [Meet Amelie Josten](https://forward-college.eu/blog/student-story-interview-amelie-josten/), Jan 2025

**Implication:** First-year students in Lisbon are simultaneously excited and overwhelmed. Scaffolding matters in Semester 1 — clear expectations, early low-stakes feedback, and patience with students who are managing homesickness alongside academic demands. This is not coddling; it is meeting students where they actually are.

---

### 9. Students want real-world connection, not theory for its own sake

> "Don't leave with regrets for not taking advantage of every opportunity."
> — Yohana (now L&D at L'Oréal) | [Planning for the future](https://forward-college.eu/blog/planning-for-the-future-insights-from-forward-college-graduates/), May 2024

> "The world is continuously changing, so courses might be out-of-date. Keep up to date by reading recent papers."
> — Myriam (now MSc International Security, Sciences Po) | ibid.

> "Forward College managed to make me feel welcomed and helped me navigate the initial challenges." [contrasting with a previous experience]
> — Bruno De Martino | [Meet Bruno De Martino](https://forward-college.eu/blog/student-story-interview-bruno-de-martino/), Dec 2024

**Implication:** Currency matters. Students read Myriam's advice and take it seriously. Assignments referencing live datasets, recent policy, or current business questions will land better than textbook exercises. Berlin-year students (Year 3) are particularly attuned — they are already thinking professionally.

---

### 10. External collaborators see consulting-level analytical rigour from Forward students

> "It didn't feel like a typical student collaboration...students demonstrated ability to 'build a line of reasoning' rather than just present conclusions."
> — Beta House team member (external collaborator) | [You Could Tell They Were Thinking Like Consultants](https://forward-college.eu/blog/you-could-tell-they-were-thinking-like-consultants/), May 2026

> Students "evolved from academic thinking to professional mindset, becoming more comfortable with ambiguity."
> — Beta House team member | ibid.

**Implication:** The bar for analytical communication is high. Teaching students to structure an argument around data — not just calculate and present — is exactly what Forward's external partners are rewarding. Statistics teaching should emphasise interpretation and communication alongside technical method.
