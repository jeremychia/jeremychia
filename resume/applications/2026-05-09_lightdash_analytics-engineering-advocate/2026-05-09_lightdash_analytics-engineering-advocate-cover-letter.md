# Cover Letter — Analytics Engineering Advocate, Lightdash

---

The decision to build Lightdash as an open-source product — and to position it explicitly as a tool for analytics engineers rather than a generic BI layer — is the kind of deliberate product thinking I find genuinely interesting. The bet that data teams should own their metrics in code, and that self-serve can then follow naturally, is how I believe good analytics infrastructure should work. I've operated on that assumption at Vinted for nearly two years, and I'm curious to work at a company where it's the core product thesis.

My strongest qualification for this role is the combination of dbt depth and teaching instinct. At Vinted, I used agentic AI to raise dbt unit test coverage from under 1% to 50% in four weeks, then turned the process into a reusable guide that teammates adopted independently — which is a small version of exactly what this role requires at scale. I've pair-programmed through complex data modeling issues in internal support channels, presented analytics engineering best practices at Forward Data Conference, and hosted a dbt Meetup in Berlin. As a dbt Champion and dbt Igniter in the global dbt Community Slack, I already spend time helping practitioners debug and improve their work — which maps directly to the daily Slack and call-based support at the core of this position.

I haven't used Lightdash in a production environment. My BI experience has been primarily with Looker, which shares the same code-first, metrics-layer philosophy; the migration to a new tool is a learning curve I'm comfortable with, not a blocker.

I'm looking for a role where technical depth and teaching combine into measurable community impact — and where the work is shaped by curiosity and genuine user problems rather than process. This role is a clear match for that. I'm ready to start the conversation.
