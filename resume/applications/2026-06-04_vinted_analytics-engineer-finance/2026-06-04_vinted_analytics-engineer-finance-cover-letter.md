# Cover Letter — Analytics Engineer, Finance — Vinted Group

---

I spent two and a half years building Vinted's shipping finance data layer — the models Finance Ops and Group Reporting used to close the books each month. I left to broaden my experience. What I found is that the combination of finance domain depth and analytics engineering rigour I was able to apply there is genuinely rare, and I'd like to do that work again.

This role describes it precisely: reliable pipelines, quality controls at the model layer, translating Finance requirements into tested data infrastructure. That's the work, without the surrounding noise.

The contribution I look back on most is a shift-left testing framework applied to carrier invoice files — completeness checks, reconciliation against backend data, materiality thresholds to keep alerts actionable rather than noisy. It produced a single trusted dataset Finance could use to dispute charges against the rate card. They recovered €1.6m in overcharges because they had a clear source of truth and a structured way to escalate. The same framework reduced misstatement risk by 90% on €40m of exposure. I care about getting that infrastructure right because I understand what's downstream of a bad number — Chartered Accountant background, eleven transformation projects across APAC-listed companies, years of translating consolidation logic into technical delivery. Justina Šakalytė and I presented this framework at Forward Data Conference in Paris.

Beyond the Finance data layer, I found myself drawn into work that sits at the edges of the role description: the data catalog initiative, guild-wide tooling improvements, PR reviews across domains outside my own scope. A colleague described my approach as always leaving the codebase in a better state than I found it. I think that's accurate — and it extends to documentation, test coverage, and the reasoning I leave behind in PRs for whoever comes next. That habit doesn't switch off based on ticket scope.

Airflow is listed as preferred — my familiarity is observational, not hands-on. BigQuery, dbt, and the data quality layer are where my depth is.

I'm looking for a clear mandate, hard problems, and a team that holds a high bar. This role fits that.
