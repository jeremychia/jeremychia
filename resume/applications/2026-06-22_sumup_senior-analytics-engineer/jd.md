# Senior Analytics Engineer - Run & Grow — SumUp

**URL:** https://www.sumup.com/careers/positions/berlin-germany/data-analytics/senior-analytics-engineer-run-grow/8529484002/?gh_jid=8529484002&gh_src=1e0ad71d2
**Location:** Berlin, Germany

---

## Key Responsibilities

- Partner with squads across the tribe on event design and data contracts, maintaining staging pipelines, applying modelling conventions, and keeping domain outputs consistent, tested, and discoverable
- Model key business domains, including merchant activity, product adoption, lifecycle events, and risk scoring, building well-documented, quality-assured data products
- Build and maintain the insights layer on top of governed domains, producing reusable KPI models, funnels, cohorts, and segmentations
- Implement technical improvements including incremental processing strategies, performance optimisations, and scalable data architecture
- Contribute to SumUp's broader data domain strategy, helping establish durable ownership, consistent definitions, and a shared catalogue of data products

---

## Required Qualifications

- Strong, proven experience in analytics engineering or data engineering, with a track record of building and maintaining production data systems
- Expert-level SQL skills for complex transformations and query optimisation, with hands-on experience building layered data models in a modern data warehouse or lakehouse
- Ability to think in terms of business domains, not just tables, translating complex business logic into clean, durable, and reusable data models
- Comfort working across squads with Product Managers, Engineers, Analysts, and Data Scientists
- Deep care for data quality, trust, and discoverability, building models others can rely on
- Proficiency with dbt including testing, documentation, and modelling conventions
- Experience with platforms like Snowflake, Iceberg

## Layer B — Behavioural Analysis

**Signal ordering:** The first responsibility is cross-squad collaboration on event design and data contracts — they want someone who can partner across teams and enforce modelling standards, not just build in isolation. Domain modelling (merchant activity, product adoption, lifecycle, risk scoring) is listed second, signalling that breadth of domain coverage is the primary technical output. The insights layer (KPIs, funnels, cohorts) comes third — this is an output of good domain modelling, not the primary goal. Technical performance improvements come fourth, signalling they value speed but it is not the headline.

**Repetition = fear/desire:** "Domain" appears 4 times (business domains, domain outputs, domain strategy, data domain strategy) — this is not a team that wants ad-hoc models; they want a structured, domain-driven approach and are afraid of inconsistency and duplication. "Discoverable" / "discoverability" appears in both responsibilities and qualifications — fear of models existing but not being findable or trustworthy. "Consistent" / "consistency" appears 3 times — strong signal of a team burned by drift and disagreement over definitions.

**Seniority signals:** "Contribute to SumUp's broader data domain strategy", "helping establish durable ownership", "consistent definitions", "shared catalogue" — this is a senior IC who shapes standards, not just implements them. Manager-adjacent elements are absent. Autonomy IC framing applies: lead with outcomes and scale, not collaboration steps.

**Loss aversion:** "Data quality, trust, and discoverability", "building models others can rely on", "tested and discoverable", "quality-assured data products" — the fear is untrustworthy data. Frame achievements as risk reduction and trust-building wins.

**Culture signals:** Absent — no personality adjectives. This is a technical team that values craft over culture fit language. Mirror with precision and directness; no enthusiasm marketing.

**Absence signals:** No mention of mentorship, line management, or people leadership — this is a pure senior IC role. No stakeholder management language beyond "comfort working across squads" — collaborator, not project manager. No mention of roadmap ownership — they want someone who executes domain strategy, not defines the product strategy.
