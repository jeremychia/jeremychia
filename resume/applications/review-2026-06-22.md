**2. dbt depth as a differentiating tipping point** — **Medium confidence (1 explicit + pattern)**

- Pattern: Many adapted resumes lead with dbt as a primary differentiator. The Seven-Senders loss is the only explicit evidence it was a competitive gap — but given how many roles require dbt at depth, any perceived shallowness is high-risk.

**3. Ambiguity and chaos tolerance framing** — **Medium confidence (1 explicit + 1 inferred)**

- Inferred pattern: Many AE roles in the dataset emphasise autonomy and fast-paced environments. Framing stories as "building systems that eliminate ambiguity" vs. "thriving within ambiguity" may be reading differently to interviewers than intended.

## Recommended changes

### Resume (`resume-base.json`)
No changes are supported by 3+ explicit rejection signals. The evidence does not point to a resume problem.

**Conditional (Medium confidence):** If Seven-Senders' comment reflects a perception gap rather than a true skill gap, consider whether dbt depth is sufficiently visible in the base resume. Currently it leads with Vinted's scale ("~1B rows daily"). The depth signal may be lost if recruiters skim. **Evidence: 1 explicit (Seven-Senders).** Consider adding a concrete dbt complexity signal to the summary (e.g., a named advanced pattern: incremental strategies, custom macros, semantic layer) rather than relying on scale alone.

### Interview preparation

**1. Lead with the conclusion — immediate priority** *(High confidence, 2 explicit mentions)*
- In every STAR story: state the result in sentence 1. Do not front-load context. If the result is a number, say the number first.
- Practice rule: deliver stories at 70% of the detail you think is needed. Stop at the result. Wait.
- Targeted practice: Take the 5 best STAR stories from the Distribusion prep docs and re-drill them with the conclusion first. Time them. Target under 90 seconds for the headline version.

**2. Add a story for "thriving in chaos" (not "converting chaos to structure")** *(Medium confidence, 1 explicit + 1 self-inferred)*
- Current framing: stories tend to show "I identified the mess, built the system, resolved it." 
- Missing framing: "priorities shifted, I reprioritised on the fly, kept delivering."
- Candidate story: Vinted's scaling context (carrier billing complexity, multiple simultaneous workstreams) may have a version of this. Extract it explicitly.

**3. dbt depth — be specific under pressure** *(Medium confidence, 1 explicit)*
- If an interviewer probes dbt depth, don't lead with scale. Lead with a specific technical decision: "I wrote custom generic tests for financial reconciliation that caught X." or "I chose strategy=incremental with unique_key over full refresh because of Y."
- The Seven-Senders loss was to someone with more hands-on dbt depth — this may mean the other candidate gave more specific technical answers, not that they had more experience.

### Application strategy

**Prioritise:**
- Finance-domain analytics engineering roles (finance AE, revenue analytics, billing infrastructure) — highest stage conversion in the small sample
- Autonomous IC at scale-ups (50–500 people), not enterprises or pure BI shops
- Roles that explicitly want dbt + BigQuery or dbt + Snowflake + a finance/ops domain

**Deprioritise:**
- Pure BI analyst roles (Seven-Senders, Scoot) — stack is backwards-facing vs. skills; risk of being overqualified
- Team lead roles without seniority path clarity (Helmes) — structural gaps (language, scope) waste time
- High-chaos, low-structure environments (Distribusion type) — feedback suggests cultural friction with that operating mode

**Unknown-outcome April cohort:**
- 28 applications from April–June have no signal. If most have now silently lapsed (no contact after 6+ weeks), the conversion rate is low. Consider whether the April cohort's role types/companies skew toward categories now identified as lower-fit.
