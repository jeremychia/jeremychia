# Teaching Statement

## Philosophy

Analytics tools change rapidly. The job is to teach students *how to approach problems*, not which tool to use. Structure courses around frameworks and concepts so learners can pick up new tools as they emerge. A tool-agnostic foundation is more durable than tool-specific training.

Specific anchors used in practice:
- **CRISP-DM** (Wirth & Hipp, 2000) — structured approach to data problems
- **PEP 8** — exposes students to how practitioners actually work, not just syntax
- Direct instruction for deterministic content; inquiry-based / indirect learning for applied, subjective content

## Teaching Objectives

1. Common foundational understanding of concepts and frameworks (via direct instruction)
2. Confidence to execute analytics tasks and communicate with colleagues and potential employers
3. Reinforced learning through exercises and applied projects (e.g., with industry partners)
4. A shared vocabulary that makes learners credible in interviews and collaborative settings

## Methodology

Mix of three strategies:
- **Direct instruction** — concepts, frameworks, principles
- **Indirect / inquiry-based learning** — co-created curriculum; dataset selection shaped by questions students ask; applied projects
- **Independent study** — portfolio projects, exposure for students who want more mileage

Learners typically have varied motivations:
- Some will operationalise an analytics stack for their organisations
- Some will manage those who do
- Most will collaborate with analytics teams

All three groups need a common foundational understanding. The course should serve each.

## What Works in Practice

- **Co-created curriculum**: listen to what students ask → infer what datasets and contexts resonate → incorporate. Example: a team's housing prices in Berlin project connected directly to shared migrant experience in the city.
- **Portfolio support**: help learners craft projects that bolster their CV for the job market
- **Long-term connection**: stay connected with former students on LinkedIn. Hear how they apply what they learned. Refer learners to each other. The community is an underrated value of business school education.
- **Programming as enabler**: teach learners to see analytics tools as an enabler to pair with domain knowledge, not the domain itself.

## View on Flipped Classroom

Flipped methodology works well for subjective, applied content where students benefit from articulating their thinking and learning from each other. Direct instruction remains appropriate for deterministic / technical content where there is a right answer. A good balance of both is optimal.

## Core Teaching Problem: Decision Paralysis

The real obstacle in analytics education is rarely mathematical. Students who freeze in front of a confidence interval or a decision tree are not failing to understand the concept — they are failing to trust themselves to act on uncertain, quantified information. The course goal is to make *deciding under uncertainty* feel executable, not frightening.

This frames how to teach every topic in a course like ST2187:
- **Decision trees (week 7):** make uncertainty explicit and structured — paralysis comes from treating a decision as binary when it is probabilistic
- **Confidence intervals (week 9):** reframe "I'm not sure" as "here is the range I can stand behind" — shifts the mindset from certainty-seeking to precision-calibration
- **Hypothesis testing (week 10):** teach the cost of both errors, not just the p-value — managers need to understand what they are trading off, not just whether to reject H₀
- **Monte Carlo simulation (week 15):** modelling uncertainty is not admitting ignorance — it is the most rigorous way to reason about an unknown

In practitioner settings: the €1.6m recovered at Vinted required deciding to investigate carrier billing without certainty of overcharges first. The 90% risk reduction on €40m required building controls that would flag anomalies before they were confirmed errors. These are decisions made under uncertainty using exactly the frameworks the course teaches.
