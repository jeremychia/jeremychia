## What resonates

- **Problem solver with a visionary bent** — I genuinely do see the "big picture" and how to improve systems; this tracks with how I work
- **Not taking criticism personally** — feedback = data, not judgment. I actively seek critiques that make me better
- **Impatience with inefficiency** — half measures and confusion genuinely frustrate me; I notice broken processes immediately
- **Pragmatic + logical** — I reach decisions through reasoning and past experience, not intuition or committee consensus
- **Acting as the team conscience** — I do push back on unfair shortcuts or decisions that ignore the bigger picture; I care about fairness and doing right by people
- **Willing to make tough calls** — I'm comfortable with uncomfortable decisions if the logic is sound
- **High standards** — I set a bar and expect others to meet it. But I'm learning that "high standards" requires nuance: some decisions are reversible (1-way door vs. 2-way door thinking), some collaborations require meeting people where they are, and I need to create places where people can fail safely and learn from it.
- **Disciplined, caring, and quietly driven** — I'm genuine and kind-hearted with people who respect honesty and share a commitment to doing good work. I show care through reliability and clear feedback, not through constant emotional expression

**Example from work:** Led a refactor that required stepping on some toes to improve system stability. Wasn't popular in the moment, but the results vindicated the approach. Didn't regret it because the logic was sound. But in retrospect, I could have brought along the people working on related systems earlier — I was so focused on the technical correctness that I missed the opportunity to help them understand why it mattered.

## What doesn't fit

- **"Shy appearance at times"** — this feels overstated. I'm direct and outgoing when it matters; I'm just not performing warmth constantly
- **"Adopting a low profile"** — I don't naturally hold back. If I know something, I say it. The assessment might be picking up that I'm not seeking attention for its own sake

## Potential pitfalls (that I'm aware of)

- **High expectations → projecting onto others** — My own bar is very high, and I can unconsciously expect the same from everyone else. This means I can be disappointed quickly when outcomes are substandard, and I'm learning to give people more space to grow at their own pace. The question I keep asking myself: how do I maintain standards while letting people improve?

- **Action-oriented → missing emotional cues** — I move fast and focus on outcomes. But this can mean I miss the emotional subtext of a situation. How do I stay outcome-driven while actually appreciating the process and how my directness lands on people?

- **Evidence-based → over-weighted by past experience** — I trust what I've seen work before, but this can be a trap. Two situations are never exactly the same. I'm working on staying open to new context and not defaulting to "this is how we did it last time."

- **Over-protective of my own ideas** — When someone challenges an idea with weak reasoning, I can be dismissive. I'm learning to take a longer view: either the person will see why it won't work, or I'll realise I was wrong. Either way, pointing out the flaw immediately doesn't help. I need to think about where the person's argument comes from and what would actually help them understand.

- **"Stepping on toes to achieve objectives"** — I've learned that this is a real pattern. At scale, you can't just steamroll people. You have to talk to the people doing related work, understand their context, and find a way that works for everyone. The problem isn't that I want to achieve the objective — it's that I don't always see the interpersonal dimension first.

- **Self-doubt and perfectionism in the shadows** — I'm aware that perfectionism and self-doubt can hold me back. I set impossibly high standards for myself and ruminate on past decisions. I'm learning to practice self-compassion without lowering my bar, and to make peace with "good enough" on things that don't matter as much.

## How I come across vs. how I actually am

**First impression:** Serious, direct, maybe intense. People often think I'm colder or more rigid than I actually am. My straightforward communication style gets misread as inflexibility — people assume I won't listen to alternatives when really I'm just not going to pretend an unsound idea is good.

**Deeper reality:** I care deeply about getting things right and people improving. What looks like criticism is often investment — I wouldn't bother pointing out flaws if I didn't think you could do better. I'm not cold; I'm practical about emotions. And I do listen to alternatives, but I'll tell you honestly if I think they won't work.

**The gap:** I don't perform warmth naturally, and I don't have infinite patience for exploring feelings. This makes me seem less emotionally intelligent than I am. I *understand* emotional dynamics; I just prioritize directness over managing them.

## How I like to be managed

- **Direct feedback, given logically** — tell me what's not working and why. Don't soften it or make it personal
- **High autonomy with clear goals** — tell me what success looks like and what guardrails exist; let me figure out how
- **Structured check-ins, but respect my time** — regular touchpoints to align, not constant interruptions
- **Assume competence** — don't second-guess my decisions without reason; challenge me on the logic, not on whether I "should have asked first"
- **Value my directness** — don't ask me for my opinion and then get upset if it's not what you wanted to hear
- **Escalate only when needed** — most issues I can handle; call me in when my judgment is needed, not for every decision
