## Key traits (what resonates)
- Problem solver with visionary bent — sees the big picture and how to improve systems
- Feedback = data, not judgment; actively seeks critique
- Impatient with inefficiency; notices broken processes immediately
- Pragmatic + logical — decides through reasoning, not intuition or committee consensus
- Willing to make tough calls when logic is sound
- High standards; learning that some decisions are reversible and people need space to fail safely
- Disciplined, caring, quietly driven — shows care through reliability and honesty, not constant emotional expression

## Potential pitfalls (self-aware)
- **High expectations → projecting** — can be disappointed quickly; working on giving people space to grow at their own pace
- **Action-oriented → missing emotional cues** — moves fast on outcomes; may miss emotional subtext or that someone needs encouragement alongside correction
- **Over-weighted by past experience** — trusts what's worked before; working on staying open to new context
- **Over-protective of own ideas** — can be dismissive of weak challenges; learning to take a longer view
- **Stepping on toes at scale** — need to bring people along, not steamroll; talk to people doing related work first
- **Perfectionism and self-doubt** — sets impossibly high standards for self; learning self-compassion without lowering the bar

## How I come across vs. how I actually am
- **First impression:** serious, direct, possibly intense; straightforward style misread as inflexibility or unwillingness to listen
- **Reality:** cares deeply about getting things right; criticism is investment; listens to alternatives but will say honestly if they won't work
- **Gap:** doesn't perform warmth; understands emotional dynamics but prioritises directness over managing them

## How I like to be managed
- Direct, logical, specific feedback — no softening or wrapping in praise
- High autonomy with clear goals; tell me what success looks like, let me figure out how
- Assume competence; challenge on logic, not on "should have asked first"
- Value directness — don't ask for opinion then reject honesty
- Regular touchpoints to align, but respect my time; minimal interruptions
