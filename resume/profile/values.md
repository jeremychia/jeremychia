# Values

Non-negotiables and strong preferences. Use this to filter opportunities and to answer "why this company?" authentically.

---

## Non-negotiables

- **Truth and competence matter more than politics** — if the company optimises for optics over correctness, I'm out
- **Clear expectations and authority structure** — I need to know exactly what's expected and what my lines of responsibility are
- **Merit-based advancement** — advancement and compensation tied to actual results, not tenure or relationships
- **Autonomy to do the work right** — can't be in an environment where I'm forced to ship half-measures or ignore obvious problems
- **Environment where people can be themselves** — I don't want to work somewhere that treats politics as inevitable and necessary. I believe that wherever someone comes from, they probably have a reason for how they want to work. There's usually a point where you can join up and find common ground. I want to work in places that make that easy, not hard.
- **Kindness, fairness, and meaningful contribution** — work that helps others while maintaining a sense of order and purpose. I want to make a positive impact without sacrificing my own need for downtime and reflection.
- **Investing in practitioners and communities** — roles where there's real opportunity to help others improve their craft, whether formally (mentoring/teaching) or informally (code review, system design help). I want to work somewhere that values this as contribution, not a side activity. This includes the ability to raise team standards through direct, honest feedback and working with people until they understand not just *what* to do, but *why* it matters.
- **Enabling impact at scale** — I'm not interested in just solving problems myself. I want roles where I can help others develop judgment and capability, so the impact compounds. This might mean leading a small team, designing systems that are "easy to get right," or being a recognized expert that others learn from.

## Strong preferences (will bend for the right role, but default is yes/no)

| Preference | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Remote / hybrid / in-person | Flexible | Remote or hybrid preferred; in-person doable if team and work justify it |
| Company size | Mid-to-large (100+) | Prefer orgs with enough scale for interesting problems; smaller feels limiting after a point |
| Industry | Tech-forward or data-heavy | Want to work somewhere engineering and data matter strategically, not as afterthought |
| Management style | Trust-based, direct | Manager who gives autonomy, values logic, and doesn't demand constant reassurance |
| Team culture | Intellectually rigorous, low-drama | Teams that care about quality and tackle problems head-on; minimal office politics |

## Green flags (things that strongly attract you)

- **Technical depth valued** — problems that require genuine expertise; team celebrates shipping good solutions
- **Logical decision-making** — leadership that reasons through trade-offs openly instead of deciding by consensus
- **Autonomy + clear goals** — "here's the outcome we need, figure out how" vs. prescriptive roadmaps
- **Growth opportunities** — chance to become a recognized expert in a domain; time to build mastery. If there's a leadership track, it's one where I can grow people and amplify impact at scale.
- **High bar for quality** — company invests in getting things right; not optimising purely for speed. Leaders understand the difference between a reversible decision and an irreversible one, and act accordingly.
- **Straight talk** — feedback flows freely; people aren't afraid to disagree; directness is valued
- **Actual impact** — work that demonstrably matters; not theatre or make-work
- **Servant leadership modeled** — leadership that invests in helping reports get better at their craft, understand their strengths, and think about what's next. Not just managing up, but genuinely invested in the team's growth.

## Red flags (things that make you cautious)

- **Managers who can't handle disagreement** — if my push-back gets read as disrespect, we're misaligned
- **Decision-by-committee** — lack of clarity on who decides; constant re-negotiation of scope
- **Quantity over quality** — culture that ships fast without owning the consequences; technical debt as normal
- **Lots of process/politics** — heavy approval chains, status-signalling over work, "managing up" as core skill
- **Teams that avoid feedback** — everyone being nice and supportive instead of honest about what's not working
- **Unclear growth path** — role feels static; no signal of what comes next or how you'd get there
- **Vague expectations** — "passionate about X" in the job description; undefined success metrics
